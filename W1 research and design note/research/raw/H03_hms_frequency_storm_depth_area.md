# H03 HEC-HMS frequency storm and depth-area analysis

Date: 2026-10-29

## Scope

What HEC-HMS 4.12/4.13 does when it builds a design storm from frequency depths and when it runs a Depth-Area Analysis (DAA): the inputs of the two storm builders, the exact hyetograph construction, how the area-reduction factor (ARF) is applied, where the ARF table and the temporal pattern live in the project files, how the DAA sets the storm area per analysis point and what it recomputes, how results are stored and reported, and the units and time-step traps.

Read: the HMS Technical Reference Manual (TRM; confluence pages and the March 2000 PDF), the User's Manual (UM; 4.11 confluence pages and the 3.1.0 PDF), four HEC application guides, the 4.10, 4.12 and 4.13 release notes, a third-party reverse engineering of the 4.13 Frequency Storm validated against 70 HMS runs, and the Block 2 HEC-HMS 4.12 project itself (`.met`, `.pdata`, `.nals`, `.control`, `.run`, `.grid`, a DAA log, a results XML, and the paired-data tables and one computed hyetograph read from copies of the project DSS files with HEC-DSSVue's Jython). Two numerical checks were run on Block 2 output: ARF interpolation (40 analysis points) and the pattern-to-hyetograph construction (288 five-minute steps).

The first finding changes the framing of the task: Block 2 does **not** use the HMS "Frequency Storm" method. It uses the "Hypothetical Storm" method (one depth, one 24 h pattern, one ARF on the total). Both builders are documented, because Wadi2D needs the Hypothetical Storm to replicate Block 2 (test (a) of decision 6) and the Frequency Storm to read other HMS projects.

Not covered: NOAA Atlas 14 specifics, ARF theory (ARR, NOAA; other topics), the Frequency Analysis compute (one point, many frequencies), uncertainty sampling of patterns and ARF tables (4.12).

## Findings

1. **Block 2's thirteen `FM` met models use the Hypothetical Storm method, not the Frequency Storm.** [Certain] Each `.met` carries `Precipitation Method: Hypothetical Storm` with `Precipitation Method: Point Depth` (the PMP one: `Precipitation-Frequency Grid`, grid `PMP`), `Storm Depth: 205.4` (100 yr, mm), `Storm Type: Specified Pattern`, `Storm Duration: 24` (h), `Pattern Name: FM 10-1000yr` (2 yr, 5 yr, 2000-10000 yr and PMP have their own patterns), `Storm Area: 300` (km2, overridden by the DAA), `Depth-Area Reduction Method: User-Specified`, `Reduction Table Name: ARF FM`, `Uniform Depth: Yes`. Evidence: "Storm Type: Specified Pattern ... Depth-Area Reduction Method: User-Specified ... Reduction Table Name: ARF FM" (FM_100yr.met). Source: S16.

2. **HMS has two different design-storm builders and they treat the ARF differently.** [Certain] The Frequency Storm (`Frequency Based Hypothetical` in the met file) takes a depth for every duration, reduces each duration with its own ARF, and nests them with the alternating-block method; the Hypothetical Storm takes one depth for one duration, distributes it with a dimensionless pattern and reduces the total with one factor. Evidence: "only needs one depth grid that reflects the frequency and total duration" (of the Hypothetical Storm; the Frequency Storm needs all durations). Source: S7.

3. **Frequency Storm inputs.** [Certain] Point depths per duration from 5 min to 10 d (the 4.13 met writer lists 5, 10, 15, 30, 60, 120, 180, 360, 720, 1440, 2880, 4320, 5760, 10080, 14400 min), an intensity (peak) duration that is the shortest duration and normally equals the time step, a storm duration, an intensity position of 25, 33, 50, 67 or 75 % (default 50), a storm area (User-Specified value, or blank = each subbasin's own area), an area-reduction option None / TP40-TP49 / User-Specified (a paired-data table per duration, one table reusable for several durations), and, before 4.13, an annual/partial-duration conversion. Depths must be entered for every duration from the intensity duration to the storm duration (4.13 relaxes this: missing ones are log-interpolated). Evidence: "Precipitation Depth values must be entered for all durations from the peak intensity to the total storm duration." Source: S3 (also S1, S12, S15).

4. **Frequency Storm algorithm as documented (TRM).** [Certain] (i) ARF per duration applied to the entered depths (TP-40 curves to 24 h, TP-49 beyond, or the user tables); (ii) depths at every integer multiple of the time interval by linear interpolation in log(depth) vs log(duration); (iii) successive differences give the blocks; (iv) alternating-block arrangement around the intensity position, the alternation stopping when the front (25, 33 %) or back (67, 75 %) is full and the rest going to the free side. Evidence: "Linear interpolation is used, after taking logarithms of both the depth and duration data." Source: S1 (steps also in S13, pp. 25-27).

5. **What HMS 4.13 actually does differs from the TRM wording in two places.** [Likely] (a) With `Re-sort Storm Symmetrically: No` (the default, the only behaviour before 4.13) the blocks are placed in duration (nested-time) order, not re-sorted by value, so a non-monotone depth table does not give a pyramid; `Yes` re-sorts into the TRM pyramid. (b) The 30 min ARF is applied to the 5-15 min depths although the TRM says no reduction under 30 min. Reverse-engineered and matched to 68 HMS 4.13 runs within 4.8e-12 in per block; 4.12 not tested. Evidence: "HMS 4.13 applies the 30-min value below 30 min, despite the TRM wording that says no adjustment is made below 30 min." Source: S15.

6. **TP-40/TP-49 curves as implemented in HMS (fitted form).** [Likely] ARF(A, d) = 1 - (1 - RF_inf(d)) (1 - exp(-0.015 A)), A in mi2, RF_inf = 0.52 (30 min), 0.65 (1 h), 0.78 (3 h), 0.83 (6 h), 0.91 (24 h), 0.932 (2 d), 0.945 (4 d), 0.951 (7 d), 0.956 (10 d), the loss 1 - RF_inf interpolated linearly in ln(duration) vs ln(loss), duration clamped to [30 min, 10 d], factor 1 for A <= 0. Matches the TRM-2000 Figure 12 shape (24 h about 91 % at 400 mi2, 30 min about 52 %). The curves stop at 400 mi2 (1036 km2); HMS still accepts larger areas. Evidence: "TP-40/49 should not be applied to watersheds (total study areas) larger than 400 square miles." Source: S1 (constants from S15; figure in S13 p. 27).

7. **Hypothetical Storm inputs.** [Certain] Precipitation Method `Point Depth` (Storm Depth plus a Storm Area) or `Precipitation-Frequency Grid` (a Precipitation-Frequency grid dataset plus a Computation Point; the storm area defaults to the drainage area above that point, a typed Storm Area overrides it; georeferenced basin only); Storm Type: SCS Type I, IA, II, III (always 24 h, no area reduction), `Specified Pattern` (a percentage curve plus a Storm Duration in hours), or `Area-Dependent Pattern` (several patterns, one per storm area, interpolated by storm area); Depth-Area Reduction Method None / TP40 (the 24 h curve only) / User-Specified (one table); Spatial Distribution Uniform For All Subbasins (`Uniform Depth: Yes` in the file [Likely for the key mapping]) or Variable By Subbasin. Evidence: "The 24-hour area reduction curve from TP40 is used when TP40 is selected for the Area Reduction Method." Source: S3 (also S2, S7, S11).

8. **Hypothetical Storm hyetograph construction, verified on Block 2.** [Certain] The cumulative percentage curve (x = % of storm duration, y = % of depth) is interpolated linearly at every computation step; the increment of the step is the difference of the interpolated cumulative percentages times the area-reduced depth. Block 2: pattern at 10 min (145 points, 0.694 % spacing), computation at 5 min; the HMS series `//S18/PRECIP-INC//5MIN/MET:FM 100yr>S18/` (sum 205.022 mm) agrees with this construction within 0.00006 mm at every one of the 288 steps, the two 5 min blocks inside each 10 min pattern segment being equal halves. The series is period-ending (first value stamped 00:05). Evidence: "Tables lacking a 0, 0 and/or a 100, 100 entry now throw an error and halt the simulation." Source: S10 (check: S16).

9. **In the Hypothetical Storm the ARF is a single factor on the total depth, read from the table by linear interpolation in area; the hyetograph shape does not change with area.** [Certain] For the 40 analysis points of `DA 100yr B1` the logged depth / 205.4 equals the linear interpolation of `ARF FM` at the logged storm area within 2e-6 (e.g. 211.085 km2 gives 0.86075 and 176.798 mm). Contrast: the Frequency Storm's per-duration ARFs flatten the hyetograph as the area grows. Evidence: "Hypothetical storm area: 211.085 KM2. ... Area-reduced hypothetical storm depth: 176.798 mm." Source: S16 (DAA log).

10. **Where the ARF table and the pattern live.** [Certain] `.pdata` holds the metadata: `Table: ARF FM`, `Table Type: Area-Reduction Factor`, `X-Units: KM2` (or MI2; no Y units, the factor is a ratio), `Use External DSS File: NO`, `DSS File: HEC_HMS_Block_02.dss`, `Pathname: //ARF FM/AREA-REDUCTION FACTOR///TABLE/`, `Interpolation: Linear Interpolation`; the values are a paired-data record in the project DSS (Block 2: 12 points, 1 to 2000 km2). Patterns: `Table Type: Percent Graph`, `X-Units: %`, `Y-Units: %`, pathname `//FM 10-1000yr/PERCENT GRAPH///TABLE/`. The UM says the C-parts should be `AREA-FACTOR` and `PERCENT`; HMS 4.12 writes `AREA-REDUCTION FACTOR` and `PERCENT GRAPH`, so a reader must not trust the documented C-part. The ARF table must be ascending in area and extend beyond the largest storm area (checked since 4.10 beta.6); the pattern must contain (0,0) and (100,100) with strictly increasing time (depth may repeat since 4.10 beta.7). Evidence: "the area reduction function paired data table is now checked for properly ascending values". Source: S10 (file keys S16; C-part statement S5).

11. **Depth-Area Analysis mechanics.** [Certain] A DAA names a basin model, a met model (Hypothetical Storm, or a Frequency Storm whose Storm Area is set to User-Specified), a time window and interval of its own, and a list of analysis points (any element; 4.12 adds All Subbasins / All Junctions / All Elements). For each point HMS extracts the drainage area upstream, uses it as the storm area (overriding the met model's Storm Area and Computation Point), interpolates the ARF, builds the hyetograph, and computes **only the elements upstream of that point**: the Block 2 log shows 1 subbasin computed for point S18, 4 subbasins and 2 reaches for the outlet Z4C. N points = N sub-network simulations. Evidence: "Results for an analysis point are only computed at the elements upstream of that analysis point;" Source: S4 (also S1, S3, S8, S9, S14 p. 248; log S16).

12. **The DAA with a Frequency Storm.** [Certain] The TRM requires the Storm Area setting `User-Specified`; the analysis then computes the area per point and the per-duration ARFs follow from that area. Evidence: "If the Frequency Storm method is used in a Depth-Area Analysis, the Storm Area setting must be set to "User-Specified"." Source: S1 (3.x wording: "it automatically generates frequency storms based on the one specified in the meteorologic model but with appropriate storm area for each analysis point", S14 p. 248).

13. **DAA storage and reporting.** [Certain] Analyses are in `<project>.nals` (`Depth-Area Analysis Manager`; per analysis: `DSS File`, `Time Zone GMT Offset`, `Start/End Date/Time`, `Time Interval`, `Basin Model Name`, `Met Model Name`, `Analysis Point:` lines, `Time-Series Output: Save All`). Basin results go to `<analysis>.dss` with F-part `DAA:<analysis>><point>` (Block 2: 2928 records, 40 F-parts); the precipitation per analysis point goes to the **met model's** DSS (`FM_100yr.dss`) with F-part `MET:<met>><point>`; `results\DAA_<analysis>.results` is an XML listing, per analysis point and upstream element, `DrainageArea`, `SubbasinArea`, the DSS pathnames and volume statistics (no peak values). The UI's Peak Flow Summary table (analysis point, drainage area, peak flow, time of peak) and the per-point result folders (upstream elements only) are built from these. Evidence: "The principal information is a table that includes one row for each analysis point." Source: S4 (files S16).

14. **Storm area means drainage area at the evaluation point, by assumption, and the TRM records two thresholds HMS does not enforce.** [Certain for the text, Likely for HMS behaviour] "The storm area should be set equal to the drainage area at the evaluation location." The TRM-2000 also says point values are used unreduced below 9.6 mi2 (WMO 1994) and no adjustment under 30 min (HEC 1982); the fitted TP-40 form (finding 6) reduces at any area above zero and finding 5 shows the 30 min rule is not applied. Source: S1, S13 p. 26.

15. **Annual/partial conversion of the Frequency Storm.** [Certain] Factors 0.88 (50 % AEP), 0.96 (20 %), 0.99 (10 %) partial-to-annual (1.14 annual-to-partial at 50 %); dropped as an option in 4.13, old projects are converted on opening. Evidence: "50 Percent Annual to Partial (ratio 1.14), and 50 Percent Partial to Annual (ratio 0.88)". Source: S3 (factors S1; removal S12).

16. **HYDRO-35 augmentation.** [Certain for the TRM, Likely for 4.13] P10 = 0.59 P15 + 0.41 P5 and P30 = 0.49 P60 + 0.51 P15 fill the 10 and 30 min depths; in 4.13 this is applied only to legacy 3.x met files, not to blank rows of native files. Evidence: "P_{10-min} = 0.59P_{15-min} + 0.41P_{5-min}". Source: S1 (4.13 behaviour S15).

17. **Area-Dependent Pattern is the only HMS mechanism in which the pattern-storm shape follows the drainage area.** [Certain] HMS interpolates between the user's patterns using the storm area, or the analysis-point area in a DAA; since 4.12 it can be combined with a precipitation-frequency grid and a computation point. Evidence: "HEC-HMS interpolates between the temporal patterns using the user defined storm area". Source: S3 (S11 for 4.12).

18. **Cost of a DAA in HMS is trivial for a lumped model.** [Certain] `DA 100yr B1`: 40 analysis points, 18 subbasins, 5 min step, 48 h window, total runtime 2 s; Block 2 runs 13 of them (520 sub-network simulations). Evidence: "The total runtime for this simulation is 00:02." Source: S16.

19. **Units and time-step traps collected.** [Certain unless tagged] (a) The ARF table's area unit is the table's X-Units (KM2 or MI2); 4.10 beta.9 fixed a rounding in the unit conversion, all comparisons now in SI. (b) TP40 in the Hypothetical Storm is the 24 h curve only; HEC: use it only with 24 h patterns. (c) The storm duration must equal the duration of the precipitation-frequency grid. (d) The Frequency Storm's intensity duration should equal the time step and must be shorter than the storm duration; the time window must cover the storm plus the recession. (e) A plain run with the grid method needs a Computation Point (the Block 2 PMP run aborts with "No computation point selected", per the project notes [Likely]) but the DAA does not. (f) Grid scale factors: "Always check the metadata for the data scale factor." (Atlas 14 grids in inches x 1000). (g) The `.nals` stores a `Time Zone GMT Offset` of -28800000 ms (the project's America/Los_Angeles) while the control uses Asia/Dubai; HMS treats times as local-naive, the offsets are bookkeeping. (h) The precipitation series are period-ending at the computation interval. Sources: S10, S3, S8, S16.

20. **Announced change.** [Likely] HEC says v4.15 will compute ARFs directly from gridded (historic) storms, which would make the user tables derivable inside HMS. Source: S6.

## Equations and algorithms

Notation: d = duration; A = storm area; P(d) = point depth for duration d at the chosen AEP; ARF(A, d) = area-reduction factor (ratio <= 1); dt = computation interval; T = storm duration; n = T / dt blocks; p = intensity position in % of T; C(t) = cumulative depth at time t; b_k = incremental depth of block k; y(x) = cumulative percentage curve (x = % of T, y = % of depth).

### A. Frequency Storm (HMS "Frequency Based Hypothetical"), as HMS 4.13 computes it

1. Input table P(d_i) for the entered durations d_i, 5 min <= d_i <= 10 d, non-decreasing in d (HMS rejects a decreasing table, ERROR 20025). Required: P(dt) and P(T). Before 4.13 every duration between them is required; 4.13 fills missing ones by log-log interpolation (step 4) before anything else.
2. Optional HYDRO-35 augmentation (legacy 3.x files only in 4.13):
   P(10 min) = 0.59 P(15 min) + 0.41 P(5 min);  P(30 min) = 0.49 P(60 min) + 0.51 P(15 min).
3. Area reduction per duration: P'(d_i) = ARF(A, d_i) P(d_i).
   - TP-40/TP-49 (fitted form, finding 6): ARF(A, d) = 1 - L_inf(d) (1 - exp(-0.015 A)), A in mi2, L_inf(d) = 1 - RF_inf(d), ln L_inf interpolated linearly in ln d between the tabulated durations (30 min: 0.48, 1 h: 0.35, 3 h: 0.22, 6 h: 0.17, 24 h: 0.09, 2 d: 0.068, 4 d: 0.055, 7 d: 0.049, 10 d: 0.044), d clamped to [30 min, 10 d] (so 5-15 min use the 30 min factor), ARF = 1 for A <= 0.
   - User-Specified: one paired table (area, factor) per duration; factor read at A (interpolation in area presumed linear, as verified for the Hypothetical Storm; not separately verified for this builder) [Likely].
   - None: ARF = 1.
   - Before 4.13, optional partial-to-annual: P' = f P', f = 0.88 (50 % AEP), 0.96 (20 %), 0.99 (10 %), else 1.
4. Cumulative depth at every block end t_k = k dt, k = 1..n:
   ln C(t_k) = ln P'(d_a) + [ln P'(d_b) - ln P'(d_a)] [ln t_k - ln d_a] / [ln d_b - ln d_a], d_a <= t_k <= d_b the bracketing entered durations (C(T) = P'(T)).
5. Blocks: b_k = C(t_k) - C(t_(k-1)), C(t_0) = 0, k = 1..n. (With consistent DDF data the b_k decrease with k; they need not.)
6. Placement (HMS 4.13, `Re-sort Storm Symmetrically: No`): peak slot i_p = clamp(floor(p/100 n), 0, n-1); b_1 goes to slot i_p; then for k = 2, 3, ...: alternately the next free slot before the peak (i_p - 1, i_p - 2, ...) and after it (i_p + 1, ...), starting with the before side; when one side is exhausted all remaining blocks go to the other side. The blocks keep their index order (nested time order), they are not sorted by value.
   `Re-sort Storm Symmetrically: Yes` (4.13 only): the same placement after sorting the b_k in descending order, which is the TRM's wording ("the block of maximum incremental depth first at the specified location ... remaining blocks ... in descending order, alternating before and after").
   Example: n = 4, p = 25 %: i_p = 1; slots = [b_2, b_1, b_3, b_4]. n = 6, p = 50 %: i_p = 3; slots = [b_6, b_4, b_2, b_1, b_3, b_5].
7. Output: incremental precipitation per interval, the same hyetograph for all subbasins unless the storm area is blank (then each subbasin uses its own area in step 3) or the depth-duration curve is Variable by Subbasin.

### B. Hypothetical Storm with a Specified Pattern (Block 2), as HMS 4.12 computes it (verified)

1. Depth: D = Storm Depth (point depth, uniform or per subbasin), or the basin-average of the precipitation-frequency grid over the polygon upstream of the computation point (Uniform) or over each subbasin (Variable).
2. Storm area: A = Storm Area (point depth) or the drainage area above the computation point (grid); in a DAA, A = drainage area of the analysis point, always.
3. One factor: F = ARF_table(A) by linear interpolation between the table's (A_j, F_j); D' = F D (logged as NOTE 23409). TP40 option: the 24 h TP-40 curve at A. None: F = 1.
4. Hyetograph at the computation interval dt (minutes), T in hours: x_k = 100 k dt / (60 T); y_k = y(x_k) by linear interpolation of the percentage curve; b_k = (y_k - y_(k-1)) / 100 D', k = 1..n, y_0 = 0, y_n = 100. Values after T are zero. Period-ending stamps.
5. Area-Dependent Pattern variant: y(x) is itself interpolated between the patterns of the two bracketing storm areas (rule not documented; presumed linear in area [Guessing]).

### C. Depth-Area Analysis (both builders)

For each analysis point q in the list:
1. A_q = sum of the subbasin areas upstream of q (the basin model's `Area` values, `DrainageArea` in the results XML).
2. Replace the met model's storm area (and computation point) by A_q (NOTE 15405 / 23407).
3. Build the storm with A_q: builder A applies ARF(A_q, d_i) per duration; builder B applies F = ARF(A_q) to D.
4. Run the basin model restricted to the elements upstream of q, with the DAA's own time window and interval; write `//<element>/<type>//<dt>/DAA:<analysis>><q>/` to the analysis DSS and `//<subbasin>/PRECIP-INC//<dt>/MET:<met>><q>/` to the met DSS.
5. Report peak flow and time of peak at q; the results for q are valid only at q and upstream of it; a point's own area is never applied to another point.
Cost = sum over points of the upstream sub-network size (not N whole-basin runs).

## Recommendation for Wadi2D

1. **Build the Hypothetical-Storm path first and exactly, because that is what test (a) must reproduce**: one depth (point or grid average), one dimensionless cumulative pattern interpolated linearly in time at the hydrology step, one ARF read by linear interpolation in area from a (km2, factor) table, applied to the total depth, period-ending increments. Check against the Block 2 S18 series in `FM_100yr.dss` (F-part `MET:FM 100yr>S18`) to the 1e-4 mm level before touching losses or routing. For the 2D rain source the same cumulative curve gives a piecewise-constant intensity over each 10 min pattern segment; do not resample the pattern to the solver's seconds-scale step, integrate the curve.

2. **Implement the Frequency Storm (alternating block) as a second builder, HMS-4.13-faithful**: log-log depth-duration interpolation, per-duration ARF, nested-time placement with the one-sided fill rule, optional symmetric re-sort, the 30 min factor below 30 min. Validate against the 70 public HMS 4.13 fixture cases of S15 (or against an HMS 4.12 run on this machine, both versions are installed) rather than against the TRM text, which is not what the code does. Keep the TP-40/49 fitted form only for reading US projects; it is irrelevant to Oman and limited to 1036 km2.

3. **Read the HMS files as they are, not as documented**: parse `.met` for both `Precipitation Method` values and their parameter blocks (keys in findings 1 and 3), `.pdata` for the table metadata, and the DSS paired-data record by its stored pathname (C-part `AREA-REDUCTION FACTOR` / `PERCENT GRAPH`, not the documented `AREA-FACTOR` / `PERCENT`); read `.nals` for the analysis points and `results\DAA_*.results` for the per-point drainage areas and pathnames (useful as the oracle for the drainage-area lookup of decision 7). A DSS 7 reader is needed (the Jython route used here works but is not shippable; see open issues).

4. **Decision 9 is consistent with HMS, with one correction and one caution.** Correction: HMS does not rerun the whole basin per analysis point, it reruns the upstream sub-network, so the HMS cost model is smaller than stated in the project file; the design argument for the storm-area sweep (cost bounded by the number of storm areas, not points) still holds for a 2D domain where no sub-network can be cut. Caution: HMS reduces the rain uniformly over the entire upstream catchment of the point with that point's factor, which is exactly what a sweep run with storm area A does at the point whose drainage area is A. The composite map (every cell reads the run of its own drainage area) has no HMS counterpart; present it as a Wadi2D product, never as "the HMS result". For the named design points (five dam sites) run their exact drainage areas as extra sweep members instead of interpolating peaks between log-spaced areas; the ARF steps are 2 % but the peak passes through CN losses non-linearly.

5. **Reject**: SCS Type I-III patterns (US, 24 h, no ARF, not used here); the annual/partial conversion (gone in 4.13); TP40 as a default (only when reading a US project); the Area-Dependent Pattern for version 1 (not used in Block 2, interpolation rule undocumented) but keep the data model open to it, since it is HMS's only shape-varies-with-area mechanism and a possible answer to the "fixed-area vs storm-centred ARF" question of decision 9.

6. **For reviewers**: describe Wadi2D's method as "storm area equal to the drainage area at the design point, user area-reduction table, specified 24 h pattern, identical to the HEC-HMS depth-area analysis", and show the replication table (HMS peak vs Wadi2D lumped peak at the 40 Block 2 points). That sentence is defensible only if items 1 and 3 are exact.

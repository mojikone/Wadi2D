# N02 TUFLOW HPC sub-grid sampling (SGS), Z lines, wet/dry depths and steep terrain

Date: 2026-10-29

## Findings

### A. How SGS samples cell volume and face flow area/width

1. **SGS replaces the one-elevation-per-cell and one-elevation-per-face model with two kinds of curve: a cell volume-versus-elevation curve and, for every face, flow-area and flow-width (conveyance) versus elevation curves.** [Certain] "The cell volume is a non-linear function of elevation (i.e. a curve of cell volume versus elevation)." Source: 2020-01 release notes s.3.2.3, https://downloads.tuflow.com/_archive/TUFLOW/Releases/2020-01/Doc/TUFLOW%20Release%20Notes.2020-01-AB.pdf

2. **The cell volume curve is built from wetted area versus depth measured from the lowest sampled point in the cell; the face curves are built from the samples along the cell side, and the flow area curve is kept separately to turn the face flux into a depth-and-width-averaged velocity.** [Certain] "based on the wetted surface area versus depth measured from the lowest sampled elevation within the 2D cell" Source: TUFLOW Manual 2023-03 s.7.4.3, https://downloads.tuflow.com/TUFLOW/Manuals/2023-03/TUFLOW.Manual.2023-03.pdf

3. **Sampling is a regular grid of points inside the cell, an odd number per face so that the mid-face and the corners are always sample points; sample frequency f = cell size / target distance + 1, rounded up to odd; default 11 per face (121 per cell); capped at 31 per face by default; hard limit 127 per face (16,129 per cell). An 8 m cell with a 2 m target gives 5 per face, 25 per cell.** [Certain] "TUFLOW requires an odd number of sample locations across a cell face to ensure the cell mid-face and cell corners are sampled." Source: Manual 2023-03 s.7.4.3.1.1 (same URL as 2).

4. **There is no benefit in sampling finer than the DEM; 11 to 31 per face is said to be enough for natural waterways; the default target distance is the finest raster resolution read in the geometry file.** [Certain] "there is no benefit to specify an SGS target distance smaller than the DEM resolution." Source: Manual 2023-03 s.7.4.3.1.1.

5. **The default SGS Approach since 2023-03 is Method C: all sampled elevations are kept in memory through the whole geometry build (DEM, TINs, Z shapes, breaklines) and the curves are built once at the end; Method B (2020-2022) built curves after each dataset and discarded the samples.** [Certain] "Method C retains the SGS elevations in memory throughout the pre-processing stage" Source: 2023-03 release notes s.2.2.2, https://docs.tuflow.com/classic-hpc/release/2023-03-AC/TwoDDomConstr-1.html

6. **TUFLOW stores only the curves, not where along a face the high or low points are. This is why an SGS cell straddling a levee leaks: the face curve contains the low ground on both sides of the crest. TUFLOW's answer is a mandatory ridge breakline on every hydraulic control, more so with SGS than without.** [Certain] "TUFLOW does not store information defining the specific locations within the cell or along the cell face where the low or high points in the topography occur." Source: Manual 2023-03 s.7.4.3.1.2. This is the single most important finding for Wadi2D: the "faces that know the ground along them" requirement (decision 2) is precisely what TUFLOW's SGS does not keep.

7. **TUFLOW ships an automatic ridge finder for this weakness ("SGS Breakline Detection Delta"): for each cell it finds the lowest water level that gives a continuous wet path left-to-right and top-to-bottom, subtracts the higher of the two face inverts, and flags cells where that depth exceeds a user threshold (CHECK 3543).** [Certain] "The maximum invert elevation of the left and right faces is subtracted from the left-right minimum water level and reports it as uDelta." Source: Manual 2023-03 s.7.4.3.1.2.

8. **With SGS, gully (MIN/LOWER) lines are not recommended because they over-estimate conveyance; with a good DEM they are unnecessary. Ryan et al. 2022 found the same on the Johnstone River (hydrologic enforcement helps only on poor SRTM terrain).** [Certain] "Including gully lines can overestimate conveyance in these channels." Source: Manual 2023-03 s.7.4.3; Ryan et al. 2022, https://www.tuflow.com/media/7545/2022-direct-rainfall-hydraulic-model-validation-ryan-et-al-hwrs.pdf

### B. Manning friction with SGS

9. **With SGS the "radius" in Manning's equation is itself a curve of elevation. The default is the resistance radius R = A/W (flow area over flow width, the usual 2D-solver choice); a hydraulic radius A/P option exists but was flagged as under development with possible effects on time-stepping and cell-size sensitivity.** [Certain] "For Resistance Radius the radius value is equal to the flow area divided by the flow width." Source: 2020-01 release notes s.3.2.3.

10. **The bed-resistance term and the face velocities are "controlled by the conveyance properties of each cell face"; the momentum equation itself is the same full 2D solution as without SGS. The non-SGS upwinding of the friction depth (HPC Mannings Depth Approach) does not apply to SGS, which "use[s] a different formulation again".** [Certain] "The bed resistance term (e.g. Manning's equation) and the determination of the 2D velocities are controlled by the conveyance properties of each cell face." Source: Manual 2023-03 s.7.4.3; 2020-01 release notes s.5.3.

11. **A conveyance-weighted friction exists but is switched off. Method C's "SGS Materials" samples Manning's n at the same points as the elevations along the face and does a parallel-channel conveyance sum (1D cross-section style), K(H) = sum (1/n_i) A_i d_i^(2/3), with depth-varying n evaluated at each sample's own depth. It was beta in 2020-10, produced wrong results with depth-varying n (June 2022 notice), and was disabled in 2023-03 "until further research and benchmarking is completed".** [Certain] "For the 2023-03 Release the sub-grid sampling of materials (land-use) has been disabled." Source: 2020-10-AD release notes s.3.7.3, https://downloads.tuflow.com/TUFLOW/Releases/2020-10/TUFLOW%20Release%20Notes.2020-10-AD.pdf; 2023-03 release notes s.2.2.4. In the shipped default, therefore, each face has one n (materials are sampled at face centres) applied to the whole SGS face area, and depth-varying n is evaluated on a depth from the lowest sampled elevation [Likely, from the 2020-10 wording "not the lowest elevation as is used by the other SGS methods"].

12. **Friction is solved implicitly within each explicit stage: the friction term is written in terms of the end-of-step velocity and solved iteratively, because the explicit form "requires very small time-steps to solve for u when the water depth is shallow". Stability add-ons: Manning's n may change by at most 10 % per time step (depth-varying n or log-law), and the log-law n is capped at 10 below the singularity depth Ks/11.** [Certain] "limiting the relative rate of change of Manning's n value to no more than 10% per time step avoids these stability issues" Source: Collecutt and Syme 2017 eq. 4, https://www.tuflow.com/media/5102/2017-experimental-benchmarking-of-mesh-size-and-time-step-convergence-for-a-1st-and-2nd-order-swe-finite-volume-scheme-collecutt-and-syme-iahr.pdf; 2020-10-AD release notes s.5.8.

### C. Momentum on a partially wet face

13. **A partially wet face carries one velocity and one flux; the velocity is the depth-and-width average over the wetted part of the face area curve. One water level per cell, one velocity per face, exactly as without SGS.** [Certain] "TUFLOW still stores only one velocity value per face and one depth value per cell." Source: Quadtree and SGS FAQ, https://wiki.tuflow.com/Quadtree_and_Sub-Grid_Sampling_FAQ

14. **Partially wet cells and faces are what removes the saw-tooth wet/dry artefact: cells whose centre is above water still convey flow through the wet part of their faces, so a channel at 15, 30 or 45 degrees to the grid obeys Manning's equation and shows no spurious head loss.** [Certain] "Dry cells are now partially wet cells and irregular velocity patterns have disappeared. Uniform flow is achieved." Source: Ryan, Gao and Syme 2019 poster, https://www.tuflow.com/media/4975/201911-utilisation-of-sub-grid-sampling-of-bed-elevation-data-in-gridded-2d-swe-schemes-poster-paper-iahr-rcem-nz.pdf; Kitts et al. 2020, https://www.tuflow.com/media/5022/2020-mesh-orientation-and-cell-size-sensitivity-in-2d-swe-solvers-kitts-et-al-iahr-river-flow-delft.pdf

15. **HPC evolves momentum on every face with at least one wet neighbour, including faces that are dry because the wet cell's level is below the other cell's invert. That produced a run-away velocity on dry faces that drove the time step down; the fix ("HPC Dry Face Inertia Approach == Method B", 2020-10-AB) is the documented default.** [Certain] "the momentum equations are evolved on any face that has at least one adjacent wet cell" Source: 2020-10-AD release notes s.5.8.3.

16. **Without SGS the face depth is upwinded (depth of the cell the water flows from) and reduced by any upward bed step, floored at zero; the 2nd-order scheme is a hybrid that blends to first-order upwinding where the three-cell velocity profile reverses. How the SGS curves enter the momentum balance (which water level at a face between two partially wet cells, how V(eta) and A(eta) enter the pressure-gradient term, well-balancing) is not published in any source read.** [Certain for the first two clauses; the last clause is a documented gap] "the water depth at the face was reduced by the difference in bed elevations, and limited to be non-negative." Source: Collecutt and Syme 2017 s.2.

### D. Cell sizes and reported accuracy gains

17. **TUFLOW's cell-size rule (manual Table 3.1): Classic 4-6 cells across a flow path, HPC without SGS 6-8, HPC with SGS 3-4, the last on condition that the terrain data is at least twice as fine as the cell.** [Certain] "TUFLOW HPC with SGS | 3-4 | Very Low (assumes terrain data is finer by a factor of 2 or more)" Source: Manual 2025.2 s.3.2.2, https://docs.tuflow.com/classic-hpc/manual/2025.2/ModelResolutionDiscretisation-2.html

18. **Brisbane River 2011 (10 km reach, up to 30 m deep, 4 m/s, nine flood marks, criterion +/-0.2 m): 2nd-order SGS stays within the criterion at every cell size from 20 m to 60 m (worst -0.22 m at 60 m); 2nd-order traditional sampling only at 30 m or finer (+0.57 m at 60 m); 1st-order traditional fails at every size (+0.24 to +0.77 m at 20 m, +0.94 to +2.28 m at 60 m). Run times of the three schemes are within a few per cent of each other.** [Certain] "2nd Order SGS: Modelled water levels are in good agreement with the recorded peak flood levels when the model 2D cell resolution is equal to or less than 50 x 50 metres." Source: Huxley et al. 2022 Table 1, https://www.tuflow.com/media/7555/2022-hydraulic-modelling-2d-cell-size-result-convergence-huxley-et-al-hwrs.pdf

19. **Throsby Creek (urban rain-on-grid, 3-12 m cells): SGS converged at the 20 m wide channel below 12 m cells and at the 12 m wide drain below 9 m cells; traditional sampling never converged at the 12 m drain; SGS cost 12-50 % more run time (12 m: 31 vs 29 min; 3 m: 5:59 vs 5:00 h).** [Certain] "The simulation slow down ranges from 12% to 50%." Source: Huxley et al. 2022 Table 2 and findings.

20. **Mesh orientation: a 1000 m x 100 m rectangular channel at 0, 15, 30 and 45 degrees to the grid, 5 to 50 m cells, reproduces Manning's water surface at every orientation and size with SGS; without SGS only the aligned case does, and the error grows with cell size. The De Vriend U-bend (1.7 m wide, R 4.25 m, 0.189 m3/s, n 0.0125) matches measurements with SGS at 0.05-0.34 m cells, and over-predicts the upstream level at every size without.** [Certain] "the sensitivity of results to mesh size is greatly reduced and the sensitivity to mesh orientation is almost eliminated." Source: Kitts et al. 2020 (URL in 14).

21. **The HPC scheme itself (without SGS) converges with 8 or more cells across a feature but predicts bend head loss acceptably with 3; time-step convergence was reached at 0.8 of the control-number limits, and 0.9-1.0 showed small deviations. Recommended practice: 3+ cells, 2nd order, control numbers at 0.8 of default, minimum constant viscosity.** [Certain] "Convergence typically required 8 cells or more across feature widths, however meshes as coarse as three cells across feature widths still appear to perform well" Source: Collecutt and Syme 2017 s.6.

22. **Run-time economics of SGS as TUFLOW reports them: +20-30 % per run on a well-designed model; Johnstone River catchment from 26 h to 4 h by coarsening with SGS; a 20 m SGS model reproducing a 5 m quadtree result 5 times faster; an 11.6 km2 catchment at 20 m with SGS in 45 s on a Titan Xp (24 h event); 100 m SGS cells simulate 12 days of the Johnstone River in 20 min; finer SGS sampling does not improve velocity or hazard, only a smaller cell does.** [Certain] "Turning SGS on can increase run times by 20-30% for a model that is well designed with appropriate cell sizes." Sources: FAQ (URL in 13); https://www.tuflow.com/insights/2020_05-sgs/; Ryan et al. 2022 (URL in 8).

23. **First-order upwinding is rejected by BMT's own evidence: numerically diffusive, needs lower n to calibrate, differences "can exceed 10% of the primary flow path depth" in deep fast water; Smagorinsky-only viscosity does not converge as cells shrink below the flow depth, which is why HPC moved to the Wu depth-scaled model in 2020.** [Certain] "The Smagorinsky model, formulated as a 2D sub-grid-scale LES eddy viscosity model, performed poorly at cell sizes similar to or smaller than the fluid depth." Sources: HPC FAQ, https://wiki.tuflow.com/HPC_FAQ; Collecutt et al. 2020, https://www.tuflow.com/media/5023/2020-mesh-size-insensitive-turbulence-modelling-for-the-2d-shallow-water-equations-collecutt-et-al-iahr-river-flow-delft.pdf

### E. SGS on steep slopes

24. **The documented steep-slope problem is an output artefact, not a solver one: the solver works with cell-averaged depth (volume / cell area); the water-level output assumes the cell fills from its lowest sampled point, so for thin sheet flow on a steep cell the cell-centre water level sits below the ground at the centre and the depth maps negative. Fixed by "SGS Depth Output == CELL AVERAGE", the default since 2023-03; the water level output still sits below the DEM in such cells (acknowledged as a current limitation in version 2025.1.1).** [Certain] "This mismatch occurs at sheet flow cells, where thin layers of water flow over a steep slope." Source: 2020-10-AD release notes s.7.5.6; https://wiki.tuflow.com/TUFLOW_Output_Discussion

25. **SGS still computes one water level at the cell mid-point, so in steep areas or at sudden changes of topography the interpolated water surface remains as coarse as the cell; BMT's only remedy is a finer cell (quadtree).** [Certain] "In steep areas, or where there are sudden changes in topography the interpolation of water levels will still be subject to the coarseness of the 2D cell sizes." Source: 2020-01 release notes s.7.6; repeated in 2020-10-AD s.7.6.

26. **Rain on steep slopes without SGS makes cells flicker between wet and dry as sheet flow passes in waves near the 2 mm threshold, hence the 0.2 mm recommendation; with SGS the wetting and drying happens on the storage curve and the lower threshold is unnecessary.** [Certain] "for models with long periods of light to moderate rates of rainfall or steap slopes, the water depths may be close to the threshold" Source: Manual 2023-03 s.8 (rainfall boundaries); https://wiki.tuflow.com/Direct_Rainfall_(Rain_on_Grid)_Modelling_Guidance

27. **Linear corner interpolation between SGS cells with different minimum elevations gives bumpy water levels along narrow streams; the 2023-03 high-resolution output adds sheet-flow checks and weights corners by the number of wet SGS samples (HR Interpolation Method C), and "ON CELL SIDES" stops interpolation across thin breaklines. Maximum hazard on very steep slopes is sensitive to the corner interpolation method.** [Certain] "maximum hazard values on very steep slopes may experience a more significant change." Source: 2023-03 release notes s.2.2.5-2.2.6; Manual 2023-03 s.11.2.2.1.

28. **No source read gives a slope limit or an accuracy figure for SGS on steep ground; the only hydraulics-side statements are that SGS "vastly reduced the choking of narrow flow paths" and that HPC reproduces EA Test 6A supercritical flow and moving jumps. Classic's supercritical option (Manning with the bed slope as the friction slope) does not exist in HPC.** [Certain that the sources are silent] Source: FAQ (URL in 13); Collecutt and Syme 2017 s.3.

### F. Thin and thick Z lines, weir flow, staircase crest length

29. **A 2d_zsh line with width 0 is thin and modifies only the cell-side and corner elevations (ZU, ZV, ZH); width between 0 and 1.5 cell sizes is thick and modifies the whole cells along the line (ZC as well); wider than that is a wide line with all points in a buffer of half the width. RIDGE/MAX only raises, GULLY/MIN only lowers. A thin line does not touch the cell centre, so cell storage is unchanged and water is held back until the line is overtopped.** [Certain] "Thin lines modify cell faces (ZH, ZU and ZV Zpts)." Source: Manual 2025.2 s.7.2.6.2, https://docs.tuflow.com/classic-hpc/manual/2025.2/Common-2.html

30. **Faces wet and dry independently of the cell when the cell is wet; that is the mechanism that lets a thin line represent fences and thin levees relative to the cell size.** [Certain] "Can wet and dry when the whole cell is wet ... This allows for the modelling of 'thin' obstructions such as fences and thin embankments" Source: Manual 2023-03 s.7.2.3.1.2.

31. **Under SGS a thin line's face is not flat: the default "SGS Z Shape Line Approach == Method B" applies a gradient along the face through the two corners and the mid-side value (three points), and for thick lines a sloping cell area for the volume; Method A makes the face flat. So TUFLOW's breakline face carries a 3-point crest profile, never the surveyed crest.** [Certain] "The default, Method B, applies a gradient along the face based on the cell corners and cell side Zpt values" Source: Manual 2023-03 s.7.4.3.1.2.

32. **Weir flow over breaklines: since 2023-03 HPC applies the full broad-crested weir equation with Villemonte submergence on thin and thick breakline faces ("HPC Weir Approach == Method B Energy", upstream energy head above the crest); Method B uses the upstream water level; Method A is the pre-2023 water-level-gradient limiter that was "calibrated to match the broad-crested weir formula". Defaults Cd 0.577, Ex 1.5, a 8.55, b 0.556, calibration factor Cf 1.0 (global Set WrF or GIS WrF); separate parameter sets for thin and thick lines.** [Certain] "'Method B Energy' is the default approach. It applies the weir equation and uses the upstream energy level above the weir invert" Source: Manual 2023-03 s.7.4.4; 2023-03 release notes s.5.2, https://docs.tuflow.com/classic-hpc/release/2023-03-AD/TwoDSolv-1.html

33. **The weir length W is "the length of the weir face", i.e. the cell side. Nothing in the manual, release notes or wiki read mentions the stair-stepped length of a diagonal breakline or any correction for it; the only knobs are Cf/WrF. For walls (dry cells) the scheme does correct staircase effects by modifying effective face widths so the flow stays parallel to the average wall line.** [Certain that no crest-length statement exists in the sources read; the wall treatment is Certain] "W = Flow width. For the 2D weir approach, the length of the weir face" Source: 2023-03 release notes s.5.2; Collecutt and Syme 2017 s.2 (wall steps).

34. **In the staircase, the sum of face lengths along a straight crest at angle theta to the grid is L(|cos theta| + |sin theta|), up to 1.414 L at 45 degrees; with W = face length the weir discharge over an unsubmerged crest is over-estimated by the same factor unless Cf is set to compensate.** [Certain, arithmetic] (No TUFLOW source; derived.)

35. **Thick breaklines in Quadtree now select all faces around a raised cell "as this is required for the new HPC weir flow approach"; and a depth adjustment on faces parallel to the flow was removed for thick lines (minor result changes).** [Certain] "all faces around a raised cell are selected, as this is required for the new HPC weir flow approach" Source: 2023-03 release notes s.5.7.5, 5.8.5.

### G. The HPC adaptive time step

36. **Three control numbers, all evaluated everywhere every step; the step is the largest that keeps all three under their limits: Courant Nu = max(|u|dt/dx, |v|dt/dy) <= 1.0; shallow-wave celerity Nc = max(sqrt(2gh) dt/dx, sqrt(2gh) dt/dy) <= 1.0; diffusion Nd = max(nu_T dt/dx^2, nu_T dt/dy^2) <= 0.3, with nu_T the eddy viscosity.** [Certain] "TUFLOW will use the highest timestep possible without exceeding the limits associated with each of the control numbers." Sources: Manual 2025.2 s.3.4.4, https://docs.tuflow.com/classic-hpc/manual/2025.2/TimestepBackground-2.html; https://wiki.tuflow.com/HPC_Adaptive_Timestepping; 2018 slides, https://tuflow.com/media/4964/201804-tuflow-hpc-overview-tuflow-conference-uk.pdf

37. **The 2017 paper writes the celerity number with sqrt(gh) and a target of 1.6, which is 2.3 times looser than the manual's sqrt(2gh) <= 1.0. The manual is the current rule; the discrepancy is recorded as an open issue.** [Certain that the two sources differ] "The time step was adjusted to maintain these at or below 1.0, 1.6, and 0.3 respectively." Source: Collecutt and Syme 2017 eq. 8.

38. **The step is predicted from the previous step's state, so it can be too large; the state at the start of each step is retained, and if any control number is exceeded by more than 20 % anywhere, or a NaN appears, the step is rejected, the step size reduced and the step repeated. Ten consecutive repeats stop the run; so does reaching the minimum step, 0.1 s by default. The user may scale all limits ("Control Number Factor == 0.8" is the suggested sensitivity test; 20 % under-clocking costs 20 % run time).** [Certain] "Should a timestep need to be repeated more than ten times consecutively, the solution stops." Source: Manual 2023-03 s.3.5.4; wiki (URL in 36); 2018 slides.

39. **After a rejection the step is halved (FAQ) and a dynamic factor then recovers extremely slowly (0.001 % per step, about 10,000 steps); the 2025.2 manual describes the factor as dropping to 90 % of its value per repeat. The 2025.0 default "HPC Timestep Approach == Method B" tests against the static limits, which tolerates rapid wetting and drying better. The first step uses the TCF Timestep (1/2 to 1/5 of the cell size in metres) divided by 10.** [Certain for each statement; the two sources disagree on the reduction factor] "The control number is then allowed to increase only by 0.001% per timestep." Sources: https://wiki.tuflow.com/HPC_FAQ; Manual 2025.2 s.3.4.4; https://docs.tuflow.com/classic-hpc/manual/2025.0/Features-and-Changes-1.html

40. **When the model is dry the step would grow without bound, so "Timestep Maximum == Auto" caps it at dx / sqrt(g h_wd) with h_wd the cell wet/dry depth: 35.7 s for 5 m cells and 2 mm.** [Certain] "so for a 5m cell size with a 2mm wet/dry depth the maximum timestep is 35.7 seconds." Source: 2020-10-AD release notes s.5.6.

41. **Which number binds is diagnostic: celerity in deep cells (erroneous deep cells shrink the step everywhere; a storage reduction factor shrinks the effective dx), Courant at high velocity, diffusion in small cells in deep water; diffusion-controlled models "tend to require a timestep significantly smaller", and the remedy offered is a larger cell. Since 2023-03 the Wu viscosity uses n capped at 0.1 so that high roughness does not throttle the step. A "timestep efficiency" of 100 % means the step equals the stability minimum; output synchronisation and 1D linking lower it.** [Certain] "Models controlled by the diffusion number tend to require a timestep significantly smaller than those controlled by the shallow wave celerity or courant numbers." Source: Manual 2023-03 s.3.5.4; 2023-03 release notes s.5.7.1.

### H. Wet/dry depth

42. **Cell Wet/Dry Depth default 0.002 m (0.007 ft) for Classic and HPC; the cell is wet when its level exceeds ZC plus this depth; Classic also has a Cell Side Wet/Dry Depth (0.001 m). 0.0002 m is recommended for rain-on-grid without SGS; with SGS the default is kept. The defaults changed from 0.05 m and 0.03 m in the 2006-06 release.** [Certain] "a Cell Wet/Dry Depth of 0.0002 m ... is recommended for both Classic and HPC solution schemes, unless using SGS." Sources: Manual 2023-03 s.8 and s.7.2.3.1.1; Default changes page (URL in 39).

43. **With SGS, "a cell is wet if any part of the cell is wet" for the water-level output, while depth-based outputs treat it as wet only when the level exceeds the cell-centre SGS elevation; partially wet cells are reported as wet, so SGS flood extents include the whole wet/dry fringe and need no buffering. Rainfall is applied to the whole cell area; evaporation and infiltration rate are scaled by the wet fraction; initial loss and soil capacity use the whole area.** [Certain] "For negative rainfall (evaporation), the volume of evaporation is factored by the wet area fraction of the cell." Source: Manual 2023-03 s.7.4.3.1.2; FAQ (URL in 13).

### I. The 2020 quadtree and its level interfaces

44. **Quadtree (2020-01) divides square cells recursively into four, all levels sharing one orientation, up to 9 levels (1/256 of the base cell); a face may adjoin at most two faces, so refinement must step through intermediate levels, and a cell may have up to 8 smaller neighbours. Intermediate rings are generated automatically; only active cells are stored.** [Certain] "Each 2D cell face can have a maximum of 2 adjoining cell faces, meaning that multiple levels of refinement will need to transition through the intermediate resolutions." Source: 2020-01 release notes s.3.1.1.

45. **At a level interface the full SWE are solved with every term, no hidden 1D nodes (the Classic multi-domain trick), and BMT claims no artefacts or reflections; the larger cell's face is split into two half-faces, each with its own velocity and depth, and the momentum control volume across a 2:1 face is a rectangle spanning both cell sizes. One adaptive time step serves the whole mesh.** [Certain] "The full 2D SWE are applied across changes in cell resolution, with complete computation of all the 2D SWE terms" Sources: 2020-01 release notes s.3.1.1; Gao et al. 2020 eq. 1-2, https://www.tuflow.com/media/5021/2020-high-resolution-numerical-modelling-of-tsunami-inundation-using-quadtree-method-gao-et-al-iahr-apd-japan.pdf; wiki (URL in 36).

46. **Costs and rules of thumb: a single-level quadtree is about 20 % slower than the plain HPC grid; quadtree pays once there are at least three levels, and a cell-count cut of only 30 % may run slower; keep one cell size within a channel bed and across its active width; a Seaside tsunami flume case cut cells by 75 % and ran 4.3 times faster (3.2 times with SGS). SGS sample frequency can be set per nesting level (e.g. 21, 11, 5).** [Certain] "running a model with a single level Quadtree grid identical to a HPC grid is always slower, on average 20%." Sources: FAQ (URL in 13); Gao et al. 2020 Table 1; 2020-10-AD release notes s.3.7.2.

## Equations and algorithms

Notation: h depth, eta water level, z bed, u, v velocities, dx, dy cell sizes, dt step, g gravity, n Manning, A cell area, Phi_i face volume flux, nu_T eddy viscosity.

**E1. HPC finite-volume scheme without SGS (Collecutt and Syme 2017, eq. 1-4, 7).**
Continuity: A dh/dt = sum_i Phi_i + S_Q, with Phi_1 = dy (u_cc + u_n1)/2 * h_1, Phi_2 = -dy (u_cc + u_n2)/2 * h_2, Phi_3 = dx (v_cc + v_n3)/2 * h_3, Phi_4 = -dx (v_cc + v_n4)/2 * h_4. The face depth h_i is the depth of the upwind cell (if u_cc + u_n1 > 0 then h_n1 else h_cc), reduced by the bed-elevation difference when the downwind bed is higher, floored at zero. Face widths may be scaled by a flow reduction factor, A by a storage reduction factor.
Momentum (x): A d(hu)/dt = sum_i Phi_i u_i - A g n^2 (u^2 + v^2)^(1/2) u / h^(1/3) - A g h d(z + h)/dx, plus viscosity terms dy[h_2 nu_T2 (du/dx)_2 - h_1 nu_T1 (du/dx)_1] + dx[h_4 nu_T4 (du/dy)_4 - h_3 nu_T3 (du/dy)_3].
Implicit friction: u' = (u + dt u_dot) / (1 + a (u'^2 + v'^2)^(1/2)), u_dot = (1/h)[sum_i Phi_i u_i / A - u dh/dt] - g d(z + h)/dx, a = dt g n^2 / h^(4/3); u', v' solved together iteratively, then the effective derivatives are passed to the RK4 integrator.
Hybrid 2nd-order face velocity (left face, positive flux): u_i = (1 - xi) u_n1 + xi u_cc, xi = 2x(1 - x), x = (u_n1 - u_11)/(u_cc - u_11) clamped to [0, 1]; pure 2nd order where the three-cell profile has no curvature, pure upwind where it reverses.
Time integration: classical 4th-order Runge-Kutta; every cell's derivatives are local (thread per cell), with a handful of global reductions per step.

**E2. Control numbers (manual 2025.2 s.3.4.4; 2017 eq. 8).**
Nu = max(|u| dt/dx, |v| dt/dy) <= 1.0; Nc = max(sqrt(2 g h) dt/dx, sqrt(2 g h) dt/dy) <= 1.0; Nd = max(nu_T dt/dx^2, nu_T dt/dy^2) <= 0.3. (2017 paper: Nc with sqrt(g h) and limit 1.6.) Step = largest dt satisfying all three over all cells, from the end-of-previous-step state; reject if any exceeds 1.2 x limit or any NaN; retain the start-of-step state; halve (FAQ) or x0.9 (manual 2025.2) and repeat; stop after 10 consecutive repeats or dt < 0.1 s. All-dry cap: dt_max = dx / sqrt(g h_wd). Classic (for reference): Cr = dt sqrt(2 g H)/dx, stable to about 10, usually <= 5.

**E3. Wu eddy viscosity (Collecutt et al. 2020 eq. 9; HPC default since 2020).**
nu_T = sqrt(nu_2D^2 + nu_3D^2); nu_2D = C_2D l_m^2 |S_2D|; nu_3D = C_3D l_m U_*; U_* = sqrt(tau_bed/rho) = |U| n sqrt(g) / h^(1/6); l_m = min(h, horizontal distance to the nearest dry boundary). Since 2023-03 the n used inside this formula is capped at 0.1 (bed friction itself not capped). nu_T feeds Nd.

**E4. SGS tables (2020-01 s.3.2.3; manual 2023-03 s.7.4.3).**
Sampling: f = round_up_to_odd(cell size / target distance + 1) points per face, f^2 per cell on a regular grid including corners and mid-sides; default 11 (121), cap 31, hard limit 127 (16,129).
Per cell: V(eta) from wetted area versus depth above the lowest sample, z_min, z_max (fully wet), z_mean, z_median also stored for outputs. Per face: A_f(eta) flow area, W_f(eta) flow width, and a conveyance curve K_f(eta); resistance radius R(eta) = A_f/W_f (default), hydraulic radius A_f/P optional (under development). Velocity at a face = flux / A_f(eta). Computational depth in a cell = V/A (cell average). Method C keeps the raw samples until all geometry modifiers are applied, then builds the curves; sample positions are the same for all datasets.

**E5. SGS material (parallel-channel) conveyance, beta 2020-10, disabled 2023-03 (2020-10-AD s.3.7.3).**
At a face with samples i of area A_i, depth d_i and roughness n_i (n_i may depend on d_i): K(H) = sum_i (1/n_i) A_i d_i^(2/3); equivalent roughness n(H) = A_total d_avg^(2/3) / K(H), d_avg = A_total / wetted width. Resistance radius only.

**E6. Breakline detection delta (manual 2023-03 s.7.4.3.1.2).**
For each cell: eta_LR = minimum water level giving a continuous wet path from left face to right face through the sampled cell; uDelta = eta_LR - max(z_invert,left, z_invert,right); likewise vDelta top to bottom; SGS_Delta_Z = max(uDelta, vDelta) written to the grid check file; CHECK 3543 where it exceeds the user value (example 1 m). Adds significant initialisation time; run during set-up only.

**E7. Z-line geometry (manual 2025.2 s.7.2.6.2; 2023-03 s.7.4.3.1.2).**
Width w = 0: thin, modify ZU, ZV, ZH on the sides the line crosses (cell cross-hair test; "spider web" in quadtree). 0 < w <= 1.5 dx: thick, modify ZC, ZU, ZV, ZH of every cell the line touches. w > 1.5 dx: wide, all points within w/2. Options MAX/RIDGE raise only, MIN/GULLY lower only, ADD adds dZ. Under SGS (Method B) a thin face's profile is the straight gradient through (ZH_corner1, ZU or ZV mid-side, ZH_corner2); thick cells get a sloping cell area for V(eta).

**E8. HPC weir on breakline faces (2023-03 s.5.2; manual s.7.4.4).**
Q = (2/3) Cf Cd Csf W sqrt(2 g) Hu^Ex, Csf = (1 - (Hd/Hu)^a)^b (Villemonte). Cd 0.577, Ex 1.5, a 8.55, b 0.556 (broad-crested), Cf 1.0; W = length of the weir face; Hu = upstream energy head above the crest (Method B Energy, default) or water level (Method B); Hd = downstream water level above the crest. Applied where flow on a thin or thick breakline face is upstream controlled; Method A instead limits the water-level gradient. Staircase length (derived, not TUFLOW): a straight crest at angle theta has total face length L (|cos theta| + |sin theta|), 1.414 L at 45 degrees.

**E9. Quadtree 2:1 face (Gao et al. 2020 eq. 1-2).**
A_c dh_c/dt = sum phi_i + S_Q; full faces phi_1 = dy u_1 h_1, phi_3 = dx v_3 h_3, phi_4 = dx v_4 h_4; a face shared with two smaller cells is split: phi_2,1 = (dy/2) u_2,1 h_2,1, phi_2,2 = (dy/2) u_2,2 h_2,2; momentum control volume for a half-face is a rectangle spanning the two cell sizes. One global dt.

## Recommendation for Wadi2D

1. **Adopt the SGS table structure, but keep the along-face profile.** Build V(eta) per cell and A_f(eta), W_f(eta), K_f(eta) per face from the 0.5 m DEM with an odd sample count per face (mid-face and corners included), 11-31 samples per face, never finer than the DEM (finding 3-4). Unlike TUFLOW, store for every face the crest elevation of any breakline that crosses it and the position-ordered profile (or at least z_max and the two sub-face widths each side of the crest), so that a face straddled by a ridge blocks flow until the ridge is overtopped (finding 6). That is the one real differentiator over TUFLOW SGS; it costs a few bytes per face.

2. **Implement TUFLOW's breakline-detection delta as an automatic ridge finder in the mesher (E6).** Cheap, published, and it converts "put a breakline on every levee" from a modeller's duty into a check the UI can show. Snap faces to detected ridges above a threshold (say 0.3-0.5 m in wadi terrain [Guessing]) and carry the crest profile from the DEM.

3. **Friction: conveyance-weighted faces, validated before shipped.** Use the parallel-channel conveyance K(eta) = sum (1/n_i) A_i d_i^(2/3) with n_i sampled at the elevation samples (E5), resistance radius R = A/W as default (finding 9). TUFLOW built exactly this, broke it with depth-varying n and switched it off in 2023-03 (finding 11); so Wadi2D must pass the rotated-channel uniform-flow test (0/15/30/45 degrees, 5-50 m cells) and the De Vriend U-bend with it on before it becomes the default. Solve friction implicitly per stage (E1) and limit the per-step change of n to 10 % when n varies with depth (finding 12); thin sheet flow on 30 % hill slopes is exactly where the explicit form fails.

4. **Momentum on partially wet faces.** One velocity per face = flux / A_f(eta); evolve momentum only on faces with a wet neighbour and kill the dry-face inertia runaway (finding 15). Because TUFLOW does not publish how V(eta) and A_f(eta) enter the pressure-gradient term (finding 16), Wadi2D needs its own derivation with a still-water-on-a-slope test (decision 1's well-balancing) as the acceptance gate; do not assume the Casulli-style sub-grid formulation and TUFLOW's are the same.

5. **Weir flow over breakline faces: adopt the equation, fix the length.** Broad-crested weir with Villemonte submergence and upstream energy head, TUFLOW's 2023 default coefficients (E8), applied when the face is upstream controlled. Set W to the true crest length inside the face (from the snapped breakline segment, known because of recommendation 1), not the face length; a diagonal crest otherwise discharges up to 41 % too much (finding 34). Reject the gradient-limiter approach (Method A); BMT replaced it.

6. **Time step: the three control numbers with a global reduction, run at 0.8 of the limits.** Nu <= 1, Nc <= 1 with sqrt(2gh), Nd <= 0.3 (E2); reject-and-repeat on > 20 % exceedance or NaN with the start-of-step state retained (RK4 keeps it anyway); halve on repeat; dt_max = dx/sqrt(g h_wd); abort below 0.1 s. The diffusion number is the hidden killer for fine cells in deep reservoirs (finding 41): with 2-5 m cells behind a dam, Nd will bind unless nu_T is depth-scaled (Wu, E3) and capped. This evidence supports evaluating local time stepping (decision 1) and the steep-cell strategy already recorded in the repo (decision 18 [Likely, seen only as a commit title]): the celerity bound comes from the deepest cells, the Courant bound from the steepest fast ones, and TUFLOW accepts the global minimum of both.

7. **Wet/dry on the storage curve, depth stored.** TUFLOW computes with cell-averaged depth = V/A (finding 24), which is what decision 3 chose; wet when V > 0 on the curve, faces active when the level exceeds the face's lowest sample plus about 2 mm, no 0.2 mm tricks (finding 26, 42). Report both cell-average depth and water level; expect, and handle in the mapper, water levels below ground on steep sheet-flow cells (finding 24-25, 27): high-resolution depth = water level minus DEM only where the SGS fill logic says the sample is wet, with interpolation blocked across breakline faces.

8. **Mesh: uniform grid first, quadtree-ready faces.** TUFLOW's quadtree evidence (2:1 half-faces, rectangular momentum control volume, one global dt, +20 % overhead, pays only with 3+ levels, keep one cell size along a channel; findings 44-46) says the gain is real for the mountains-plus-plain case but only when the refinement ratio is large. Keep the version-1 uniform grid (decision 17 [Likely, commit title]) but give faces the "up to two adjoining faces" adjacency now so the quadtree is a data-structure change, not a solver rewrite; put refinement bands on the delineated wadis as planned (decision 4) and never change cell size inside a wadi bed.

9. **Rejections, with reasons.** (a) First-order upwind as the production scheme: +0.2 to +2.3 m on the Brisbane River and recalibrated n (finding 18, 23). (b) Smagorinsky-only viscosity: non-convergent once cells are smaller than the depth (finding 23); use Wu. (c) Gully/MIN lines to hydro-enforce the 0.5 m DEM when SGS tables are built: over-estimate conveyance (finding 8); keep burn lines only for designed channels (decision 10). (d) A single n per face applied to the whole SGS area (TUFLOW's shipped default) as the long-term friction model: it is what TUFLOW fell back to, not what it wanted. (e) Face length as weir length (finding 33-34).

10. **Validation to add to the ladder (decision 12):** the Kitts rotated 100 m channel (0/15/30/45 degrees, 5-50 m), the De Vriend U-bend, the Malone 90 degree bend (loss factor 1.23), and a cell-size convergence test at 20/40/60 m against a 5 m run on one Block 2 wadi; the claim to make to reviewers is TUFLOW's own: 3-4 cells across a wadi with SGS, orientation-independent.

## Open issues

1. TUFLOW's SGS momentum discretisation (face water level between two partially wet cells, pressure-gradient term with V(eta)/A(eta), well-balancing, the "different formulation" of friction depth) is not published; Wadi2D's must be derived and tested independently.
2. The staircase crest length of a diagonal thin line is not addressed anywhere in TUFLOW's documentation; whether the Cf/WrF defaults or the 0.577 coefficient silently absorb it is unknown. A single HPC test (straight levee at 0 and 45 degrees, same crest, same head) would settle it; Wadi2D's benchmark should include it.
3. Celerity limit: 1.6 with sqrt(gh) (2017 paper) versus 1.0 with sqrt(2gh) (manual, wiki, 2018 slides). Also the repeat-step reduction: halve (FAQ) versus x0.9 (manual 2025.2). Both to be resolved by Wadi2D's own time-step convergence tests, not by TUFLOW's text.
4. Whether SGS Materials (conveyance-weighted friction with depth-varying n) was reinstated after 2023-03: the 2025 manuals list the command but no source read states its status.
5. No slope limit or accuracy figure for SGS on steep ground exists in TUFLOW's documents; the steep-slope material is entirely about outputs. The hydraulics on 20-40 % hill cells with thin sheet flow is untested territory for both tools.
6. The "Journal of Hydrology" SGS paper in the task brief was not found; the search budget was exhausted before the 2025.x HPC-specific manual chapter (s.7.3.3) could be read in full; the wiki Topography Guidance was read and holds nothing on SGS or steep terrain.
7. Default C_2D/C_3D of the Wu model were not confirmed from a primary source (the 2020-01 notes mention C_2D = 4 "as a suitable starting point" only).
8. Decisions 17 and 18 of the repo (uniform grid for version 1; steep-cell time-step strategy) were seen only as commit titles; the recommendation references them on that basis.

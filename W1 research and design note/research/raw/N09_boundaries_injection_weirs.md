# N09 Boundaries, hydrograph injection, outflow conditions, crest faces, weirs and spillways

Date: 2026-10-29

## Scope

How a lumped-hydrology hydrograph should enter a 2D shallow-water domain (volume source in cells versus boundary flux; how HEC-RAS 2D, TUFLOW, ICM, 3Di, LISFLOOD-FP, Iber and SERGHEI distribute an inflow and whether they give it momentum); what outflow boundaries exist (free outfall / transmissive, normal depth with a slope, critical depth, rating curve, fixed or tidal stage) and what their reflection and stability problems are; how a weir law is applied on an internal cell face (TUFLOW HPC and Classic, HEC-RAS SA/2D connections and the 1D weir library, LISFLOOD-FP .weir, Iber internal conditions, 3Di obstacles and 1D weirs), with submergence (Villemonte and the broad-crested and roadway curves), the crest-length problem on a staircase grid, and the transition back to the shallow-water flux when the crest is drowned; spillway Q = C L H^1.5 coefficients for broad-crested and ogee crests and their submergence limits; wall faces; and how all of this enters the momentum equation without breaking the still-water (well-balanced) property.

Method: 25 primary sources read directly (manual chapters, two manuals parsed from PDF, one paper on Scipedia, vendor documentation, one abstract record), plus arithmetic of my own. The web-search budget of the session ran out part-way; everything after that point is direct fetches of known URLs, which is why a few sources (Villemonte 1947, Sanders 2002, Morales-Hernandez 2013, the ICM point-source page) could not be opened and are tagged accordingly. Tags: [Certain] read in a primary source; [Likely] strong inference or secondary source; [Guessing] filling a gap.

## Findings

### A. How an inflow hydrograph is injected

1. **HEC-RAS 2D external flow hydrograph = conveyance-weighted face flux, gated by a normal-depth water surface.** The user gives an energy slope; each time step a normal depth is computed from the flow and the terrain cross-section under the boundary line, and the flow is split among the wet boundary cells by conveyance. [Certain] "only the cells in which the water surface elevation is higher than their outer boundary face terrain will receive water." https://www.hec.usace.army.mil/confluence/rasdocs/r2dum/6.4/boundary-and-initial-conditions-for-2d-flow-areas/external-boundary-conditions

2. **In the HEC-RAS 2D solver a flow boundary is a mass source on the right-hand side of continuity, not a momentum flux.** [Certain] "Boundary face flows are included in the internal cells as a source term on the right-hand-side of the system of equations." HEC-RAS 6.0 Hydraulic Reference Manual, Chapter 2 (PDF, p. 2-58), https://www.hec.usace.army.mil/software/hec-ras/documentation/HEC-RAS_6.0_Reference_Manual.pdf . Same page: "The flow boundary condition is specified at each computational face based on the local conveyance."

3. **HEC-RAS 2D internal boundary line (the way HMS hydrographs are put inside a 2D area) splits the flow by line length per cell, and HEC warns against spreading a subbasin outflow along the stream.** [Certain] "flow is distributed across the cells based on the percentage of the line length that crosses that cell." and HEC-HMS outflows "represent discharges AT THE OUTLET" and "SHOULD NOT be distributed over a significant longitudinal length." (double routing under-estimates peak flow and stage). https://www.hec.usace.army.mil/confluence/rasdocs/r2dum/latest/boundary-and-initial-conditions-for-2d-flow-areas/internal-boundary-conditions . This is direct support for Wadi2D decision 5 (inject at the delineated outlet, not along a line).

4. **TUFLOW 2d_sa (source area) inflow: lowest cell first, then the wet cells in proportion to depth; no momentum.** [Certain] "Distributed firstly to the lowest cell and then distributing between wet cells" (Section 8.4.2.1, Read GIS SA, "This is the default option"); "The default is ON, which proportions the flow according to the depth of water." (SA Proportion to Depth == {ON} | OFF); SA Minimum Depth == {-99999} | <depth_in_m> with the manual's guidance "using a SA Minimum Depth of around 0.1m will ensure that this does not occur" (flow sprayed into thin films). Variants: SA ALL (all active cells, wet or dry), SA STREAM (streamline cells plus wet non-streamline cells), SA PITS (spread equally over the cells connected to 1D pits). SA is described as "applying flow directly onto the cells within the polygon as a source"; momentum is never mentioned for SA. https://docs.tuflow.com/classic-hpc/manual/2026.3/07-Boundaries-and-Initial-Conditions.html and https://docs.tuflow.com/classic-hpc/manual/2026.3/80-AppendicesA-TCFCommands.html

5. **TUFLOW QT (flow-versus-time line) distributes by local conveyance under one water level along the line and needs a storage multiplier to be stable; SA is recommended over QT for rapidly varying inflows (dam break).** [Certain] QT "Distributes flow in quantity and direction across the cell(s) based on their topography, bed roughness"; attribute a: "The default multiplier is 5. This can help stabilise the boundary if it is unstable." (too large over-attenuates the hydrograph); HPC Boundary Approach == {Method C} applies an energy correction to QT, HT and HQ lines. Same source as 4.

6. **TUFLOW found mass-only injection insufficient at 1D/2D links and added a momentum term (2023-03).** [Certain] SX boundaries "only added/subtracted mass flux" before 2023-03; HPC SX Momentum Approach == Method A | {Method B}: Method B adds "a simplistic momentum source/sink term". For QT/HT/HX lines on quadtree, Method B ensures "the incoming flow brings with it the correct amount of transverse momentum". Same sources as 4. Lesson for Wadi2D: an injected hydrograph in a steep sub-grid channel probably needs a momentum source, not only volume (see Recommendation 1 and Open issue 1).

7. **Iber distributes an inlet discharge along the contour as q_n = C h^(5/3) (a Manning-type conveyance weighting), normal to the contour; supercritical inflow also needs the depth imposed.** [Certain] "En los contornos de entrada se fija el caudal de agua y se asume que la dirección del flujo es perpendicular al contorno." and "En caso de que el flujo entre en régimen supercrítico, se impone adicionalmente el calado." Bladé et al. 2014, https://www.scipedia.com/public/Blade_et_al_2012a

8. **ICM gives a subcatchment only one way into the 2D mesh, a single point source, and masks direct rainfall under subcatchments to avoid double counting.** [Certain] Subcatchment drains to "A single 2D point source. This allows the hydrology aspects of a subcatchment to be applied directly to a 2D mesh."; 2D zone rainfall option outside subcatchments: "Rainfall and evaporation will only be applied to those mesh elements whose centroids are not inside a subcatchment boundary." https://help2.innovyze.com/infoworksicm/Content/HTML/ICM_ILCM/Subcatchments.htm and https://help2.innovyze.com/infoworksicm/Content/HTML/ICM_IL/2D_Zone_Data_Fields.htm . Precedent for Wadi2D's rain-mask rule under lumped subbasins (decision 5).

9. **3Di/Rana: a 2D lateral is a volume added to the cell; a "Total discharge" boundary is split over the boundary cells by a conveyance approach; a plain "Discharge" value is applied per intersected flowline.** [Certain] "the volume as specified in the supplied time series is added to the volume of the node or cell"; "distribution of the discharge over the full edge is based on a so-called conveyance approach" (wet area, roughness, vegetation); "Discharge values are applied to all intersected flowlines". https://docs.ranawaterintelligence.com/h_laterals.html , https://docs.ranawaterintelligence.com/h_boundary_conditions.html , https://docs.ranawaterintelligence.com/d_2d_objects.html

10. **LISFLOOD-FP point sources are one per cell and given per unit width, multiplied by the cell width: a pure volume source.** [Certain] "There must not be more than one point source per cell."; for a point source "the mass flux per unit width is multiplied by the cell width". LISFLOOD-FP User Manual 5.9.6, Section 3.2.4 Table 12, https://www.bristol.ac.uk/media-library/sites/geography/migrated/documents/lisflood-manual-v5.9.6.pdf

11. **SERGHEI-SWE (Kokkos, the performance-portable precedent) treats rainfall and infiltration as cell mass sources and in its test cases uses closed boundaries with a free outflow.** [Certain] "All boundaries are closed, except for the free outflow at the outlet."; rainfall and infiltration are mass source terms added as (r_o - r_f) dt after the flux step. https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/16/977/2023/gmd-16-977-2023.html

### B. Outflow boundaries and their problems

12. **HEC-RAS 2D normal depth is outflow-only, computed per cell from Manning with the user slope on the terrain under the boundary line; the rating curve is also per cell and outflow-only; a stage hydrograph works per cell and can take water in and out at the same time.** [Certain] "The Normal Depth boundary condition can only be used to take flow out of a 2D flow area."; "Just like the Stage Hydrograph boundary, the Normal Depth boundary is computed on a per cell basis."; solver form Q = K S_f^(1/2) at each boundary face. Sources as in 1 and 2. The 1D chapter of the same reference manual states the price: "The use of normal depth will introduce an error in the water surface profile at the boundary."

13. **TUFLOW HQ (stage-discharge) boundaries: build the curve automatically from a water-surface slope, span one flow path only, expect hysteresis error, and (HPC) filter the discharge in time because low-slope sections oscillate.** [Certain] "the boundary should only span one primary flow path"; "A single height-flow (HQ) curve is an approximation." (sensitivity-test and move the boundary further away); HPC HQ Boundary Filter Constant == {5} (was 1 = no filtering before 2025.0.0), with a warning when the flow falls below 80 percent of the previous step; HPC HQ Boundary Approach == CELL | {TOTAL} (each line one boundary). Water-level boundaries: "Note that both outflow and inflow may occur with water level boundaries." Source as in 4.

14. **ICM's 2D-zone edge conditions are the cleanest menu of outflow types: vertical wall; critical (broad-crested weir without loss, outflow only); supercritical (Fr > 1 passes the element's depth and velocity, outflow only); dry (bottomless pit); normal (slope balances friction, depth and velocity constant).** [Certain] "flow out of the 2D Zone will be calculated using a broad crested weir equation without energy loss."; "It is assumed that slope balances friction forces (normal flow)."; "The boundary of the 2D Zone is considered to be surrounded by a bottomless pit." https://help2.innovyze.com/infoworksicm/Content/HTML/ICM_IL/2D_Zone_Data_Fields.htm

15. **3Di/Rana offers a Sommerfeld (water-level-gradient) boundary for near-equilibrium flow and warns that boundary placement and grid-resolution transitions at the boundary matter.** [Certain] "This type prescribes the water level gradient at the boundary." (for systems "in (near) equilibrium state"); "Locations, where the flow and the bathymetry is simple and predictable, generally give better results." https://docs.ranawaterintelligence.com/h_boundary_conditions.html

16. **Iber: subcritical outlet imposes the water level, supercritical outlet imposes nothing.** [Certain] "En los contornos de salida se impone el nivel de la lámina de agua en caso de que se produzca un régimen subcrítico." and "no es necesario imponer ninguna condición en el caso de que el régimen sea supercrítico". Source as in 7.

17. **Sanders (2002) non-reflecting flux: the open-boundary flux is built from the Riemann problem at the boundary face; good for incidence angles below about 45 degrees, minor reflections beyond.** [Likely: abstract via the search engine and the UCI publication list; full text not accessible] Sanders, B.F., Advances in Water Resources 25(2):195-202, DOI 10.1016/S0309-1708(01)00055-0; listed at https://floodlab.eng.uci.edu/publications/

### C. Weirs, crests and spillways on internal faces

18. **TUFLOW HPC (2023-03 and later) applies the full weir equation on cell faces along thin and thick breaklines whenever the flow there becomes upstream controlled; the flow width is the face length; the default coefficients are a broad-crested weir with a Villemonte-form submergence factor.** [Certain] "Where flow in the 2D domain becomes upstream controlled, TUFLOW HPC triggers a weir flow approximation."; "For 2D weir approach, this is the length of the weir face (m or ft)."; "The default coefficients (Cd, Ex, a and b) are based on the parameters used for a Broad-Crested Weir." (0.577, 1.5, 8.55, 0.556); HPC Weir Approach == Method A | Method B | {Method B Energy}: Method A "applies the water level gradient limiter only" (the pre-2023 approach: "The original weir flow approximation in TUFLOW HPC was to adjust the water level gradient"), Method B uses the upstream water level above the invert as Hu, Method B Energy (default) the upstream energy level. https://docs.tuflow.com/classic-hpc/release/2023-03-AF/TwoDSolv-1.html and https://docs.tuflow.com/classic-hpc/manual/2026.3/06-2D-Domains.html (Section 7.3.4). Note: the TCF appendix prints the default b as 0.566 while Table 5.16 and the release notes print 0.556; a documentation inconsistency, recorded in Open issue 8.

19. **TUFLOW Classic trigger and fallback: the weir equation replaces the sub-critical calculation only where the flow is upstream controlled (adverse bed slope test with ZC, comparison of the two regime flows preferring the lower, Froude check); when downstream controlled, the full 2D equations are used.** [Certain] "Weir flow only occurs if the bed slope is adverse (different direction) to the water surface slope."; "When the flow is downstream controlled, regardless of the ground surface slope, the full 2D equations are applied"; "The broad-crested weir equation is divided by the weir factor." (Global Weir Factor == {1.0}; "The weir factor is not the broad-crested weir coefficient."; the appendix notes 1.0 to 1.1 is needed to reproduce upstream-controlled weir flow); Free Overfall == {ON} (Syme 1991), Froude Check == {1}. https://docs.tuflow.com/classic-hpc/manual/2026.3/06-2D-Domains.html (Section 7.4.2) and the TCF appendix.

20. **TUFLOW's face-topology lesson: a crest must be on every face around a raised cell and aligned with faces, or the sub-grid tables sample lower ground and the crest leaks.** [Certain] "The 2023-03 release ensures all the cell faces surrounding a raised cell are selected by thick breaklines."; "It is strongly recommended to add MAX or RIDGE lines" for hydraulic controls under SGS, because if a crest is not aligned with a cell face "lower elevation values may be sampled in the SGS cell face width vs depth curve"; thin lines "modify cell faces (ZH, ZU and ZV Zpts)" only, and cell sides "can wet and dry when the whole cell is wet", which "allows for the modelling of 'thin' obstructions such as fences and thin embankments." Sections 7.1.3.1.2, 7.2.6.2, same chapter.

21. **TUFLOW's weir library gives crest-type-specific Cd and Villemonte exponents (Table 5.16), and chose the Villemonte form because it has two parameters and is exact at both ends (Csf = 1 at Hd/Hu = 0, Csf = 0 at Hd/Hu = 1).** [Certain] Rows: SP ogee/nappe 0.75, 1.5, 6.992, 0.648; WB broad-crested 0.577, 1.5, 8.55, 0.556 (Abou Seida & Quarashi 1976); WC Crump 0.508, 1.5, 17.87, 0.59; WR/WT sharp thin plate 0.62/0.63, 1.5, 2.205, 0.483 (Hager 1987); WV V-notch exponent 2.5; WW Bradley 1978 roadway 0.542, 1.5, 21.15, 0.627; the legacy W weir used "Csf = 1 - (1 - dH/H)^20" above 70 percent submergence. https://docs.tuflow.com/classic-hpc/manual/2026.3/05-1D-Domains.html (Section 5.7.4, Table 5.16)

22. **HEC-RAS SA/2D connections inside a 2D area: either the 1D weir equation or the "Normal 2D Equation Domain", one method per structure, no automatic switching; the 2D equations must not be used on a high structure, and they over-predict free-fall flow.** [Certain] "The "Normal 2D Equation Domain" option should NOT be used if the height of the structure is high."; "You can currently only choose one method per structure."; "If HEC-RAS cannot balance the momentum equation across the weir (e.g. free-falling flow) the 2D equations will substantially over-predict velocities." (and "push too much flow over the weir, under-predicting upstream stages"); the 2D option "will generally give better results" for a highly submerged structure. The headwater may be the water surface or (since 6.7) the energy line. https://www.hec.usace.army.mil/confluence/rasdocs/r2dum/latest/development-of-a-2d-or-combined-1d-2d-model/hydraulic-structures-inside-of-2d-flow-areas and https://www.hec.usace.army.mil/confluence/rasdocs/hgt/latest/guides/modeling-weirs-in-2d-areas

23. **HEC-RAS 2D-connection coefficients are far below textbook weir values, and ordinary high ground should not get the weir equation at all.** [Certain] Levee or roadway 3 ft or more above ground 1.5 to 2.6 (default 2.0, US units); 1 to 3 ft 1.0 to 2.0 ("becomes submerged easily"); natural high ground barrier 1 to 3 ft 0.5 to 1.0 ("Flow does not pass through critical depth"); non-elevated overbank terrain 0.2 to 0.5; lateral coefficients "should be lower than typical values used for inline weirs"; for overland-flow interfaces 0.2 to 1.0 and "should be calibrated to produce reasonable results whenever possible." 24. **HEC-RAS lateral structure in the 2D domain floors the tailwater at critical depth taken as two-thirds of the 2D hydraulic depth, and when the structure's drawn length differs from its true length the face-to-face lengths are proportioned to the true length (HEC's answer to the staircase crest).** [Certain for the critical-depth floor; Likely for the exact wording of the proportioning, which the page states in a sentence I did not capture verbatim] "When the WSE on the 1D side is lower than the WSE on the 2D side at a given face" the program uses the higher of that critical-depth WSE and the 1D WSE as tailwater. Same source as 23.

25. **HEC-RAS 1D weir library (what reviewers know): Q = C L H^(3/2) with H the upstream energy head; typical C broad-crested 2.6 to 3.1, ogee 3.2 to 4.1, sharp 3.1 to 3.3 (US units); ogee C corrected for He/Ho by the USBR 1977 curve; submergence = downstream depth above crest divided by upstream energy depth above crest; three submergence curves by crest shape; at 98 percent submergence the program abandons the weir equation for the energy equation.** [Certain] "C = Weir flow coefficient, typical values will range from 2.6 to 4.1"; Table 8-1 "Broad Crested 2.6 - 3.1 / Ogee Crested 3.2 – 4.1 / Sharp Crested 3.1 – 3.3"; "If the spillway is sharp crested, then the Villemonte equation (Villemonte, 1947) is used to compute the flow reduction coefficient." HEC-RAS 6.0 Hydraulic Reference Manual, Chapters 5 and 8 (PDF).

28. **3Di/Rana does not use a weir equation on 2D obstacles at all: an obstacle line only raises the exchange level of the crossed flowlines, the momentum equation does the rest, and the face cross-section is built from the maximum of the pixel pairs on either side of the edge.** [Certain] "Only when these are located at the cell edges they are detected and will block flow."; "the obstacle's crest level is used as exchange level"; "For each pixel pair, the maximum value is used to describe the 2D cross-section."; on steep steps "a limiter for the water level gradient may be used". https://docs.ranawaterintelligence.com/h_surfaceflow.html and https://docs.ranawaterintelligence.com/d_2d_objects.html . ### D. My own arithmetic (all [Certain, arithmetic])

32. **The TUFLOW default Cd = 0.577 is the ideal critical-flow broad-crested coefficient: (2/3) x 0.577 x sqrt(2g) = 1.704 against (2/3)^1.5 sqrt(g) = 1.705 (SI).** No energy loss is assumed by default; HEC's broad-crested range 2.6 to 3.1 US converts to 1.44 to 1.71 SI (multiply US values by 0.3048^0.5 = 0.5521).

33. **The three submergence families disagree by a factor of up to 2 at the ratios that matter on a wadi dam.** At Hd/Hu = 0.85: Villemonte sharp-crested (1.5, 0.385) gives 0.56, TUFLOW broad-crested (8.55, 0.556) gives 0.85, the energy-based drowned formula 2.598 r sqrt(1 - r) gives 0.86, the Bradley roadway curve (21.15, 0.627) gives 0.98. At 0.95: 0.37, 0.56, 0.55, 0.77. At 0.99: 0.20, 0.25, 0.26, 0.36. The TUFLOW broad-crested Villemonte fit and the Iber/3Di energy-based formula are the same curve to within 0.01 over the whole range; the roadway curve is flat to 0.85 and then falls fastest, which is the steepness HEC blames for oscillations (finding 23).

34. **A plain Riemann flux across a raised dry face does not reproduce the weir law.** Per unit width with H the depth above the crest: ideal critical flow 1.705 H^1.5; HLL flux at the first instant for still water against a dry step 2.088 H^1.5 (122 percent of critical); the Ritter dam-break steady value at the dam site 0.928 H^1.5 (54 percent). This is why TUFLOW first limited the water-level gradient and then imposed the weir equation, and why HEC says the 2D equations over-predict free-fall flow (findings 18, 22).

35. **A crest at 45 degrees across a square grid has a staircase face length sqrt(2) = 1.414 times the true crest length.** Any face-length weir law (TUFLOW: "the length of the weir face") over-estimates the discharge by that factor unless the length is apportioned from the true polyline (HEC's proportioning, finding 24).

## Equations and algorithms

**E1. Standard weir equation (HEC-RAS eq. 8-6/8-7, 1D library and SA/2D connections).**
Q = C L H^(3/2); Q discharge (cfs or m3/s), C weir coefficient (2.6 to 4.1 in US units by crest shape; multiply by 0.5521 for SI), L crest length, H upstream energy head above the crest (water surface optional since 6.7). Submergence S = (tailwater depth above crest) / (headwater energy depth above crest); the coefficient is reduced by a curve chosen by crest shape (broad-crested: Bradley 1978 roadway curve; ogee: USACE EM 1110-2-1603 Plate 3-5; sharp: Villemonte); the exponent of that reduction can be raised to 3 for stability; above S = 0.98 (default) the weir equation is abandoned for the energy equation. Ogee: C = C0 x f(He/Ho) from USBR 1977 (HEC Figure 8-10), He the energy head, Ho the design head.

**E2. TUFLOW advanced weir equation (eq. 5.11; applied in HPC on breakline faces).**
Q = (2/3) Cf Cd Csf W sqrt(2g) Hu^Ex, with Csf = (1 - (Hd/Hu)^a)^b.
Q flow over the weir face; Cf calibration factor (default 1.0); Cd weir coefficient; Csf submergence factor (Villemonte form); W flow width = length of the weir face in 2D; Hu upstream water-surface (Method B) or energy (Method B Energy, default) depth above the weir invert; Hd downstream water-surface depth above the invert; Ex exponent (1.5; 2.5 for V-notch); a, b crest-type exponents (Table 5.16: broad-crested 8.55, 0.556; ogee 6.992, 0.648; sharp 2.205, 0.483; roadway 21.15, 0.627; Crump 17.87, 0.59). Csf = 1 at Hd/Hu = 0 and 0 at Hd/Hu = 1. Trigger: the face is "upstream controlled"; otherwise the shallow-water solution applies (Classic rule: adverse bed slope relative to the water-surface slope, compare the two regime flows and take the lower, Froude >= Froude Check). Classic divides the broad-crested formula by a Weir Factor (global x spatial, default 1.0).

**E3. Villemonte (1947) as used for sharp crests.**
Q_sub = Q_free [1 - (h2/h1)^n]^0.385; h1, h2 upstream and downstream heads above the crest; n = 1.5 rectangular, 2 parabolic, 2.5 triangular (n is the exponent of the free-flow law). The 0.385 exponent is fixed.

**E4. Energy-based broad-crested weir (3Di/Rana 1D weirs; Iber internal weir).**
Energy balance h_I + u_I^2/2g = h_II + a + u_II^2/2g (a crest height above the upstream bed).
Free (critical at the crest): h_c = (2/3)(h_I - a); Q_cr = C W sqrt(g) [(2/3)(h_I - a)]^(3/2) (with C = 1 this is 1.705 W H^1.5 in SI).
Drowned: Q_sub = C W sqrt(2g (h_I - h_II)) h_II (Rana), i.e. Iber's Q = 2.598 Cdw B (Zd - Zw) (Zu - Zd)^0.5 with Cdw dimensional (1.705 ideal); switch at (Zd - Zw)/(Zu - Zw) = 0.67 where both give the same Q (2.598 x 0.67 x sqrt(0.33) = 1.00). Ratio to free flow: Csf = 2.598 r sqrt(1 - r), r = Hd/Hu, which matches TUFLOW's (8.55, 0.556) fit to 0.01.
Rana breach: supercritical when (h_I - h_III) > (1/3)(h_I - a); u_II = sqrt(2g(h_I - a) / (3(1 - gamma L))) (supercritical) or C1 sqrt(2g(h_I - h_II - a)/(1 - gamma L)) (subcritical), gamma = c_f/R, c_f = 50, L = 30 m.

**E5. Inflow distribution rules (as read).**
HEC-RAS external line: compute normal depth y_n from Q and the terrain section under the line with the user energy slope; give flow only to cells whose y_n water surface is above the cell's outer face terrain, split by conveyance K_i = A_i R_i^(2/3)/n_i of those faces; the flow enters continuity as a source term.
HEC-RAS internal line: Q_cell = Q x (length of line inside the cell)/(total length).
TUFLOW SA: all flow to the lowest cell while all cells are dry; then Q_cell = Q x d_cell / sum(d_j) over wet cells with d >= SA Minimum Depth (0.1 m recommended); SA ALL spreads equally over all active cells.
TUFLOW QT: one water level along the line; flow split by cell conveyance (topography and roughness); storage multiplier 5 for stability; energy correction (Method C).
Iber: q_n(s) = C h(s)^(5/3) along the contour, C chosen so the integral equals Q; direction normal to the contour; depth also imposed if supercritical.
3Di total discharge: split by conveyance (wet area, roughness, vegetation); 2D lateral: volume added to the cell.
LISFLOOD-FP: Q_cell = q (m2/s) x cell width; one point source per cell.

**E6. Outflow rules (as read).**
HEC-RAS normal depth: Q_face = K_face S_f^(1/2) per boundary face, outflow only; rating curve per cell, outflow only; stage per cell, both directions.
TUFLOW HQ: Q(H) built from the water-surface slope attribute b (Manning, depth-varying n honoured), per line (TOTAL) or per cell, discharge filtered in time with constant 5.
ICM critical edge: Q = broad-crested weir without loss on the element's face level; ICM normal edge: depth and velocity held constant across the boundary; ICM supercritical edge: element depth and velocity passed out when Fr > 1.
Riemann-based transmissive (Sanders-type, [Likely]): ghost state equal to the interior state (or the interior Riemann invariant), flux from the Riemann solver, non-reflecting for incidence below about 45 degrees.

**E7. Crest face in the momentum balance without breaking well-balancing (construction proposed for Wadi2D; [Likely] on the hydrostatic-reconstruction part, which is the standard Audusse-type treatment the research note on the numerical scheme covers; [Guessing] on the blended structure flux, to be verified on the tests listed in Open issues).**
Face bed level z_f = max(z_L, z_R, z_crest). Reconstructed depths h_L* = max(0, eta_L - z_f), h_R* = max(0, eta_R - z_f), eta the water level. Riemann flux F_SWE from (h_L*, u_L) and (h_R*, u_R); pressure correction on each side with (g/2)(h_i^2 - h_i*^2). Still water: h_L* = h_R*, F_SWE has zero mass flux and the pressure terms cancel; a wall (z_crest very high) gives h* = 0 on both sides and zero flux; the dry side of an overtopped crest sees h_R* = 0 and gets flow only when eta_L > z_f. This alone is what 3Di does. For a flagged structure face (dam crest, spillway, levee, road) add the weir law: Q_w from E2 or E4 with the true apportioned crest length (E8), applied as the face mass flux when the face is upstream controlled (r = Hd/Hu < 0.67 broad-crested, or the TUFLOW regime test), with the momentum flux of that mass taken at the crest velocity u_c = Q_w/(W h_c) and the pressure term kept as the reconstructed hydrostatic one; between r = 0.67 and about 0.9 use the continuous Csf of E4/E2; above that return to F_SWE. Choosing the lower of (Q_w, mass flux of F_SWE) in the upstream-controlled regime guards against the 22 percent over-shoot of finding 34; the still-water state is preserved because Csf = 0 at r = 1 and both branches are zero.

**E8. Crest length apportioning.** For a crest polyline of true length L_true crossing faces f with staircase lengths l_f: L_f = L_true x l_f / sum(l_j) over the faces it crosses (equivalent to HEC's proportioning to the structure length). Never use sum(l_f) itself (sqrt(2) error at 45 degrees).

**E9. Spillway.** Q = C L H^1.5 with H the energy head (reservoir approach velocity small, so the water surface is adequate); C = C0 x f(He/Ho) for an ogee (C0 = 2.18 SI = 3.95 US at design head for a high crest, USBR, [Likely]; his dams use about 2.1 SI); submergence by the ogee curve (TUFLOW SP: a = 6.992, b = 0.648), which starts reducing earlier than the broad-crested one (0.78 at r = 0.85 against 0.85).

## Recommendation for Wadi2D

1. **Inject lumped hydrographs as a volume source into the sub-grid channel cells at the delineated outlet, TUFLOW-SA style, never along a line.** HEC says a HMS outflow is the discharge at the outlet and must not be spread along the stream (double routing); the natural data model is a point plus a short transverse polygon over the mountain-front channel. Rule: all flow to the lowest cell while dry, then proportional to depth over wet cells above a minimum depth (0.1 m), added to continuity. Carry a momentum source as an option: Q x (Q/A_wet) along the stream-segment direction, capped at the local critical velocity, because TUFLOW had to add a momentum term to its mass-only 1D/2D links and the wadi heads are supercritical. Decide by test (a) of decision 6: mass-only versus mass-plus-momentum against the HMS hydrograph and timing at the next design point downstream. Reject: edge QT lines with a single water level, and conveyance-weighted boundary lines, for anything inside the domain; they belong only to the model perimeter.

2. **Mask direct rain under lumped subbasin polygons (ICM's "outside subcatchments" rule) exactly as decision 5 states; nothing in the sources contradicts it.**

3. **Outflow boundaries: three types, all per face.** (i) Sea: stage hydrograph (tidal or fixed) that allows inflow and outflow per face (HEC, TUFLOW). (ii) Landward perimeter on a slope: normal depth per face, Q_f = K_f S^0.5 with the user slope defaulting to the local terrain slope, outflow only, with a time filter on the discharge (TUFLOW's HQ lesson at low slopes); place it one fan width beyond the area of interest and expose the sensitivity test in the UI. (iii) Supercritical or drop edges: a Riemann transmissive face (ghost state = interior state) by default, with ICM's "critical" edge (broad-crested weir without loss on the face level) as the option for a drop. Reject fixed stage on steep perimeters (reflection) and reject diagonal boundary lines across resolution changes (3Di's constraint).

4. **Crest faces: the sub-grid face carries the crest level and the ordinary scheme handles overtopping; the weir law is only for engineered crests, never for natural high ground.** Default: hydrostatic reconstruction against z_f = max(z_L, z_R, z_crest) (E7), which blocks flow until overtopped, keeps still water still, and makes a wall the trivial case z_crest = +inf. This is 3Di's design and HEC's own coefficient table says natural high ground "does not pass through critical depth" (C 0.28-0.55 SI, i.e. not a weir). Enforce TUFLOW's two topology rules in the mesher: every face around a raised cell carries the crest, and crest polylines snap to faces so the face width-depth table never samples ground lower than the crest.

5. **Structure faces (dam crest, spillway, levee, road): E2/E4 weir law with TUFLOW's broad-crested defaults (Cd 0.577, a 8.55, b 0.556) and the ogee set for spillways, headwater as energy head, crest length apportioned from the true polyline (E8), applied only while the face is upstream controlled (r < 0.67) and returning continuously to the shallow-water flux as it drowns.** Take the lower of the weir flow and the Riemann mass flux in the free regime (finding 34), keep the hydrostatic pressure terms, and give the transferred mass the crest velocity so momentum is neither lost nor invented. Reject: Villemonte's sharp-crested exponents (1.5, 0.385) for broad crests (halves the flow at 85 percent submergence where the broad-crested data say 15 percent); the Bradley roadway curve as default (flat then vertical, the oscillation HEC patches with an exponent of 3); LISFLOOD's "weir controls the whole face, no floodplain component" (admitted instabilities); HEC's one-method-per-structure switch; depth-based weir heads where the approach velocity is significant.

6. **Spillway as a structure face, Q = C L H^1.5 in SI with C = 2.1 for his dams (within HEC's ogee range 1.77-2.26 SI), USBR He/Ho correction when the design head is known, ogee submergence curve, free up to r about 0.67, drowned above.** The reservoir behind the crest is the DEM (decision 10), so routing needs nothing more.

7. **Verification before any of this is believed:** still water over a crest (zero flux, machine precision), lab broad-crested weir (free and drowned, against Q = 1.705 Cd W H^1.5 and the energy-based drowned curve), dam-break onto a dry step (the 1.22 over-shoot must not appear when the weir branch is on), a 45-degree crest (discharge independent of orientation), and the Block 2 dam spillways against the W2 hand calculation.

## Open issues

1. Momentum of the injected hydrograph in supercritical sub-grid channels: no source states a rule for a point injection; TUFLOW's SX experience says mass-only is not enough at 1D/2D links. Needs the test in Recommendation 1.
2. The exact TUFLOW HPC criterion for "upstream controlled" at a breakline face was not stated in the pages read (Classic's rules are explicit; HPC's are not).
3. The staircase crest length: TUFLOW's handling was not found in the pages read; HEC proportions to the true length (paraphrase, not verbatim). Wadi2D's E8 rule is my construction.
4. Sanders (2002) full text not accessible; the Riemann-ghost-state transmissive face is standard practice but its reflection at oblique incidence must be measured on the 45-degree test.
5. Morales-Hernandez et al. (2013) and Echeverribar et al. (2019), the Zaragoza group's internal-boundary formulation (how the imposed discharge becomes the numerical flux on both sides in a Roe scheme, with well-balancing), are paywalled and repository copies are bot-blocked; E7 is therefore my construction, tagged [Guessing] until the tests pass.
6. Ogee coefficient versus He/Ho: the USBR 1977 curve (HEC Figure 8-10) was not transcribed; C0 = 3.95 US is from memory [Likely].
7. Iber's submerged-weir formula: the root term was garbled in the extraction and reconstructed by continuity; confirm in the Iber reference manual.
8. TUFLOW documentation prints the default b as 0.556 (Table 5.16, release notes) and 0.566 (TCF appendix); irrelevant to Wadi2D but a reminder to keep such constants in one place with a test.
9. ICM's 2D point-source and 2D boundary-line pages were unreachable (help site errors); how ICM distributes a point source inside the element is unknown.
10. Villemonte (1947) itself was not read; the formula is from INRAE's documentation and HEC's citation.
11. Whether to expose a "drowned-flow blend" parameter (the r range over which the weir flux is faded into the shallow-water flux) or hard-code 0.67-0.9; decide after the lab-weir test.

## 1. TUFLOW HPC: the face depth with and without SGS, the friction depth options, the 2nd-order hybrid, quadtree, and slopes

### Takeaway
Without SGS, TUFLOW HPC carries the upwind cell's **depth** to the face, not its water **level**: on a face that steps down in the flow direction the face depth is the upwind cell's own depth, and it is cut only where the bed steps up, so the S·dx/2 over-reach described in the brief cannot arise there [Likely, see Inferences]. The current manual's 2nd-order face depth is the **average of the two cells' depths**, blended back to the upwind depth "when the solution shows short scale reversal or upstream controlled supercritical flow"; that is TUFLOW's answer to exactly the uniform-flow versus supercritical dilemma in the brief. How the SGS face curves are read (at which level) is not published, beyond a 2nd-order interpolation of the water **level** to the face, which an option admits can fall back to 1st order on steep boundary cells and then give a water-level error.

### Cited Findings

**Face depth without SGS (the 2017 scheme paper)**
- [Certain] Continuity flux through each face is the face-normal velocity times the face depth times the face width; the face depth is upwinded: "The water depth at a face was taken to be the water depth of the cell that the water was flowing from (known in computational fluid dynamics as 'upwinding')." Selection rule: if (u_cc + u_n1) > 0 then h_n1, else h_cc. — [Collecutt and Syme 2017, IAHR World Congress, s.2](https://www.tuflow.com/media/5102/2017-experimental-benchmarking-of-mesh-size-and-time-step-convergence-for-a-1st-and-2nd-order-swe-finite-volume-scheme-collecutt-and-syme-iahr.pdf)
- [Certain] The bed-step reduction acts only when the bed rises in the flow direction: "In the case that the bed elevation of the downwind cell was higher than the bed elevation of the upwind cell, the water depth at the face was reduced by the difference in bed elevations, and limited to be non-negative." — [Collecutt and Syme 2017, s.2](https://www.tuflow.com/media/5102/2017-experimental-benchmarking-of-mesh-size-and-time-step-convergence-for-a-1st-and-2nd-order-swe-finite-volume-scheme-collecutt-and-syme-iahr.pdf)
- [Certain] In that paper the 2nd-order option acts on the **velocity** interpolated to the face, not on the depth: a pure average u = (u_n1 + u_cc)/2 "is not total variation diminishing (TVD) and is known to be unstable", so a hybrid is used: u_i = (1 − ξ) u_n1 + ξ u_cc, ξ = 2x(1 − x), x = (u_n1 − u_11)/(u_cc − u_11); "In the limit that the solution shows no curvature, pure second order interpolation was used, and in the limit that the solution shows reversal ... then the first order scheme (up-winding) is adopted." — [Collecutt and Syme 2017, eq. 7](https://www.tuflow.com/media/5102/2017-experimental-benchmarking-of-mesh-size-and-time-step-convergence-for-a-1st-and-2nd-order-swe-finite-volume-scheme-collecutt-and-syme-iahr.pdf)
- [Certain] The paper warns that the surface-gradient forcing term "requires care in its discretised implementation as it can become a net source of energy within the model if incorrectly posed"; it does not print that discretisation. — [Collecutt and Syme 2017, s.2](https://www.tuflow.com/media/5102/2017-experimental-benchmarking-of-mesh-size-and-time-step-convergence-for-a-1st-and-2nd-order-swe-finite-volume-scheme-collecutt-and-syme-iahr.pdf)
- [Certain] Results of the paper: mesh-size convergence in both 1st and 2nd order with a constant viscosity, "Convergence typically required 8 cells or more across feature widths", 3 cells acceptable for bend head loss; EA Test 6A reproduced "including supercritical flow and hydraulic shocks/jumps", time-step convergence at 0.8 of the limiting targets; without viscosity "the 1st order solution demonstrated a dampening of eddy formation most likely due to numerical diffusion". No slope or steep-channel case was run. — [Collecutt and Syme 2017, s.6](https://www.tuflow.com/media/5102/2017-experimental-benchmarking-of-mesh-size-and-time-step-convergence-for-a-1st-and-2nd-order-swe-finite-volume-scheme-collecutt-and-syme-iahr.pdf)

**Face depth in the current manual (2026.0, s.7.1.1 "TUFLOW HPC 2D Solver")**
- [Certain] 1st order: "this uses depth of the upstream cell (often referred to as upwinding)", "bounded to be greater than or equal to 0, and less than or equal to the surface elevation of the upstream cell" [the fetch tool paraphrased the end of the sentence as: minus the bed elevation at the cell-side midpoint, i.e. the face's ZU/ZV point]. — [TUFLOW Classic/HPC Manual 2026.0, s.7.1.1](https://docs.tuflow.com/classic-hpc/manual/2026.0/Solvers-2.html)
- [Certain] 2nd order: "the depth at the face is computed as the average of the two cell averaged depths"; the plain average is "not total variation diminishing (TVD) and is known to be unstable". — [Manual 2026.0, s.7.1.1](https://docs.tuflow.com/classic-hpc/manual/2026.0/Solvers-2.html)
- [Certain] The hybrid moves the face depth "from interpolated depth to the upstream depth (1st order upwinding)" "when the solution shows short scale reversal or upstream controlled supercritical flow". — [Manual 2026.0, s.7.1.1](https://docs.tuflow.com/classic-hpc/manual/2026.0/Solvers-2.html)
- [Certain] 2nd order is the HPC default and recommended; the 2nd-order solution "demonstrates no discernible numerical diffusion", the 1st-order one "can experience numerical diffusion, like all 1st order schemes" and "does not resolve strongly two-dimensional hydraulics"; velocities sit at cell mid-sides (adopted to remove a checkerboard mode). — [Manual 2026.0, s.7.1.1](https://docs.tuflow.com/classic-hpc/manual/2026.0/Solvers-2.html)
- [Certain] The momentum equations in s.7.1 carry the friction as (g n²/h^{4/3} + k/(2Δx)) h √(u²+v²) u; the section does not say which h. — [Manual 2026.0, s.7.1.1](https://docs.tuflow.com/classic-hpc/manual/2026.0/Solvers-2.html)
- [Certain] The upstream-controlled test: "The ZC value also determines the bed slope when testing for the upstream controlled flow regime" (stated for Classic); the HPC version of the test is in s.7.3.4, which was not read. — [Manual 2026.0, s.7.1.3.1.1](https://docs.tuflow.com/classic-hpc/manual/2026.0/Solvers-2.html)
- [Certain] Time integration: "HPC Temporal Scheme == 1 | 2 | {4}", 4th order the default; "We recommend the use of the 4th order temporal scheme as it is unconditionally stable" (with adaptive time stepping). "Solution Scheme == HPC 1st" selects the 1st-order spatial solver; the manual recommends 2nd order for accuracy. — [Manual 2026.3, Appendix A TCF commands](https://docs.tuflow.com/classic-hpc/manual/2026.3/80-AppendicesA-TCFCommands.html)

**Friction depth: "HPC Mannings Depth Approach"**
- [Certain] Syntax "HPC Mannings Depth Approach == Method A | {Method B}", Method B the default. It sets the depth used in the bed-resistance term in u and v; Method B "upwinds the depth of flow at a face", said to improve stability; Method A is "the previous approach", whose depth the entry does not state; "if using the TUFLOW HPC SGS functionality, this command has no effect." There is no Method C. — [Manual 2026.3, Appendix A](https://docs.tuflow.com/classic-hpc/manual/2026.3/80-AppendicesA-TCFCommands.html)
- [Certain, per N02] The 2020-01 release notes say SGS models "use a different formulation again" for the friction depth; with SGS the resistance radius R = A/W of the face curves is the default, a hydraulic radius A/P option exists. — [TUFLOW Release Notes 2020-01-AB, s.3.2.3 and 5.3](https://downloads.tuflow.com/_archive/TUFLOW/Releases/2020-01/Doc/TUFLOW%20Release%20Notes.2020-01-AB.pdf)

**With SGS: how the face curves are read**
- [Certain] With SGS the face flow area becomes "a curve of flow area versus depth" and the cell's storage "a curve of volume versus depth"; faces convey through the momentum equation, or a weir equation where upstream control applies. — [Manual 2026.0, s.7.1.3.1.1 to 7.1.3.1.2](https://docs.tuflow.com/classic-hpc/manual/2026.0/Solvers-2.html)
- [Certain] "SGS 2nd Order BC Slope == {Method A} | Method B": "This command specifies the method to conduct 2nd order spatial interpolation of the water level at HPC model boundaries." Method B "fixes an issue in HPC SGS models where the solver can inadvertently apply" 1st-order interpolation at boundary cells with steep bed slopes (the entry's example threshold is "> 0.01m") while using 2nd order elsewhere, which "can cause a slight water level error near the boundary". Method A remains the default. Release not stated. — [Manual 2026.3, Appendix A](https://docs.tuflow.com/classic-hpc/manual/2026.3/80-AppendicesA-TCFCommands.html)
- [Certain] "SGS Sheet Flow Approach == {Method A} | Method B": Method A "assumes water fills up from the bottom of the cell" and uses the depth above the cell's minimum elevation in the bed friction; Method B detects SGS cells in sheet flow and uses the cell-averaged depth (volume/area) to adjust the friction depth there. Default A; release not stated. — [Manual 2026.3, Appendix A](https://docs.tuflow.com/classic-hpc/manual/2026.3/80-AppendicesA-TCFCommands.html)
- [Certain] Other SGS defaults in 2026.3: SGS Approach Method C (all samples kept in memory, curves built at the end; Method B legacy); SGS Depth Output CELL AVERAGE ("dividing the cell volume by cell area"); SGS Z Shape Line Approach Method B (a gradient along the face through the corner and mid-side Zpts; Method A flat faces); HPC Weir Approach Method B Energy (weir equation on the upstream energy level above the invert); HPC Dry Face Inertia Approach Method B (cures runaway velocities on dry faces). — [Manual 2026.3, Appendix A](https://docs.tuflow.com/classic-hpc/manual/2026.3/80-AppendicesA-TCFCommands.html)
- [Certain, per N02] TUFLOW "still stores only one velocity value per face and one depth value per cell"; the curves do not store where along a face the high and low points lie. — [Quadtree and SGS FAQ](https://wiki.tuflow.com/Quadtree_and_Sub-Grid_Sampling_FAQ); [Manual 2023-03, s.7.4.3.1.2](https://downloads.tuflow.com/TUFLOW/Manuals/2023-03/TUFLOW.Manual.2023-03.pdf)

**Slopes, cell size and quadtree**
- [Certain, per N02] Cells across a flow path, manual Table 3.1: Classic 4-6, HPC without SGS 6-8, HPC with SGS 3-4 "(assumes terrain data is finer by a factor of 2 or more)". — [Manual 2025.2, s.3.2.2](https://docs.tuflow.com/classic-hpc/manual/2025.2/ModelResolutionDiscretisation-2.html)
- [Certain, per N02] Brisbane River 2011 (nine flood marks, ±0.2 m criterion): 2nd-order SGS within the criterion at 20 to 60 m cells (worst −0.22 m at 60 m); 2nd-order without SGS only at 30 m or finer (+0.57 m at 60 m); 1st-order without SGS fails at every size (+0.24 to +0.77 m at 20 m, up to +2.28 m at 60 m), i.e. too high, not too low. — [Huxley, Syme, Ryan and Collecutt 2022, HWRS, Table 1](https://www.tuflow.com/media/7555/2022-hydraulic-modelling-2d-cell-size-result-convergence-huxley-et-al-hwrs.pdf)
- [Certain, per N02] The documented steep-slope SGS issue is an output one: thin sheet flow on a steep SGS cell gives a water level below the ground at the cell centre (fixed in depth output by CELL AVERAGE; the level output limitation is acknowledged in 2025.1.1), and "In steep areas, or where there are sudden changes in topography the interpolation of water levels will still be subject to the coarseness of the 2D cell sizes." — [Release Notes 2020-01-AB s.7.6](https://downloads.tuflow.com/_archive/TUFLOW/Releases/2020-01/Doc/TUFLOW%20Release%20Notes.2020-01-AB.pdf); [TUFLOW Output Discussion](https://wiki.tuflow.com/TUFLOW_Output_Discussion)
- [Certain, per N02] Quadtree: a single-level quadtree runs about 20 % slower than the plain grid; keep one cell size across a channel's bed and active width; nothing on cell size against bed slope. — [Quadtree and SGS FAQ](https://wiki.tuflow.com/Quadtree_and_Sub-Grid_Sampling_FAQ)
- [Certain] TUFLOW's own EA-benchmark submission of 2012: the 2012-05-AA Classic release improved Test 5 (dam break down a valley sloping about 0.01 in its upper part, easing to about 0.001) "due to improved representation of steep flows and transitioning between flow regimes"; TUFLOW GPU (the HPC precursor: finite volume, "1st order spatial, 4th order time") gave "Even better reproduction of the hydraulic jump" in Test 6A "using a 0.05m grid" instead of the specified 0.1 m. — [TUFLOW submission to the EA 2D benchmark, 22 June 2012](https://tuflow.com/media/5004/2012-uk-ea-benchmarking-of-2d-hydraulic-modelling-packages-tuflow-submission.pdf)

### Inferences
- [Likely] The two halves of the non-SGS rule combine to: face depth = min(h_up, η_up − z_face) floored at 0, where h_up is the upwind cell's centre depth. On a downhill face η_up − z_face = h_up + S·dx/2 > h_up, so the face reads h_up, the upwind cell's own depth. On uniform flow down a plane this is the normal depth, exact. The W47 scheme's reading of the face table at η_up over the face invert is the case TUFLOW does not use: TUFLOW's level-based term enters only as the upper bound for an upward step. This is the documented precedent for "carry the depth, not the level, to the face" while keeping the scheme upwind.
- [Likely] The 2017 paper and the 2026 manual describe different 2nd-order mechanics: the paper upwinds the depth always and hybridises the velocity; the manual hybridises the depth (average of the two depths, upwind under reversal or upstream-controlled supercritical flow). Either the scheme changed after 2017 or one text is loose; the sources do not say which. Both versions keep an upwind fallback for supercritical and transitional flow, which is what the brief says the plain mean-level read breaks.
- [Likely] With the face bed at the mean of the two cell beds, "average of the two depths" equals "mean of the two levels minus the face bed", so TUFLOW's 2nd-order face depth is the mean-level read the brief tried, made safe by the upstream-controlled and reversal switches.
- [Likely] The SGS 2nd Order BC Slope entry shows that under SGS HPC interpolates the water **level** to the face at 2nd order, and that a 1st-order fallback on a steep bed gives a water-level error: the same mechanism as the brief's 2.5 % to 9 % storage deficit, admitted by TUFLOW for boundary cells only.
- [Likely] SGS Sheet Flow Method A (default) puts the depth above the cell minimum into the friction term, which is larger than the cell-average depth on a sloping cell, so friction is under-read and the face conveys too easily on steep sheet-flow cells; Method B is TUFLOW's knob against it. No source states its effect on peaks or storage.

### Gaps
- The text of Method A of "HPC Mannings Depth Approach" (what depth it used), and the release that switched the default to B: not in the entry read.
- How HPC SGS reads a face's area curve, which level between two partially wet cells, and how V(η) and A(η) enter the pressure term: not published in any source read (also N02's open issue 1).
- The release that introduced SGS Sheet Flow Approach and SGS 2nd Order BC Slope: not stated in the 2026.3 entries; the 2025.x and 2026.x release notes were not read.
- HPC s.7.3.4 (the HPC upstream-controlled flow test) was not read, so the exact trigger for the hybrid's "upstream controlled supercritical flow" is unknown.
- No TUFLOW source read reports too-low or too-high water, or attenuation, in coarse cells on steep channels; no slope limit for any cell size is given.

## 2. InfoWorks ICM 2D: scheme, sub-grid, terrain-sensitive meshing, and steep terrain

### Takeaway
ICM's 2D engine is documented as a **first-order** explicit Godunov finite-volume scheme with Roe's approximate Riemann solver on an unstructured triangle mesh, after Alcrudo and Mulet-Martí (2005, the MULFLOOD model); the help does not describe the bed-slope source term or any edge reconstruction. Since ICM 2026 it has Subgrid Sampling (volume/level tables per element), out of Tech Preview from 2027.2, and its help states plainly that subgrid elements "can sometimes lead to over-rapid propagation speeds, especially at flows going down slopes unimpeded", and advises not to use it on hilly rain-on-mesh zones. That is the closest vendor statement to the brief's symptom; the remedy offered is a finer, non-subgrid mesh, not a numerical fix.

### Cited Findings
- [Certain] The 2D engine is "based on the procedures described in Alcrudo and Mulet-Marti (2005)"; the equations are discretised with "a first-order finite volume explicit scheme", "based upon the Gudunov [sic] numerical scheme", interface fluxes from "the standard Roe's approximate Riemann solver"; robust for "shock capturing" and "super-critical and transcritical flows". Bed slope terms S0,x, S0,y appear in the equations; their discretisation is not described. — [ICM Help 2027, Basic 2D Hydraulic Theory](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2027/ENU/ICM-Help/files/GUID-2869BC5A-BE5B-4ED8-9460-37436F36F65F.htm)
- [Certain] Wet/dry: velocity "is set to zero if the depth is below the threshold value", default Depth Threshold 0.001 m; time step from the CFL condition with "Timestep Stability Control" default 0.95. — [ICM Help 2027, Basic 2D Hydraulic Theory](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2027/ENU/ICM-Help/files/GUID-2869BC5A-BE5B-4ED8-9460-37436F36F65F.htm)
- [Certain] The 2D engine for InfoWorks and SWMM networks is based on the 2D model MULFLOOD (search-result snippet of the Autodesk help; page not opened). — [ICM Help, 2D Simulations](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2024/ENU/ICM-Help/files/GUID-25BBF0B4-338B-4613-9B8B-2268A86DF1B1.htm)
- [Certain] The 2010 EA benchmark's package description (supplied by the vendor): InfoWorks 2D "is based on the Godunov numerical scheme and the so-called Riemann solvers", "fully conservative and shock capturing", unstructured meshes. — [Néelz and Pender 2010, EA SC080035/SR2, Appendix](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a75765c40f0b6360e4744dd/scho0510bsno-e-e.pdf)
- [Certain] Element ground level (standard mesh): vertex heights interpolated from the ground model; the element level is "the average of the sample point levels" of the ground model sampled within the element's triangles, at most 64 points per triangle. Terrain-sensitive meshing splits a triangle when "the range of heights within the triangle exceeds the Maximum height variation", repeating until within the limit or the split would fall below the Minimum element area; it may "not detect narrow linear features such as embankments or channels", which should be modelled explicitly. — [ICM Help 2027, 2D Mesh Generation Methodology](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2027/ENU/ICM-Help/files/GUID-24EB7F2A-778D-4815-8F54-4DE299B8D532.htm)
- [Certain] Subgrid: "The subgrid triangles for each 2D element are used to build volume/level tables"; these tables with the 2D face ground levels define the crest levels between elements; each subgrid face uses 2 to 9 sample points. — [ICM Help 2027, 2D Mesh Generation Methodology](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2027/ENU/ICM-Help/files/GUID-24EB7F2A-778D-4815-8F54-4DE299B8D532.htm)
- [Certain] Subgrid Sampling limits: "Subgrid Sampling can sometimes lead to over-rapid propagation speeds, especially at flows going down slopes unimpeded"; caution for shallow water flowing downhill such as rain on the mesh in hilly areas; "We recommend that Subgrid Sampling is not used for the 2D zone in such cases", and instead on a Mesh zone over the area of interest, low-lying regions or defined flow paths. "Subgrid Sampling only accounts for topographic barriers on the boundaries of the elements", so a crest through an element centre can "tunnel"; for rivers "it is recommended to use a 2D element size smaller than the river bed". The subgrid triangle size removes small initial entries of the volume/level tables, "essential to keep stability", with "a slight volume error" when an element has few triangles. Speed: "often about 5-10 times faster" on GPU than non-subgrid at the same triangle resolution, "as much as 40-80 times faster" on CPU. "From version 2027.2 onwards, Subgrid Sampling is no longer a Tech Preview." — [ICM Help 2027, Using Subgrid Sampling](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2027/ENU/ICM-Help/files/GUID-0AB4AD8A-99DD-4AB1-9E27-083F538B3AE0.htm)
- [Certain] The page refers the reason for the over-rapid propagation to a "Wave propagation speed" section, which was not reached. — [ICM Help 2027, Using Subgrid Sampling](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2027/ENU/ICM-Help/files/GUID-0AB4AD8A-99DD-4AB1-9E27-083F538B3AE0.htm)
- [Certain, title only] Subgrid Sampling was announced for ICM 2026 (blog of 7 April 2025) and extended in 2026.3 (blog of 12 January 2026); an Autodesk post of 25 September 2026 is titled "When should you use subgrid sampling in 2D hydraulic modelling". All three returned HTTP 403; only titles and dates were seen. — [Autodesk Water blog 2025-04-07](https://www.autodesk.com/blogs/water/2025/04/07/new-in-infoworks-icm-2026-sub-grid-sampling-offers-speedier-sims/); [2026-01-12](https://www.autodesk.com/blogs/water/2026/01/12/infoworks-icm-2026-3-raster-workflows-expanded-subgrid-meshing-2d-boundaries-and-a-more-polished-interface/); [2026-09-25](https://www.autodesk.com/blogs/water/2026/09/25/when-should-you-use-subgrid-sampling-in-2d-hydraulic-modelling/)

### Inferences
- [Likely] A first-order Godunov scheme takes the piecewise-constant cell states to each edge; on sloping ground the Riemann problem at an edge then sees each side's centre depth and a bed step, and the result depends on the (undocumented) bed-source treatment. Nothing ICM publishes says whether the edge sees levels or depths, so no claim about S·dx/2 can be made for ICM.
- [Likely] ICM's "over-rapid propagation ... down slopes" with subgrid is the same family of error as the brief's (a sub-grid face that conveys too easily on a slope, so the wave runs fast and attenuates little); ICM documents the symptom and the workaround (no subgrid on hilly rain-on-mesh zones), not the cause.

### Gaps
- Alcrudo and Mulet-Martí (2005) was not read; whether ICM's bed-slope source term is upwinded (Bermúdez-Vázquez type) or a hydrostatic reconstruction is unknown.
- How a subgrid face's conveyance is computed (which water level, flow area or a crest only) is not in the pages read; the "Wave propagation speed" section was not read.
- No Autodesk/Innovyze guidance on element size against bed slope, nor any accuracy figure for coarse elements on steep channels, was found.
- The 2027 help's "first-order" statement could not be checked against an engine paper; whether any higher-order option exists (GPU engine) is not stated in the pages read.

## 3. MIKE 21 FM (DHI), briefly

### Takeaway
MIKE 21 FM is a cell-centred finite-volume model on triangles and quadrilaterals with a "lower order" option (first order in time and space) and a "higher order" one (two-stage 2nd-order Runge-Kutta in time, gradient reconstruction in space); partially dry elements carry mass but no momentum, and a face closes when the wet side's surface is below the partly wet side's bed. DHI's documents read say nothing on face depth over a sloping bed or on cell size for steep channels; the only steep-terrain statement found is that very steep gradients can give negative depths, which are reset with a mass error.

### Cited Findings
- [Certain] "The spatial discretisation of the primitive equations is performed using a cell-centred finite volume method"; 2D elements are triangles or quadrilaterals. — [DHI, MIKE 21 & MIKE 3 Flow Model FM Hydrodynamic Module, Short Description, 2007/2008](https://www.dhigroup.com/upload/dhisoftwarearchive/shortdescriptions/marine/HydrodynamicModuleHD.pdf)
- [Certain] Time integration and space discretisation each have a lower-order option ("First-order explicit Euler scheme. Faster but less accurate.") and a higher-order one ("Two-stage explicit second-order Runge-Kutta scheme."); higher order costs about 2 x in time integration and 1.5 to 2 x in space. — [MIKE+ 2026 documentation, 2D Overland, 2D Numerical Settings](https://userdoc.mike-plus.mikepoweredbydhi.com/en/2026/2DOverland/2DOverland_HydraulicModelling/2D_Numerical_Settings/)
- [Certain, search snippet only] Second-order accuracy is "achieved through a variable gradient reconstruction". — snippet in a DHI search result, document not opened ([search result: DHI hydraulics paper](https://www.dhigroup.com/upload/dhisoftwarearchive/papersanddocs/hydrodynamics/Hydraulics.pdf)).
- [Certain] Wet/dry: "The default values are: drying depth h_dry = 5 mm and wetting depth h_wet = 10 mm"; dry elements leave the computation, partially dry elements have momentum fluxes set to zero and only mass fluxes, wet elements both; a face is treated as closed when the wet element's surface is below the bed of the partially wet element; negative depths are set to zero and the water taken from neighbours (up to 100 iterations). The page says nothing on steep terrain, the bed-slope term or mesh size. — [MIKE+ 2026, 2D Numerical Settings](https://userdoc.mike-plus.mikepoweredbydhi.com/en/2026/2DOverland/2DOverland_HydraulicModelling/2D_Numerical_Settings/)
- [Certain] DHI's flooding-and-drying guidance for the classic (rectilinear) MIKE 21 Flow Model: negative depths "can occur for instance in areas with very steep gradients in the bathymetry ... or by a relatively large time step"; the reset "may potentially create mass"; rule of thumb drying 0.01 m, flooding 0.02 m; single precision at 1,000 m altitude gives depths to about ±0.1 m. Undated; applies to classic MIKE 21, not FM. — [DHI, MIKE 21 Flow Model: hints and recommendations in applications with significant flooding and drying](https://www.dhigroup.com/upload/dhisoftwarearchive/papersanddocs/hydrodynamics/MIKE21SignificantFlodryGuidelines.pdf)

### Inferences
- [Likely] With gradient reconstruction, MIKE 21 FM's higher-order option reconstructs states to the face inside a Riemann solver, so its face depth is a limited reconstruction rather than an upwinded cell value; whether it reconstructs level or depth is not stated in what was read.

### Gaps
- The MIKE 21 FM Hydrodynamic Scientific Documentation was not read: the Riemann solver (Roe is commonly cited for MIKE 21 FM, not verified here), the limiter, whether the reconstruction is of level or depth, and the bed-slope treatment are unconfirmed.
- No DHI guidance on element size for steep channels was found.

## 4. Independent benchmarks (EA 2010 and 2013) on cell size and sloping ground

### Takeaway
The EA benchmark reports read do not isolate the brief's effect: they ran each test at one prescribed resolution (Test 5 valley at 50 m, Test 8A urban at 2 m) with at most one optional alternative, and report spread between packages, not convergence with cell size on slopes. The 2010 report found that on the sloping valley dam break (Test 5) the full models agreed within about 20 % on travel time and 0.4 m on peak level, with TUFLOW (Classic ADI then) and MIKE FLOOD giving oscillating, high velocities; in Test 8A it found 2 m cells too coarse. The 2013 technical report (SC120002) itself was not read.

### Cited Findings
- [Certain] Each 2010 test was specified at one grid (Test 5 "Model grid resolution: 50m" on a 10 m DEM; Test 6A 0.1 m; Test 8A 2 m on a 0.5 m DEM); "Model results for one alternative resolution or mesh may also be provided." — [Néelz and Pender 2010, SC080035/SR2, test specification sheets](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a75765c40f0b6360e4744dd/scho0510bsno-e-e.pdf)
- [Certain] Test 5 (valley about 0.8 x 17 km, slope about 0.01 upstream easing to 0.001, an inflow chosen so that supercritical and subcritical flow both occur): "All full models predicted similar results in terms of travel times (within ~20%), peak water levels (within 0.4m)"; velocity differences up to about 30 %; the DEM was "an artificially smoothed 10m resolution DEM", so effects would be larger on 1 m LiDAR. — [Néelz and Pender 2010, s.4.5.4](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a75765c40f0b6360e4744dd/scho0510bsno-e-e.pdf); slope figures from [TUFLOW 2012 submission, Test 5](https://tuflow.com/media/5004/2012-uk-ea-benchmarking-of-2d-hydraulic-modelling-packages-tuflow-submission.pdf)
- [Certain] "predictions from TUFLOW, ANUGA, and to a lesser extent MIKE FLOOD oscillate in some locations. This results in higher water level and velocity predictions than those obtained by the other shallow water equation packages"; "larger areas of high velocities were predicted by TUFLOW and MIKE FLOOD". These are TUFLOW Classic (ADI) and MIKE FLOOD with classic MIKE 21, not HPC or FM. — [Néelz and Pender 2010, executive summary p.vii and Test 5 discussion p.50](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a75765c40f0b6360e4744dd/scho0510bsno-e-e.pdf)
- [Certain] Test 8A: coarsening the 0.5 m DEM to 2 m by each package's rule changed dry-ground elevations by "typically within a 0.05-0.1m range, although up to ~0.2m"; "the choice of an adequate grid resolution (finer than 2m in a case similar to the present one) is crucial"; velocity differences up to 100 % at 2 m. DHI resampled by averaging the fine cells in each coarse cell. — [Néelz and Pender 2010, Test 8A discussion pp.82-83 and the MIKE FLOOD developer comments](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a75765c40f0b6360e4744dd/scho0510bsno-e-e.pdf)
- [Certain] In TUFLOW's 2012 submission to the second round, TUFLOW FV's "1st order solution produces lower peak velocities" in Test 5 than its 2nd order; TUFLOW GPU (1st order spatial) improved the Test 6A jump at 0.05 m against the specified 0.1 m. — [TUFLOW 2012 submission](https://tuflow.com/media/5004/2012-uk-ea-benchmarking-of-2d-hydraulic-modelling-packages-tuflow-submission.pdf)
- [Certain] The 2013 report (Environment Agency, published 14 August 2013) is distributed on gov.uk as a ZIP (LIT 8570, 7.6 MB); the consultation-site PDF copy returned 404. — [gov.uk publication page](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/benchmarking-the-latest-generation-of-2d-hydraulic-flood-modelling-packages)

### Inferences
- [Likely] The benchmarks give no evidence for or against the brief's mechanism: no test pairs a coarse and a fine grid on a sloping channel with storage or attenuation as the metric; Test 5's 20 % spread in travel time is larger than the 2.5 % to 9 % storage effect under study, so the brief's effect would be invisible there.
- [Guessing] Test 5 at 50 m on a 0.01 slope has S·dx/2 = 0.25 m, so a level-upwinded face would read 0.25 m deep extra; with depths of metres in a dam-break wave this is under 10 %, consistent with it being lost in the spread.

### Gaps
- The 2013 SC120002 technical report (with TUFLOW HPC's predecessor, ICM and MIKE 21 FM results on Tests 5, 6A, 7, 8A) was not read: the gov.uk copy is a ZIP and was not downloaded without the user's word; the consultation PDF is gone.
- No independent study was found that reports TUFLOW HPC, ICM or MIKE 21 FM results against cell size on a steep channel with storage, attenuation or peak flow as the metric.

# Sloping faces have over-read depth since day one

**The face over-read is a day-one kernel property that most sub-grid solvers share. Curing it is worth doing, but the cure will not close the Faydh race, and W47's carry is not the published cure.**

W47's "full form" is not a regime-aware carry in any published sense. [Certain, W47 record] It is a central interpolation of the level, weighted by where the face stands between the centres, with upwind fallbacks at crests, boundaries and fronts. [Likely] That is the one face value the staggered literature marks as unsafe without an upwind switch for supercritical flow, so its eight analytic failures were foreseeable, not bad luck. [Certain, git] The over-read itself was not added later. The face has been read at the upwind cell's level over the face's own invert since the first solver commit, `35ad921` of 1 November, and the quadtree and the aligned strips reuse the same face step. The bias therefore belongs to the kernel; the mesh generator only decides how loudly it shows. [Likely] It is also the norm for sub-grid faces. SFINCS sub-grid and D-Flow FM document the same reading, 3Di uses it by default (and, in its sloping preset, above a 0.3 m thin layer), and HEC-RAS probably does, though HEC never documents its rule. The codes that avoid the bias (TUFLOW HPC without SGS, LISFLOOD-FP, Delft3D's flooding scheme) carry the upwind *depth* to the face, not the level. [Certain, manual read] The only published regime-switched face depth is TUFLOW HPC's 2nd-order rule: the mean of the two cells' depths, sent back to the upwind depth under "short scale reversal or upstream controlled supercritical flow". Its trigger is not in anything read, and no staggered code fades the face value by Froude number. [Certain, W47 runs] The uncomfortable part is this. The first-form fix put uniform flow right (0.966 to 1.010 of normal depth) and brought the strip's flood on Faydh's reach within 0.6 %. Yet it moved the race peaks only 1 to 2 % and left the spread between generators in place. The 3 % gate on 20 m aligned cells therefore needs a second mechanism, and nobody has named it. My choice is a first-order upwind depth carry, the form four production codes document. It is exact in uniform flow on any slope and identical to today's reading on a flat bed. It should be judged on the analytic and slope cases, with the race run and judged separately.

## The over-read was born with the solver on 1 November

[Certain, git, checked against the commit for this report] The first solver commit (`35ad921`, 2026-11-01, "Solver core: staggered explicit scheme with sub-grid tables...") picks the upwind side with `from_left = uf > 0 or (uf == 0 and e_l >= e_r)` and looks up the face section at that side's `e`. Here `e_l` is the left cell's level above its lowest pixel less the face invert above that pixel, which is the left cell's water level over the face's own invert. [Certain, git] The block-uniform quadtree (`655b875`, same day) steps every face, interface faces included, through the same `face_step`. The polygon kernel of the aligned strips (`d8a898e`, 2026-11-07, not yet on the remote) copied the reading. **Squares, the quadtree and the strips have carried the bias since the first commit.** It is not a regression introduced while developing.

[Certain, design record] The scheme family was chosen deliberately on 1 November (decision 19, source review N01): level per cell, conservative momentum per face, the family of TUFLOW HPC, 3Di, SFINCS and HEC-RAS 2D. N01 knew the flat-level problem from 3Di's documentation and added a volume-over-length read of the face section for thin sheet flow, faded out as the downstream depth approaches the upwind depth. [Likely, from my reading of the first commit's kernel; the current kernel may differ] That fade weights the level read by the fourth power of downstream over upwind depth at the face. The ratio tends to one in deep uniform flow, so the thin-flow cure switches itself off in exactly the regime where the deep-flow bias lives. [Certain] Deep flow on a slope was neither covered nor tested.

[Certain, arithmetic] The gates could not see the bias. W3's channel test, the gate for every scheme change, runs at 0.2 % with a normal depth of 0.778 m. Half the bed drop across a 20 m cell is 0.020 m, which is 2.6 % of the depth and inside the test's 3 % rule; W3 measured 0.986 of normal depth. The half-drop S·dx/2 grows with slope and cell size, as the table shows. Measured errors run at about half to two thirds of the half-drop.

| Slope, normal depth | 20 m cells | 40 m cells | 60 m cells |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.2 %, 0.78 m (W3's gate) | 2.6 % | — | — |
| 1 %, 2.1 m | 5 % | 9 % | 14 % |
| 2 %, 2.5 m | 8 % | 16 % | 24 % |

Did the work go in a wrong direction? Four parts of it need separate verdicts.

[Likely] **The scheme choice was not a wrong turn.** SFINCS sub-grid, D-Flow FM and, by a secondary account, HEC-RAS read their faces the same way, so Wadi2D made the mainstream sub-grid choice.

[Certain] **The test ladder was the gap.** No deep-flow case on a slope existed before W47, and the gate's slope was too mild for its own tolerance.

[Certain, W47 runs] **W46's attribution overreached.** Its closing commit (`418ad7c`) put the race excess on the face reading, but the first-form fix moved every race peak by 1 to 2 % and left the generator spread. Where the face is fixed and the race still fails, the face reading cannot be the whole explanation.

[Certain, W47 record] **W47's full form built neither standard form.** The record says plainly that the carry is "a central interpolation ..., not an upwind-biased limited reconstruction and not an upwind-depth carry". The literature, below, prefers those two.

## The kernel owns the bias; the mesh only sets how loudly it shows

The cleanest evidence that the bias is in the kernel and not in the generator is a straight synthetic valley at 1 % slope, with a 40 m bed and 300 m³/s steady. No staircase is involved there. [Certain, W46/W47 runs] At 20 m, squares and aligned cells store **the same water to the digit, 2.5 % less than 5 m squares**; at 10 m they store 1.0 % less. Aligned cells laid on square cells reproduce the lattice kernel to the last digit. A new test, W3's section at 1 % and 300 m³/s on 20 m cells, reads **0.966 of normal depth on both kernels**. Identical errors from two generators on staircase-free ground leave only the shared face reading.

On real ground the generator decides how visible the bias is. [Certain, W47 runs] On Faydh 01's main stem, at 300 m³/s steady, against 2 m squares (converged to 0.2 % at 5 m):

| Mesh | 20 m | 10 m | 5 m |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aligned strip, stored water | **−9.3 %** (surface 0.20 m low) | −4.2 % | — |
| Squares, stored water | +1.0 % | −1.1 % | −0.7 % |

In a 300 m³/s flash flood with no rain, the strip under-attenuates by about 4 % over 14 km, **at 20 and 10 m alike**. The 20 m squares over-attenuate by 5 %, and the 10 m squares come within 1 %.

[Likely, per the W46 record] The squares' staircase across a winding thalweg hides the face over-read. The mechanism of that hiding has not been measured. [Guessing] It looks like one error offsetting another, so the squares read right on Faydh for a reason that would not hold on a straight reach.

The race shows a second, separate fault. [Certain, W46/W47 runs] In the rain-on-grid race to the dam axis (16 h, against 5 m squares), the 20 m squares read −2.8 %. The 20 m centre mesh reads +5.3 % and W45's ring corridors +0.9 %. W45's generator with fixed 20 m thalweg cells reads +13 %. Against the 20 m squares those excesses are **+8.3 %, +2.4 % and +15.8 %**, all from the same kernel. The excess sits on the strip's own channel lines (+11 to 14 % on the main stem's lines). Tributary strips leave those lines untouched, and putting rain only on the strip or only on the hills changes nothing.

[Certain] The first-form fix lowered every peak by 1 to 2 % and left the centre mesh at +9.2 % over the 20 m squares, so the spread did not shrink. **The answer to "kernel or mesh generator" is therefore split.** The depression of stored water and level is the kernel's face reading, on every mesh. The generator-dependent excess in the race is not explained by that reading. It comes either from the generators' geometry or from a kernel term that only aligned geometry excites, and the notes do not decide which.

## Most sub-grid peers share the half-drop; only depth-carriers avoid it

| Model | Face value used for the flow area | Uniform flow on a slope | How it was read |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wadi2D today | Upwind level over the face table's own invert | High by about S·dx/2 | Code |
| SFINCS sub-grid | Higher of the two levels, read in the face table | High [Likely] | Paper, full text |
| SFINCS regular | max(zs) − max(zb), the upwind depth downhill | Exact [Likely] | Paper, full text |
| D-Flow FM | Upwind level minus the lower end-node bed; limited option `Limtyphu`, off by default | High [Likely] | Manual and source code |
| 3Di / Rana | Uniform level; area limiters only below the thin layer | High in deep flow [Likely] | Manual |
| HEC-RAS 2D | Undocumented; "higher of the two" per a 2015 blog | High if the blog holds [Likely] | Manuals and one blog |
| TUFLOW HPC, no SGS, 1st order | Upwind depth, cut only where the bed rises | Exact [Likely] | Paper and manual |
| TUFLOW HPC, no SGS, 2nd order (default) | Mean of the two depths, upwind under reversal or upstream-controlled supercritical flow | Exact [Likely] | Manual |
| TUFLOW HPC, SGS | 2nd-order level interpolation to the face; the rest unpublished | Unknown | Manual command entries |
| Delft3D-FLOW flooding scheme | Upwind level minus the higher bed | Exact [Likely] | Manual |
| LISFLOOD-FP (ACC) | max η − max z | Exact [Certain, algebra] | Paper, full text |
| InfoWorks ICM | First-order Godunov/Roe; sub-grid edge rule unpublished | Unknown | Help pages |

**HEC-RAS: silent on the rule, with a hint that it shares the bias.** [Certain, manuals read] HEC documents three things about the face water surface. It exists. Area, hydraulic radius and n are read from the face table at it. Its time level is θ-averaged in the 5.0 manual, frozen as an explicit face area in 6.6's Diffusion Wave and semi-implicit in the latest SWE-ELM page ([HEC-RAS 5.0 Reference Manual](https://www.hec.usace.army.mil/software/hec-ras/documentation/HEC-RAS%205.0%20Reference%20Manual.pdf); [6.6 DWE solver](https://www.hec.usace.army.mil/confluence/rasdocs/ras1dtechref/6.6/theoretical-basis-for-one-dimensional-and-two-dimensional-hydrodynamic-calculations/2d-unsteady-flow-hydrodynamics/numerical-methods/diffusion-wave-equation-solver)). HEC never documents how that surface is built from the two cells. [Certain] The SWE-ELM page is also internally inconsistent: z_s^(n+θ) in Eq. 3 and z_s^(n+1/2) in step 6 ([SWE-ELM, latest](https://www.hec.usace.army.mil/confluence/rasdocs/ras1dtechref/latest/theoretical-basis-for-one-dimensional-and-two-dimensional-hydrodynamic-calculations/2d-unsteady-flow-hydrodynamics/numerical-methods/eulerian-lagrangian-shallow-water-equation-solver)). The SWE-EM page defines a face depth for advection as a weighted mean of the cells' volume depths, but says nothing on how the area in Q = A·u is evaluated ([SWE-EM, latest](https://www.hec.usace.army.mil/confluence/rasdocs/ras1dtechref/latest/theoretical-basis-for-one-dimensional-and-two-dimensional-hydrodynamic-calculations/2d-unsteady-flow-hydrodynamics/numerical-methods/eulerian-shallow-water-equation-solver)). No user option selects the face level ([2D Computation Options](https://www.hec.usace.army.mil/confluence/rasdocs/r2dum/latest/running-a-model-with-2d-flow-areas/2d-computation-options-and-tolerances)).

[Likely, blog only] The one explicit rule found is a pre-release 2015 blog that cites no HEC source. It says face parameters are "based on the higher of the two neighboring water surface elevations". In downhill flow that is the upwind level, Wadi2D's own reading ([Goodell 2015](https://therassolution.kleinschmidtgroup.com/ras-post/2d-troubleshooting-fragmented-inundation/)).

[Certain] HEC's answer is resolution, not a scheme fix. It asks for smaller cells where the water-surface slope is steep or changing ([Selecting Grid Size and Time Step](https://www.hec.usace.army.mil/confluence/rasdocs/r2dum/latest/running-a-model-with-2d-flow-areas/selecting-an-appropriate-grid-size-and-time-step)). It also admits that the sloping render "can also have the appearance of more water or less water volume" in steep terrain with large cells ([5.0 2D User's Manual](https://www.hec.usace.army.mil/software/hec-ras/documentation/HEC-RAS%205.0%202D%20Modeling%20Users%20Manual.pdf)).

[Certain, full paper] The only controlled steep-ground study found ran HEC-RAS 5.0.7 full momentum on a catchment of 24.6 % mean slope. Coarsening the mesh from 5 to 10 m triangles cut the outlet peak by **3.8 to 3.9 %**: 152.8 to 147.0 m³/s at 30 years and 242.3 to 232.9 at 200 years. A reference finite-volume model moved under 0.7 % ([Costabile et al. 2020](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4441/12/9/2326)). [Guessing] Coarse cells attenuating more is the sign of Wadi2D's squares (1,835, 1,871 and 1,887 m³/s at 20, 10 and 5 m), not of its strips. HEC-RAS's published behaviour therefore matches the mesh in which the bias is hidden.

**TUFLOW HPC: the documented depth carry, and an undocumented SGS reading.** [Certain, paper read] Collecutt and Syme state that "the water depth at a face was taken to be the water depth of the cell that the water was flowing from", reduced only "in the case that the bed elevation of the downwind cell was higher" ([Collecutt and Syme 2017](https://www.tuflow.com/media/5102/2017-experimental-benchmarking-of-mesh-size-and-time-step-convergence-for-a-1st-and-2nd-order-swe-finite-volume-scheme-collecutt-and-syme-iahr.pdf)). [Likely] That rule is min(h_up, η_up − z_face). On a downhill face it returns h_up exactly, so the half-drop cannot arise, and this is the documented precedent for carrying the depth while staying upwind.

[Certain, manual read] In the current manual, 2nd order (the default) takes "the average of the two cell averaged depths". It moves to the upstream depth "when the solution shows short scale reversal or upstream controlled supercritical flow" ([TUFLOW Manual 2026.0, s.7.1.1](https://docs.tuflow.com/classic-hpc/manual/2026.0/Solvers-2.html)). [Likely] With the face bed at the mean of the two cell beds, the average of the depths equals the mean level minus the face bed. That is W47's first form, made safe by two switches W47 never built. [Certain] The 2017 paper hybridises the velocity and always upwinds the depth; the 2026 manual hybridises the depth. The sources disagree, and neither says which describes the current code.

[Certain, manual] Under SGS the reading is not published. The "SGS 2nd Order BC Slope" entry says that inadvertent 1st-order interpolation of the level at boundary cells with steep beds "can cause a slight water level error near the boundary". Method B fixes it, but Method A remains the default ([Manual 2026.3, Appendix A](https://docs.tuflow.com/classic-hpc/manual/2026.3/80-AppendicesA-TCFCommands.html)). [Likely] That entry is TUFLOW admitting the same mechanism, for boundary cells only.

[Certain, from an earlier note, not re-read] On the Brisbane River 2011 flood, 2nd-order SGS stayed within ±0.2 m of flood marks at 20 to 60 m cells. First order without SGS read too high, not too low ([Huxley et al. 2022](https://www.tuflow.com/media/7555/2022-hydraulic-modelling-2d-cell-size-result-convergence-huxley-et-al-hwrs.pdf)). [Certain] No TUFLOW source read reports too-low water or under-attenuation in coarse cells on steep channels.

**InfoWorks ICM: names the symptom, prescribes a workaround.** [Certain, help read] ICM's 2D engine is a first-order explicit Godunov scheme with Roe's solver on triangles. Its sub-grid option, out of Tech Preview from 2027.2, "can sometimes lead to over-rapid propagation speeds, especially at flows going down slopes unimpeded", and Autodesk advises against sub-grid on hilly rain-on-mesh zones ([ICM Help 2027, Using Subgrid Sampling](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2027/ENU/ICM-Help/files/GUID-0AB4AD8A-99DD-4AB1-9E27-083F538B3AE0.htm)). [Likely] That is the same family of error: a sub-grid face that conveys too easily on a slope. The cause sits in a "Wave propagation speed" section that was not reached, and Autodesk's blog posts returned 403.

**MIKE 21 FM: silent.** [Certain] Nothing DHI publishes in what was read addresses face depth over a sloping bed or cell size on steep channels. Its Scientific Documentation was not read ([MIKE+ 2026, 2D Numerical Settings](https://userdoc.mike-plus.mikepoweredbydhi.com/en/2026/2DOverland/2DOverland_HydraulicModelling/2D_Numerical_Settings/)).

**3Di, SFINCS, D-Flow FM: the same reading, the problem acknowledged, no deep-flow fix.**

[Certain, manual] Rana states that a uniform level per cell "overestimates" the wet edge section and the discharge on slopes. Its area limiters engage only below the thin layer, 0.3 m in the sloping preset. Type 2, upstream volume over upstream maximum surface area, is a depth carry for sheet flow ([Rana simulation settings](https://docs.ranawaterintelligence.com/h_simulation_settings.html); [slope tutorial](https://docs.ranawaterintelligence.com/e_2d_slope_tutorial.html)). [Likely] At 1 to 3 m depth on 1 to 3 % slopes every 3Di limiter is inactive, so 3Di carries the same bias.

[Certain, full text] SFINCS sub-grid reads its face tables at z_u = max(zs_m, zs_m+1). The 2025 paper admits that the constant-level assumption fails where gradients are steep, and offers no correction ([van Ormondt et al. 2025](https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/18/843/2025/)).

[Certain, manual] D-Flow FM sets hu = ζ_up − min(bl1, bl2), and its manual says that higher order is not available "regardless of limtyphu" ([D-Flow FM Technical Reference 2027.01](https://content.oss.deltares.nl/delft3d/D-Flow_FM_Technical_Reference_Manual.pdf)). [Certain, code] Its source does implement a limited reconstruction of the face level ([sethu.f90](https://github.com/Deltares/Delft3D/blob/main/src/engines_gpl/dflowfm/packages/dflowfm_kernel/src/dflowfm_kernel/compute/sethu.f90)), so manual and code disagree. [Guessing] The code adds the full minmod with no ½ factor, which for a uniform gradient lands the face level on the downstream cell's level. It should not be copied unchecked.

**Delft3D-FLOW and LISFLOOD-FP: exact by construction, and Delft3D states the trade-off.** [Certain, manual] Delft3D's flooding scheme takes the flow-through height from the upwind level over a staircase bed whose face value is the higher of the two beds. The manual also states the trade-off: averaging the level near steep bed gradients "sets velocity points dry too early", while upwinding raises discharge because the upwind level exceeds the average ([Delft3D-FLOW User Manual §10.8.2](https://content.oss.deltares.nl/delft3d4/Delft3D-FLOW_User_Manual.pdf)). [Certain, algebra on a full paper] LISFLOOD-FP's h_f = max η − max z equals the upwind depth downhill. Its known weakness is the local-inertial set above Fr ≈ 0.5, not the face depth ([Shaw et al. 2021](https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/14/3577/2021/)).

**Collocated research codes reconstruct a planar surface.** [Certain, preprint read in full] A DHI/DTU sub-grid finite volume with a planar free surface per cell cut the MacDonald L2 depth error from 9.65e-2 and 4.73e-2 to 2.16e-3 and 5.42e-4. The authors also report that constant-level cells concentrate water "at the downhill end" of steep elements ([Bitsch et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.01895)). [Certain] That gain mixes the planar surface with WENO momentum and RK2, so it does not isolate the face effect.

[Likely] **The pattern across all of this is the main finding of this section.** Non-sub-grid staggered codes take the face bed as the higher bed (max z, or Delft3D's MIN depth option), so the upwind level minus that bed is the upwind depth and the half-drop never appears. A sub-grid table moves the face invert down to the lowest pixel along the face, below the upwind cell's mean bed. Every code that reads that table at the upwind level brings the half-drop back. Wadi2D, SFINCS sub-grid and D-Flow FM end up in the same place for the same reason. No sub-grid vendor publishes a deep-flow cure. TUFLOW hints at one, and ICM and HEC tell users to refine or to avoid sub-grid on hills.

## A regime-aware carry exists only as TUFLOW's hybrid; the depth carry is the standard

The literature offers three forms for a staggered continuity face value, and a Froude fade is not among them.

**The first-order upwind depth carry** is used by TUFLOW without SGS, LISFLOOD-FP, Delft3D and regular SFINCS. [Certain, preprint read in full] Zijlema names it ("surface elevation upwind, bed by a min operator") as possibly more accurate on coarse grids, though he does not use it ([Zijlema 2019 preprint](https://swash.sourceforge.io/online_doc/rh.pdf)). [Certain, algebra] Carrying depth rather than level is exact for uniform flow on any slope without a limiter, because the depth is constant there.

**The upwind-biased limited reconstruction** has two published versions. [Certain, full text] Herbin and co-workers apply it to the depth. They require the face value to be a convex combination of the two cells and upwind-biased, prove positivity at **half the upwind time step**, and test only flat-bed shocks, a smooth vortex and closed-domain topography ([Herbin et al., arXiv:2111.09726](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.09726)). D-Flow FM's `Limtyphu` applies it to the level, in code. [Certain, algebra] Every TVD limiter has φ(1) = 1, so η_f = η_U + ½φ(r)(η_U − η_UU) is exact on a uniform gradient and reverts to upwind wherever r ≤ 0: at a jump, at a front and at an extremum. [Likely] Since the limited value lies between η_D and η_U, the face area never exceeds A(η_U).

**The regime hybrid** is TUFLOW HPC's 2nd-order rule above. Its trigger lives in a manual section (s.7.3.4) that was not read.

[Certain, abstract only] The nearest relative of a Froude fade is collocated. Aureli et al. 2008 blend depth-gradient and surface-gradient intercell depths with a Froude-dependent weight, and the blend scored at least as well as either method in their tests. The weight formula was not read ([Aureli et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.advwatres.2008.03.005)). [Certain] No staggered or sub-grid scheme found fades the face value by Froude number.

The full form failed in the places the literature predicts. [Certain, W47 record] Its failures are tabulated below.

| Case | Upwind read (today) | Full-form carry | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ritter dam break, depth RMSE, m | 0.0085 | 0.0117 | 0.01 |
| MacDonald subcritical, m | 0.0100 | 0.0206 | 0.015 |
| MacDonald supercritical, m (discharge ratio) | 0.0105 (1.000) | 0.0341 (0.986) | 0.012 |
| MacDonald jump, m (discharge ratio) | 0.0324 (1.000) | 0.0581 (0.959) | 0.05 |
| 45° channel, 3 cells, vertical walls, depth error % | 0.13 | 3.86 | 3 |
| W3's channel at 0° / 45°, depth over normal | 0.986 / 1.025 | 1.007 / 1.069 | 3 % |

[Certain, algebra] A centred face value violates Herbin's upwind-bias condition, so their positivity proof does not cover it. [Certain, manual] TUFLOW calls the plain average "not total variation diminishing (TVD) and ... known to be unstable". [Guessing] In supercritical flow a central value brings downstream information into the continuity flux. That fits the discharge deficits of 1.4 % and 4.1 % on the supercritical and jump profiles, but no source tests it directly. [Likely] The 45° failure is geometric, not a matter of regime: averaging across a staircase face pairs a wet channel cell with a bank cell, which is Delft3D's "dry too early" warning. The full form had fallbacks at crests, boundaries and fronts, but none for upstream-controlled supercritical flow or short-scale reversal, the two triggers TUFLOW publishes.

Would a regime-aware carry solve the issue? It depends on which issue. [Certain, W47 runs] **For the stored-water deficit in uniform and gradually varied flow, the evidence says yes.** The first form took W3 from 0.986 to 1.007 and the 1 % test from 0.966 to 1.010. It brought straight-valley storage equal across cell sizes to 0.4 %, and the strip's flood on Faydh's reach to within 0.6 % at 20 m. **For the Faydh race, the evidence says no.** [Likely] A TUFLOW-type switch should keep those gains where friction sets the depth and return to upwind in supercritical flow. Building one, however, means inventing a trigger no source read publishes, in a sub-grid setting where TUFLOW's own reading is unpublished.

Translating any depth-based form into sub-grid tables raises a problem the notes leave open. [Likely] "Depth" has no single meaning in a sub-grid cell. The depth-carry analogue is to read the face table at η_U − Δz_ref, the upwind level lowered by the bed drop from the upwind cell's reference bed to the face's. It must be capped at η_U so that a crest face never sees the pool's depth over the crest. [Likely, geometry] The reference cannot be the cell's lowest pixel. On a plane, or on a prismatic channel along the slope, the upwind cell's lowest pixel lies at its downstream edge, on the face, so the carry would be zero; the reference has to be a mean or centre bed. [Guessing] One candidate is half the drop between a cell's inflow and outflow face inverts; only a test can settle it. [Certain, algebra] On a flat bed Δz_ref = 0 and the carry reproduces today's reading exactly, so a flat-bed case such as Ritter cannot move. On an adverse step the cap returns today's reading. [Certain] No source applies this form to deep flow in sub-grid tables. Its precedents are all non-sub-grid, except Rana's type 2, which serves thin flow only.

## Conclusion: options and the choice

[Likely] The pivotal insight is that the sub-grid table, not a slip in Wadi2D, brought back an error that non-sub-grid staggered codes had designed out with max(z). Two consequences follow. [Certain, in what was read] The cure is a depth carry dressed for sub-grid tables, a form nobody has published for deep flow, so Wadi2D's own tests are the only acceptance evidence available. [Certain, W47 runs] And the face fix and the race are two problems: the race's 3 % rule cannot be the face fix's acceptance test, because the first form already showed that the race spread survives a face that is right in uniform flow.

| Option | What it would fix | What it would risk | Test that settles it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. First-order upwind depth carry**: face table read at η_U − Δz_ref, capped at η_U | The uniform-flow deficit, by construction. Upwind character kept everywhere, so supercritical flow, jumps, fronts and crests keep today's behaviour. Flat-bed cases unchanged. No upwind-upwind neighbour needed on quadtree interfaces or polygons. Area ≤ A(η_U), so no new positivity limit [Likely] | Δz_ref undefined in sub-grid. First order in gradually varied flow, so MacDonald subcritical can move either way. The 45° staircase is untested. No sub-grid precedent | The eight analytic cases at today's limits; W3 at 0° and 45° within 3 %; the 1 % test within 3 % of normal depth; straight-valley storage across 5, 10 and 20 m within the first form's 0.4 %; Faydh main-stem steady storage and flash-flood attenuation against the 2 m squares |

I would build **A** on `feature/face-level`, run **E** alongside it on HEC-RAS 6.6, and adopt **F** as the acceptance rule. A goes first for five reasons:

- [Certain] It is the form four production codes document: TUFLOW HPC without SGS at first order, LISFLOOD-FP, Delft3D's flooding scheme and regular SFINCS.
- [Certain, algebra] It is exact in uniform flow on a slope, the case that fails today.
- [Certain, algebra] It cannot move a flat-bed case.
- [Likely] It keeps the upwind fallback everywhere the central form broke.
- [Certain] It needs neither a new stencil nor a regime threshold.

I would escalate to C only if A leaves MacDonald subcritical or the slope cases outside their limits, and I would hold B back because it rests on a trigger no source publishes. A's real risk is Δz_ref, so the first runs should be the plane, the straight valley and the 1 % test, where the right answer is known in closed form.

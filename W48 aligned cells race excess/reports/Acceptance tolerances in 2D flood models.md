# The 3 % rule came from the agent

**[Certain] The coding agent set the 3 % rule, and you never approved it. No vendor, regulator or benchmark programme publishes a rule of its form, which is a new mesh within x % of a peer mesh at the same cell size. The rule's flaw is its reference, not its number.**

[Certain, project record] The rule began on 1 November in W3 as a sound test: the depth had to be within 3 % of Manning normal depth, an analytic reference. W36 kept the 3 % but swapped the reference for the same run with no lines. W45 carried that form into the aligned-mesh race as "Faydh 01 within 3 % of the 20 m squares". [Certain, read] Seven vendors were checked: HEC-RAS, TUFLOW, InfoWorks ICM, MIKE 21, Flood Modeller, Iber and Deltares. They publish numeric thresholds only for mass balance and run-time stability. For cell-size convergence, and for a new mesh or solver option judged against a reference, every one shows comparisons without a pass mark. TUFLOW's developers write that setting convergence criteria still needs industry consultation. [Certain, read] The guidance bodies fix numbers for three things. Levels against observed marks: EA ±0.15 m and FEMA 0.5 ft, while ARR gives 0.2 to 0.3 m as examples and refuses to make them prescriptive. Mass balance: SEPA ±1 % cumulative, ARR investigates above 1 to 2 %, and ARR's Project 15 accepts 0 to 5 % of outlet volume on rain-on-grid. Cells across a flow path: 3 to 8. The only model-against-model number anywhere is FEMA's 0.5 ft tie-in of water levels between models, and no document sets a peak-discharge tolerance between meshes. [Likely] 3 % is a reasonable size, inside what vendors report as agreement on easy cases. The reference is what is wrong: two 20 m meshes compared with each other measure the difference of two discretisation errors. [Certain, arithmetic] Re-scored against the 5 m squares, which is the finest run and TUFLOW's own kind of reference, the 20 m aligned mesh reads +4.6 % and the 20 m squares −4.3 % on the current kernels. The "+9.3 % failure" is therefore two errors of similar size on opposite sides, and at 10 m both constructions sit within 2 %. My recommendation, for your decision: retire the peer comparison as a gate. Judge each mesh against the converged answer, on the quantities you design with, with the tolerance written down before the run.

### Vendors gate only mass and stability

[Certain, read] **Every published vendor threshold is an internal health check.** The list is TUFLOW's ±1 % cumulative mass error, ICM's 0.5 % node flag, Flood Modeller's example 5 % stop, and the Courant and control-number limits. For anything that judges accuracy, the vendors report and do not rule. HEC runs MacDonald's case from 1 to 32 m, fits an order of about 1.3 and sets no pass mark ([RD-52](https://www.hec.usace.army.mil/confluence/rasdocs/hgt/files/latest/384140705/384140719/1/1790798753494/HEC-RAS+7.0+V%26V+Spetember+2026.pdf)). Its coarse-mesh evidence is one doubling per EA test, judged by eye ([RD-51](https://www.hec.usace.army.mil/software/hec-ras/documentation/RD-51_Benchmarking_2D.pdf)). TUFLOW gives four methods for convergence and a 95 %-of-points template with the tolerance left empty. On whether a model has converged, the manual says the call "is subjective and will depend on the purpose of the modelling" ([TUFLOW Manual 3.2](https://docs.tuflow.com/classic-hpc/manual/2026.0/ModelResolutionDiscretisation-2.html)). Huxley, Syme, Ryan and Collecutt write that their 2022 paper does not address convergence acceptance criteria, and that setting them needs industry consultation ([Huxley et al. 2022](https://www.tuflow.com/media/7555/2022-hydraulic-modelling-2d-cell-size-result-convergence-huxley-et-al-hwrs.pdf)). Iber's within 1 % of the EA packages is the same kind of evidence as W45's gate: peer agreement, reported after the fact. [Likely] The nearest vendor statement to "within 3 % of the squares" is TUFLOW's remark that HPC 2nd order and Classic usually differ by a few % of depth in the main flow paths. That is a description of typical difference in level, not a rule on discharge ([HPC FAQ](https://wiki.tuflow.com/HPC_FAQ)).

### Agencies fix observed levels and mass balance, not mesh tolerances

[Certain, read] **The guidance numbers cluster on peak level against flood marks, and they match on both sides of the Atlantic.** The EA's ±0.15 m and FEMA's 0.5 ft (0.152 m) are the same figure ([EA best practice](https://gov.uk/government/publications/river-modelling-technical-standards-and-assessment/hydraulic-modelling-best-practice-model-approach); [FEMA GD 52](https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/documents/fema_general-hydraulics-guidance_112022.pdf)). ARR declines to make calibration criteria prescriptive and gives 0.2 to 0.3 m only as examples ([ARR 2019](https://arr-software.org/pdfs/ARR_190514.pdf)). USACE says rigorous calibration guidance cannot be given ([EM 1110-2-1416](https://chet-aero.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/em-1110-2-1416.pdf)). On mass balance, SEPA's ±1 % cumulative and ARR's investigate-above-1-to-2 % agree with TUFLOW ([SEPA](https://www.sepa.org.uk/media/219653/flood_model_guidance_v2.pdf)). Project 15 allows 0 to 5 % of outlet volume on rain-on-grid ([P15](https://arr.ga.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0019/40573/ARR_Project15_TwoDimensional_Modelling_DraftReport.pdf)). The only discharge tolerance in any guidance is CIWEM's +25/−15 % on peak flow, ±10 % at critical locations, and it is against observed sewer-survey data ([CIWEM](https://www.ciwem.org/assets/pdf/Special%20Interest%20Groups/Urban%20Drainage%20Group/Code%20of%20Practice%20for%20the%20Hydraulic%20Modelling%20of%20Ur.pdf)). **The only fixed model-against-model number is FEMA's 0.5 ft tie-in of water levels where two models meet.** When FEMA accepts a new model or software version, it asks for testing and documentation, with no percentage ([GD 81](https://transportation.ky.gov/Highway-Design/Drainage%20Manual/FEMA%20-%20Hydraulics%20Two-Dimensional%20Analyses%20Guidance%20-%20Nov%202023.pdf); [GD 98](https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/documents/fema_accepting-numerical-models-guidance_022018.pdf)). The EA benchmark that most vendors cite set no pass mark. It described spreads between mature shallow-water codes: about 0.4 m on peak level, about 20 % on travel time on the dam-break valley, and up to 100 % on velocity in shallow urban flow at 2 m cells ([EA 2010](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a75765c40f0b6360e4744dd/scho0510bsno-e-e.pdf); [EA 2013](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6033a943d3bf7f721f4b0d49/_SC120002_Benchmarking_2D_hydraulic_models_Report.pdf)). [Likely] A self-set 3 % on peak flow between two meshes of one code is therefore neither required nor contradicted by any standard. It is tighter than any spread the EA treated as normal between packages, and it measures discharge where the profession measures level.

## Practice judges a mesh against the finest run, an exact answer or data

[Certain, read] **Where anyone judges a coarse or new mesh, the reference is the finest feasible run, an exact solution, or observations. It is never a peer mesh at the same size.** TUFLOW halves the cell until results stop changing and takes the penultimate size. Its histogram method compares 100 or more points against the finest feasible size ([TUFLOW Manual 3.2](https://docs.tuflow.com/classic-hpc/manual/2026.0/ModelResolutionDiscretisation-2.html)). Huxley's 2018 slides use the finest run as the target. They also say a converged model is not thereby correct, and that calibration to historic events is still needed ([Huxley 2018](https://tuflow.com/media/4968/201809-2d-model-design-fundamentals-what-2d-mesh-resolution-is-necessary-fma-nevada-usa.pdf)). SFINCS's sub-grid paper uses the analytic uniform-flow discharge. It reports **within 2 % at every grid size from 5 to 500 m in a straight channel**, and about **+51 % at 500 m once the meanders sit inside a cell** ([van Ormondt et al. 2024](https://egusphere.copernicus.org/preprints/2024/egusphere-2024-1839/egusphere-2024-1839.pdf)). Kitts et al. use Manning's solution and the De Vriend U-bend flume ([Kitts et al. 2020](https://tuflow.com/media/5022/2020-mesh-orientation-and-cell-size-sensitivity-in-2d-swe-solvers-kitts-et-al-iahr-river-flow-delft.pdf)). The one paper found with numeric mesh-acceptance thresholds is David and Schmalz on HEC-RAS rain-on-grid. They screen every configuration against a 1 m mesh on a 0.25 m sub-grid with NSE at least 0.8, a level difference of 0.03 to 0.05, a volume deficit of 5 % and a flooded-area fit of 0.28. The units are printed as cm while the results are in m; [Likely] metres is meant ([David and Schmalz 2021](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/water/water-13-02346/article_deploy/water-13-02346.pdf)). The same study found that ±10 % on Manning moved the peak by 5 to 8 %, which is as large as the mesh band at adequate resolution.

[Certain, read] **The grid convergence index is defined, but nobody states an acceptable value for it.** Roache's method uses a safety factor of 1.25 on three grids and 3.0 on two. Celik et al. recommend a refinement ratio above 1.3 and an asymptotic-range check ([NPARC](https://www.grc.nasa.gov/www/wind/valid/tutorial/spatconv.html); [Celik et al. 2008, copy](https://es.scribd.com/document/288804583/Procedure-for-Estimation-and-Reporting-of-Uncertainty-Due-to-Discretization-in-CFD-Applications)). NPARC asks only that the value be small. No 2D flood study found applies the GCI with an accepted value. A 2026 HEC-RAS and TUFLOW comparison explicitly applied neither Richardson extrapolation nor a GCI ([MDPI Sci 2026](https://www.mdpi.com/2413-4155/8/8/219), search summary only). [Likely] That leaves a solver developer free to adopt the formal route. It is stricter than what the flood profession does in practice.

[Likely] **By every published cells-across rule, the W48 pair is under-resolved, so its excess is expected behaviour, not an anomaly of the aligned construction.** The rules are 3 to 4 cells with sub-grid sampling and 6 to 8 without (TUFLOW), at least 5 (Project 15), 3 to 4 (SEPA), 5 to 7 for in-channel detail (HEC), and 6 to 8 for flow separation (ARR). Two cells across a 20 m gorge in a tight bend sits below all of them. Huxley's 2018 valley converged only at 5 or more elements across. He shows why with a bend: each cell carries one velocity per face, so the super-elevation between the outer and inner bank is lost when few cells span the channel ([Huxley 2018](https://tuflow.com/media/4968/201809-2d-model-design-fundamentals-what-2d-mesh-resolution-is-necessary-fma-nevada-usa.pdf)). SFINCS's meander failure is the published cousin: planform curvature inside a cell defeats the sub-grid tables. [Likely] It also follows that the aligned 20 m and 10 m runs are not a systematic refinement of the channel. If the bank rule keeps two cells across at every size, as W48 records, the channel section is not refined between the pair. A Richardson estimate from the aligned pair has no formal basis in that reach, and refinement alone will not remove the bend deficit. More rows across the channel would.

The aligned pair, extrapolated at any plausible order from 1 to 2, settles at 1,847 to 1,882 m3/s: 0.8 to 2.8 % below the squares' limit. To meet the squares' limit it would need an order of 4.1, which this scheme does not have. **On option A, the two constructions do not demonstrably converge to one answer.** With W49's local radius on, they do. The aligned pair reaches the squares' limit of about 1,808 at an order of 1.3, inside the squares' own 1.25 to 1.51. The old kernel needed 2.2. [Likely] So a converged-reference test is also a test of the kernel: it separates a mesh that is coarse from a construction that heads somewhere else. The peer gate read +9.3 % and +8.3 % on these two kernels and could not tell them apart.

[Guessing] **Four caveats limit how far Table 3 can be pushed.** First, an observed order of 0.95 on option A, for a scheme meant to be second order away from fronts, suggests the squares are not yet in the asymptotic range. A fourth grid is the only check, and with three grids the asymptotic test is circular. Second, the dam-axis peak is one number that integrates the whole hillslope response. W45's rings land close at 20 m on that one number (−0.6 % against the 5 m squares), and that proves little. The project record has a single result line on 20 m cells of either kind reading 15 % high or 55 % low at some stations ([project state](<../../CLAUDE.md>)). Third, the race's noise floor is unmeasured. In the Block 2 hybrid at 80 m, the same dam axis's raw peak moved 1.9 % under a 10^-6 nudge of the Courant number, while its 15-minute peak moved 0.06 % ([W33 README](<../../W33 backend parity/README.md>)). Fourth, the aligned pair is not a geometric refinement of the channel, as argued above. [Likely] Converted to level, 3 % on discharge in a 4.6 m deep gorge is 1.8 to 3.0 % of depth, for ratings between Q ∝ h^(5/3) and Q ∝ h. That is **8 to 14 cm, inside the ±0.15 m the EA accepts against flood marks**. A mesh error of that size would be invisible to calibration. It is still a bias that stacks onto the lag, ARF and Manning uncertainties.

## Recommendation, for your decision: score each mesh against the converged answer

**This is my proposal, not a standard.** No body publishes one to adopt, and every number in it is yours to fix before the next run. It follows the three things the published practice agrees on: the reference is a converged or exact answer, the quantity is an essential result, and the tolerance is written down and agreed in advance ([TUFLOW Manual 3.2](https://docs.tuflow.com/classic-hpc/manual/2026.0/ModelResolutionDiscretisation-2.html); [ARR P15](https://arr.ga.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0019/40573/ARR_Project15_TwoDimensional_Modelling_DraftReport.pdf); [SEPA](https://www.sepa.org.uk/media/219653/flood_model_guidance_v2.pdf)).

| Part | Proposed rule | Precedent |
|---|---|---|
| Reference | The converged estimate: Richardson extrapolation from the squares at 20, 10 and 5 m, reported with its GCI, and checked by a fourth grid at 2.5 m (or a non-integer series at r ≈ 1.4). If the fourth grid moves the estimate by more than the GCI, the finest run becomes the reference and the report says so | TUFLOW's finest feasible size; HEC's analytic case with observed order; Celik et al. |
| Quantities | At each design line: the 15-minute peak, the hydrograph volume and the time to peak; the raw peak reported, not gated. At design points: peak water level. Over the design area: the share of wet cells whose peak depth lies within tolerance of the converged map | TUFLOW's histogram method; EA and FEMA work in levels; ARR asks for timing and shape |
| Tolerance, proposed | 15-minute peak and volume within 3 % of the converged estimate, at the cell size used for design. Peak level within 0.10 m at design points. 95 % of wet cells within 0.10 m or 5 % of depth, whichever is greater | TUFLOW's 95 % template; CIWEM's depth form (a metre figure or a percentage, whichever is greater); kept well inside the EA's ±0.15 m against marks |
| Noise floor | Before any gate, repeat each run with the Courant number nudged by 10^-6. A tolerance under twice the spread is not testable | Project record, W33 |
| Build checks | Cumulative mass error within ±1 %. A reach with fewer than 3 cells across the design-flow water width is reported as under-resolved and checked by a finer local run | TUFLOW and SEPA ±1 %; TUFLOW's 3 to 4 cells with SGS, SEPA 3 to 4, P15 at least 5 |
| Peer comparison | Aligned against squares at the same cell size is kept as a diagnostic, never as a gate | No vendor or guidance uses it as a gate |

[Certain, arithmetic] On the raw peaks recorded so far, this criterion moves the decision from which construction to use to which cell size each can be used at. **At 20 m neither passes, and the 20 m squares, the old yardstick, fail worst.** On option A they read −4.3 % against the finest run and −5.8 % against the extrapolation; with W49 on, −3.6 % and −4.2 %. The aligned 20 m reads +4.6 % and +2.9 %, or +4.4 % and +3.75 %. **At 10 m both pass a 3 % band** with W49 on (−1.6 % and +1.6 % against the extrapolation). On option A the aligned mesh passes (+1.8 %, +0.2 %) and the squares sit on the line (−1.5 %, −3.0 %). That agrees with the project's current working advice to use 10 m for design lines. Note, though, that the gate's quantity, the 15-minute peak, has not been read for these runs yet. [Guessing] The extra cost is one 2.5 m square run of Faydh 01's 74 km², about 12 M cells. Scaled from Block 2's 10 m timing, that is of the order of 2 h on the RTX 5080, once per kernel change. The criterion drops nothing W3 did right: the analytic cases stay as code verification with their own pre-set tolerances.

## Conclusion

[Likely] The peer gate's real failure is that it took an unconverged mesh as truth. On Faydh 01 that mesh, the 20 m squares, fails a converged-reference test by more than the aligned mesh it was judging. Once the reference is the converged answer, the question is which cell size each construction needs in which reach, and the record already answers it for design lines: 10 m, with narrow tight bends flagged by a cells-across check. The more useful by-product is diagnostic. On option A the two constructions do not head to one answer, and with W49's local radius they do. Any kernel change could be gated on that consistency, which the 3 % peer rule cannot see.

[Likely] Because the profession has no number to borrow, the strongest position before a reviewer is the one TUFLOW and FEMA both point to: a criterion written before the run, against a converged reference, on the design quantities, and documented with its GCI. That makes the tolerance a design decision, not a software convention. It belongs beside the freeboard and spillway margins it protects, and only you can set those. A 3 % mesh error is about 40 to 60 % of what ±10 % on Manning did to the peak in David and Schmalz's catchment. Whether that is small enough depends on how much of your design margin you are prepared to spend on discretisation.

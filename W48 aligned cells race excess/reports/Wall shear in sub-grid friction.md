# Bed-only faces read narrow gorges shallow

**[Likely] Wall shear explains the whole gap between the two solvers in the gorge, and the uncomfortable half is that Wadi2D's clean, mesh-independent 4.40 m is the low answer: the physical depth is near 5.3 m.**

[Certain, arithmetic] Wadi2D's 4.40 m is not a numerical result waiting to be explained. It is the exact normal depth (4.398 m) of the face formula Wadi2D uses: a sum of pixel strips on plan width that gives the walls no shear. Every Wadi2D mesh from 2 to 20 m reproduces it. [Likely] HEC-RAS's 5.28 m at 20 m and 4.70 m at 5 m come from its own documented formula, one hydraulic radius per face with the walls in that face's wetted perimeter. An emulation of that rule lands within 0.15 m and 0.03 m of the two runs, and it falls toward the bed-only sum as the faces shrink. The gap between the solvers is therefore wholly a difference in how they charge the walls, and HEC-RAS's 12 % spread between its own cell sizes is the price of lumping wall perimeter over a face's width. [Likely] The physics sits near the top of the range, not the bottom. Smooth-flume shear data put about 40 % of the boundary shear on the walls at this aspect ratio. Spada et al.'s calibrated local-hydraulic-radius method gives 5.34 m and single-section Manning 5.39 m. Once one misread variable is traced, all three research notes converge on 5.2 to 5.4 m, so Wadi2D reads this gorge about 17 % shallow. A published 1D example at the same aspect ratio shows nearly the same depth ratio when the walls are dropped, 0.80 against the gorge's 0.82. [Likely] The bias exceeds 10 % of depth below a width-to-depth ratio of about 6, exceeds 5 % below about 15, and falls under 2 % above 50, so it lives in incised hill wadis and dam gorges, not on the plain. [Certain, measured] It cannot cause the aligned-versus-square difference: the straight gorge reads 4.38 to 4.41 m on every Wadi2D mesh. [Certain, arithmetic] It does disqualify HEC-RAS as a referee in narrow gorges, because HEC-RAS's aligned 20 m match on the tight sine is two errors of about 0.6 m cancelling. My choice is a local-hydraulic-radius face table, built in the table builder behind a switch. It should be judged first on mesh independence and on wide channels, then on measured narrow-channel data. I would not adopt HEC-RAS's per-face radius, more cells across or a blanket n. The HEC-RAS 2.5 m run of the straight gorge was still running when the notes were written; its result decides whether HEC-RAS converges toward Wadi2D's bed-only answer.

## Two face formulas account for the whole 0.3 to 0.9 m gap

[Certain, read in full] HEC's hydraulic reference defines the face area as the integral of the wetted width up to the water surface. It defines the face wetted perimeter as the arc length of the wetted ground profile, so a near-vertical wall contributes almost its full wetted height. It adds that "hydraulic radius and conveyance can be easily computed" from these ([HEC-RAS TRM, Subgrid Bathymetry](https://www.hec.usace.army.mil/confluence/rasdocs/ras1dtechref/latest/theoretical-basis-for-one-dimensional-and-two-dimensional-hydrodynamic-calculations/2d-unsteady-flow-hydrodynamics/subgrid-bathymetry)). [Certain, read in full] The user's manual calls each face "like a detailed cross section", tabled as elevation against area, wetted perimeter and roughness. Its composite-n option weights each segment by its local wetted perimeter "in the same manner as for 1D channels" ([HEC-RAS 2D User's Manual, hydraulic property tables](https://www.hec.usace.army.mil/confluence/rasdocs/r2dum/latest/development-of-a-2d-or-combined-1d-2d-model/creating-hydraulic-property-tables-for-2d-flow-areas)). [Likely, from an earlier project note, not re-read] The SWE-ELM friction is c_f = n²g|V|/R^(4/3), with R read from the face tables ([HEC-RAS TRM, momentum](https://www.hec.usace.army.mil/confluence/rasdocs/ras1dtechref/latest/theoretical-basis-for-one-dimensional-and-two-dimensional-hydrodynamic-calculations/2d-unsteady-flow-hydrodynamics/hydraulic-equations/momentum-conservation)). Whether SWE-EM, the solver these runs used, takes the same R was not re-read. The emulation below suggests it does. **HEC-RAS carries wall shear, but only as one section per face.**

[Certain] Wadi2D's face conveyance is K = Σ (1/n) d^(5/3) dy on plan width. This is the divided-channel (Lotter) sum taken to pixel strips, one step beyond Lotter because each strip's wetted perimeter is replaced by its plan width. Wadi2D is in the majority here. [Certain, read in full] SFINCS's sub-grid flux is literally the grid average of h^(5/3)/n, with no perimeter anywhere in it ([van Ormondt et al. 2025](https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/18/843/2025/)). Volp et al.'s friction depth, behind 3Di, "only depends on the subgrid depths and roughness coefficients" under a uniform friction slope ([Volp thesis, ch. 2](https://www.vliz.be/imisdocs/publications/ocrd/300563.pdf)). Rana computes 2D friction "using the flow depth at each subgrid pixel" ([Rana docs, 2D surface flow](https://docs.ranawaterintelligence.com/h_surfaceflow.html)). [Certain, release notes read] TUFLOW HPC's SGS default is a resistance radius A/W, "traditionally the approach used by 2D solvers". Its A/P option "considers side wall friction", but TUFLOW flagged it as "under-development" and said it would be checked "for any effects on time-stepping and cell size sensitivity" ([TUFLOW Release Notes 2020-01-AB](https://downloads.tuflow.com/_archive/TUFLOW/Releases/2020-01/Doc/TUFLOW%20Release%20Notes.2020-01-AB.pdf)). Its pixel-sum SGS Materials beta supported the resistance radius only ([TUFLOW Release Notes 2020-10-AD](https://downloads.tuflow.com/TUFLOW/Releases/2020-10/TUFLOW%20Release%20Notes.2020-10-AD.pdf)). [Certain, schema read] D-Flow FM offers R = A/P as `Conveyance2D = 1`, with the face depth as its default ([Deltares HYDROLIB-core](https://github.com/Deltares/HYDROLIB-core/blob/main/hydrolib/core/dflowfm/mdu/models.py)).

[Certain, arithmetic on the exact section] Each documented form, integrated across the gorge at one water level, gives its own normal depth:

[Certain, Wadi2D's own arithmetic] The emulation cuts the section into faces of width w, gives each face its own A/P from its own profile, and sums the conveyances. It gives 5.42 m at w = 20 and 40 m, 4.73 m at 5 m, 4.46 m at 2 m and 4.41 m at 1 m, with face ends at the axis. [Likely] That lands within 0.15 m of HEC-RAS's 5.28 m at 20 m and within 0.03 m of its 4.70 m at 5 m. The mechanism is visible in the numbers. A face that spans the gorge charges both walls' perimeter to the whole section, which is the single-section answer. Faces that divide the section see R ≈ h in the middle, and only the wall faces carry the walls. [Certain, arithmetic] The same rule is also sensitive to face alignment. Shifting the faces by half a face width moves the 10 m answer from 5.42 to 4.74 m and the 5 m answer from 4.73 to 5.11 m. That is an 8 to 13 % swing from where the face ends happen to fall relative to the walls. [Certain, read in full] HEC never says that results depend on cell size through this. Its nearest words are a request for "5 to 7 (or more) cells across the channel" and a description of coarse cells moving water "in a 1D-type of mode" ([HEC-RAS 2D User's Manual](https://www.hec.usace.army.mil/confluence/rasdocs/r2dum/latest/development-of-a-2d-or-combined-1d-2d-model/creating-hydraulic-property-tables-for-2d-flow-areas)). [Likely] TUFLOW is the only vendor that named the risk in writing, and the notes found no published outcome.

## The physical depth sits near 5.3 m, a fifth above Wadi2D's

The three research notes appear to disagree. The session's own reading says HEC-RAS's extra depth is a coarse-face artefact, which at fine resolution should fall toward the bed-only sum plus the walls' slant length (4.41 to 4.69 m at 1 to 2 m faces). The literature note says the physical answer is 5.3 to 5.4 m. The evidence note brackets it between 4.8 and 5.4 m. The measured evidence decides between them, and it is thin but one-directional.

[Certain, read in full] In smooth rectangular flumes the walls carry 69 % of the boundary shear at b/h 1.16, 38 % at 2.9 and 36 % at 3.2, against perimeter shares of 63, 41 and 39 %. At b/h 5.3 to 6.0 they carry only 18 to 19 % (these rows were realigned from a garbled table, so [Likely]), against perimeter shares of 25 to 27 % ([Tavakkol and Zarrati 2018](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1611.01608)). Up to b/h ≈ 3, then, the walls carry 0.93 to 1.1 of their perimeter share, which is what single-section Manning implies. [Certain, geometry] The gorge's walls make up 43 to 48 % of the wetted perimeter at b/h 2.2 to 2.7, and the strip sum gives them 0 %. [Likely, abstract only] Guo states that the depth method and the hydraulic-radius method "set the upper and lower bounds for bed-shear stress" ([Guo 2015](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/abs/10.1080/00221686.2014.971449)). The strip sum sits at the extreme: the upper bound on every bed strip and zero on the walls. [Likely, abstract only] The one direct laboratory test of a depth-averaged 2D model points the same way. With bed friction only and free-slip walls, "the 2D numerical solution does not coincide with the experimental data unless the 2D friction slope equation is modified to account for sidewall friction" ([Molls, Zhao and Molls 1998](https://ascelibrary.org/doi/10.1061/%28ASCE%290733-9429%281998%29124%3A1%2881%29)). Its error size and aspect ratio sat behind the paywall. [Certain, read in full] A 1D worked example at B/h 2.7 (Q 12 m³/s, B 4 m, n 0.014, S 0.001) gives 1.48 m with the walls in the perimeter and 1.19 m without them, a ratio of 0.80. The gorge's 4.40 against 5.39 m is 0.82 ([Kleinschmidt, Vertical Walls](https://therassolution.kleinschmidtgroup.com/ras-post/vertical-walls-what-are-the-implications/)). **Wadi2D's face sum is numerically the textbook "no wall perimeter" error, at the same aspect ratio and the same size.**

[Certain, read in full] Spada et al.'s local hydraulic radius method is the one published closure calibrated to sit between the divided channel and single-section Manning. It takes each vertical's hydraulic radius as depth over wetted boundary length inside a window of β local depths. It reduces to the divided channel as β → 0 and to single-section Manning as β → ∞. It was calibrated at β = 9 on the HR Wallingford flood channel facility, with under 2 % discharge variability for any one-digit β ([Spada et al. 2015](https://hess.copernicus.org/preprints/12/2607/2015/hessd-12-2607-2015.pdf)). On Knight and Demetriou's series, all with a vertical main-channel wall, it scored a Nash-Sutcliffe of 0.96 to 0.997, against −14.5 to 0.29 for the divided channel. The K1 series is a plain smooth rectangle. [Guessing] How Spada et al. subdivided that rectangle for the divided channel is not stated in the text read, so the −14.5 should not be quoted as "the divided channel on a rectangle". [Likely, the shape function was rebuilt from garbled text] On the gorge the method gives 4.52 m at β → 0, 4.92 at β = 1, 5.22 at β = 3, **5.34 at β = 9** and 5.388 at β = 1000. Its two limits reproduce the slant-length sum and single-section Manning, which checks the implementation. [Likely] The decomposition matters for the remedy. The walls' slant length is worth only 0.1 m. The other 0.85 to 0.9 m is the walls' drag carried into the core by lateral turbulence and secondary currents. A divided channel leaves that out by construction, and no pixel-level fix can recover it.

[Likely, my arithmetic on the notes' numbers] The evidence note's 4.8 m lower bound rests on a misread variable. It came from a wall-share relation, %SF_w = 103.23 (1 + 2b/3h)^−1.4128, extracted from HTML with the exponent's sign reconstructed and attributed to a closed-duct paper ([PLOS ONE 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7145149/)). Read with b as the full width, it gives 26 % on the gorge. Read the same way, though, it gives 23 % at b/h 2.9 and 46 % at 1.16, where the measured shares are 38 and 69 %. Read with b as the half-width, the same relation gives 40, 37, 65 and 24 % against measured 38, 36, 69 and 19 %, and 42 to 48 % on the gorge. [Likely in form] Feed that share into the evidence note's own uniform-flow relation, h ≈ h_bed-only (1 − f_w)^−0.3, and iterate to self-consistency: it returns **5.31 m**. [Likely] The three notes then reconcile. The session note was right about mechanism: HEC-RAS reaches 5.28 m by lumping, a rule that neither converges nor ignores face alignment. It never claimed 4.40 m was physical; it left the physics open. The literature note was right about value. The evidence note's bracket shrinks to 5.2 to 5.4 m once its lower input is corrected. The best estimate is **about 5.3 m**, and the strip sum's 4.40 m is the one value the flume data exclude.

[Certain, from the notes' reading logs] Every shear-split dataset found is a smooth flume with equal roughness on bed and walls. No data were found for rock walls at n 0.05, for walls rougher than the bed, or for a natural gorge. [Likely] The 5.3 m also holds only if the n of 0.05 means a boundary roughness applied uniformly over bed and walls, which is how 1D tables and hand calculation use their n values. [Likely, snippet or abstract only] The practitioner rule that 2D n should be 15 to 34 % below 1D n comes from floodplain rivers, where 1D folds bend, expansion and exchange losses into n ([CSUS Napa River thesis](https://scholars.csus.edu/esploro/outputs/graduate/A-comparison-of-1D-and-2D/99257831153901671); [SurfCity H2O](https://surfcityh2o.com/selecting-mannings-n-values-for-2d-hec-ras-models/)). In a straight gorge the only loss a bed-only 2D model lacks is the walls, so the correction runs the other way. [Certain, page read] TUFLOW's wiki says that because 2D schemes apply no side-wall friction, they "may require a slightly lower Manning's n". [Likely] The physics requires a higher n, so the wiki sentence reads as a slip and is evidence for neither side ([TUFLOW wiki, FMA Challenge 2](https://wiki.tuflow.com/index.php?title=FMA_Challenge_2)). [Likely, abstract only] Flash-flood reconstructions add that tabulated n under-states resistance in steep confined channels even in 1D ([Lumbroso and Gaume 2012](https://eprints.hrwallingford.com/876/)), so a bed-only 2D model given table n errs further in the same direction.

The table is therefore the case of near-vertical rock walls, the gorge's case. [Certain, measured in Wadi2D's Faydh 01 runs] Faydh's main stem gives the scale on real ground. Where the aligned mesh stood low, about 20 m of water ran 4.6 to 4.8 m deep, so B/h ≈ 4.2. The tight but wide reaches run 32 to 35 m wide and 2.9 to 3.2 m deep, so B/h ≈ 11. [Likely] By the table, the bed-only face reads the gorge reaches 10 to 14 % shallow, the wide reaches 3 to 7 %, and a braided plain wadi with B/h far above 50 under 2 %. [Likely] Hill wadis leave the 2D domain in mode (b), so for design flows the bias concentrates where mode (b) keeps 2D cells in a gorge. It applies in full to mode (c) and to rain-on-grid runs routed through gorges, the race among them. [Guessing] The gorge cells mode (b) keeps are often the dam axes themselves. [Guessing] On a peak, extra resistance in the gorges should slow the wave and attenuate it more, so a bed-only model would read gorge-routed peaks somewhat high and stages low. No run in the notes measures that.

[Likely] The published validation of sub-grid friction cannot see any of this. SFINCS's straight test is a 100 m wide channel with 1:5 sides, within 2 % of Manning from 5 to 500 m cells ([van Ormondt et al. 2025](https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/18/843/2025/)). TUFLOW's orientation test is a 100 m wide rectangle, and its bend test a 1.7 m shallow flume ([Kitts et al. 2020](https://tuflow.com/media/5022/2020-mesh-orientation-and-cell-size-sensitivity-in-2d-swe-solvers-kitts-et-al-iahr-river-flow-delft.pdf)). Every such case sits at B/h far above 15, where the table above puts the bias at a few per cent. [Likely, read in full] The closures aim to reproduce a fine-grid depth-averaged solution, and that reference carries the same omission, so it is structural and not argued anywhere.

## Wall shear is common to every Wadi2D mesh, so it cannot make the race excess

[Certain, measured] On the straight gorge Wadi2D reads 4.407 m on 2 m squares, 4.382 m on 5 m, 4.383 m on 20 m, and 4.407 and 4.408 m on aligned 20 and 10 m cells. The missing wall shear is common-mode across generators and cell sizes, so it cannot produce a difference between aligned and square meshes. [Likely, from the same day's runs] The aligned pair's deficit on the tight sine (4.57 m at 20 m and 4.53 at 10 m, against 4.81 m on 2 m squares) is the bends' resistance that two cells across a narrow channel cannot carry. HEC-RAS shows it too: its aligned 20 m mesh rises 0.02 m from straight to sine, while its 5 m squares rise 0.62 m.

[Certain, arithmetic on the runs] The friction finding does bear on how the aligned-versus-square question is refereed. On the tight sine, HEC-RAS's aligned 20 m mesh reads 5.287 m against 5.319 m on its own 5 m squares, an apparent match. On the straight gorge, the same 20 m construction stands 0.57 to 0.58 m above the 5 m squares, all of it coarse-face wall lumping, and on the sine it misses about 0.60 m of bend resistance. The match is those two errors cancelling. **HEC-RAS cannot adjudicate meshes in a narrow gorge without first removing its own cell-size-dependent friction**, and the same holds for any code with per-face A/P. [Likely, by the emulation's logic] The same trap applies to Wadi2D's own remedy. A per-face A/P in Wadi2D would treat a 20 m square face spanning the gorge as the single section, while the aligned pair's cross faces each span half the gorge and one wall. On a straight channel the meshes would then read differently, which is exactly the confound the race must avoid. Any wall term has to be independent of face width. [Guessing] Whether wall shear changes the bend rise is open: the bend added 0.62 m in HEC-RAS at 5 m and 0.40 m in Wadi2D at 2 m. Nothing in the notes separates bend loss from wall loss.

[Likely] The HEC-RAS 2.5 m run of the straight gorge decides the convergence question. The emulation predicts 4.46 to 4.69 m at 2 m faces, depending on where the face ends fall, and 4.49 m as the per-pixel limit. A 2.5 m result below about 4.6 m would confirm that HEC-RAS converges toward Wadi2D's bed-only answer plus the walls' slant length. That would make its 5.28 m a coarse-face artefact that happens to sit near the physics. A result at or above 4.7 m would mean something beyond the per-face radius holds HEC-RAS up, such as eddy viscosity or a wall treatment the emulation omits, and the artefact reading would need revisiting.

[Likely] I would build the local-hydraulic-radius table, behind a switch, off by default. It is the only remedy the evidence supports that meets both requirements at once. It carries the lateral exchange that accounts for nine tenths of the missing depth, and it does not depend on face width, which the aligned-versus-square work needs above everything else. It also leaves Wadi2D's validated behaviour alone where that behaviour is right, because on a wide flat bed the window sees only bed and returns today's sum. [Certain, from the notes] Its acceptance cannot come from the literature, because nothing published applies such a window inside a 2D sub-grid face. The order follows from that. First, read the HEC-RAS 2.5 m result, which costs nothing and settles whether HEC-RAS's coarse answer is an artefact. Second, prototype the table and pass the mesh-independence and wide-channel tests, which are the code's own business. Third, with the owner's permission to obtain them, run the Molls et al. paper and Knight and Demetriou's data, the only measured stage evidence named in the notes. The default changes only after that and on his word. If the flume data cannot be had, the honest interim is the first option, a stated B/h rule, rather than a calibrated n that would be right at one flow. I would reject HEC-RAS's per-face radius outright: it would import the very cell-size and alignment dependence this report traces in HEC-RAS.

## Conclusion

[Likely] The finding that changes the picture is that refinement moves HEC-RAS away from the physics in a narrow gorge. HEC's own guidance asks for five to seven cells across the channel, about 2 m in this 12 m gorge, and on the emulation that setting carries HEC-RAS to 4.5 to 4.7 m, close to Wadi2D's bed-only answer. Its coarsest mesh is its most physical one, only because a single face spanning the gorge happens to be single-section Manning. [Likely] A depth-averaged model with sub-grid faces has no native place for wall drag. Every sub-grid code either leaves it out, as Wadi2D, SFINCS, 3Di and TUFLOW's default do, or ties it to the face width, as HEC-RAS does, and neither family's published validation runs in channels narrow enough to tell.

[Likely] For Wadi2D the friction question and the race have now come apart cleanly. The race is a mesher question about cells across tight bends. Wall shear is a solver question about absolute stage in incised reaches, and B/h sets its size, not the mesh. [Guessing] Its stakes sit where Batinah design work is most exposed: dam axes in gorges, where a stage read 10 to 20 % low feeds tailwater, pool and spillway levels. That argues for running the narrow-channel flume test alongside the face-depth and race tests, rather than after them.

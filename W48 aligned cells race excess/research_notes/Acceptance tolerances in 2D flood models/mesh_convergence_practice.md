# How mesh / cell-size convergence is judged for 2D flood models (practice and literature)

Research notes, 2026-11-08. Scope: the criteria used to decide that a 2D flood model's mesh is fine enough, or that a coarse mesh or a new mesh type is acceptable against a reference. Each finding is labelled **[Result]** (a number someone reported) or **[Criterion]** (a threshold someone stated as acceptable), so the two are not confused. Several primary PDFs could not be fetched (HTTP 403 or 404). Those items are in Gaps rather than guessed.

## 1. What do practitioners and papers use as the reference: finest mesh, Richardson-extrapolated answer, analytic solution, or observed data?

### Takeaway
In flood-model practice the reference is almost always **the finest mesh that could be run**, with analytic solutions used for idealised channels and observations used for calibration. Richardson extrapolation (the GCI route) is standard in CFD but **I found no 2D flood study in this search that uses an extrapolated answer as its reference**, and one recent HEC-RAS / TUFLOW comparison says so explicitly. The vendor guidance (TUFLOW) adds that **converged does not mean correct**.

### Cited Findings
- [Criterion, procedure] The TUFLOW manual calls cell-size convergence testing "the most robust way" to verify a 2D cell size. The procedure is to reduce the cell size progressively and compare results, aiming "to identify the largest cell size possible to achieve a consistent simulation result". It gives four comparison methods: overlaid flood extents; flows across primary paths over time; a key result plotted against cell size, where "the largest cell size for which the error is within some defined tolerance may then be considered sufficiently converged"; and a histogram of many results against the finest feasible cell size. In the extent method you "keep halving the cell size until the results become acceptably similar", and "the 2nd to last (i.e. penultimate) cell size is then the one that may be considered as sufficiently converged". The reference is therefore the finest run. — [TUFLOW Classic/HPC Manual 2026.0, §3.2 Model Resolution](https://docs.tuflow.com/classic-hpc/manual/2026.0/ModelResolutionDiscretisation-2.html)
- [Result] TUFLOW's 2018 FMA presentation (Huxley) ran the UK EA Test 5 valley at 10, 20, 50, 100, 150, 200 and 250 m, and an urban direct-rainfall catchment at 3 to 23 m. In each case the **finest run was the "baseline" / "target result for comparison"**. Convergence was "observed when ≥ 5 grid elements laterally across the valley". The closing slide reads: "Does mesh convergence guarantee model results will be correct? NO! All models should be calibrated to historic events." — [Huxley, TUFLOW, 2D Model Design Fundamentals, FMA 2018](https://tuflow.com/media/4968/201809-2d-model-design-fundamentals-what-2d-mesh-resolution-is-necessary-fma-nevada-usa.pdf)
- [Result] David & Schmalz (Water 13, 2346, 2021; HEC-RAS rain-on-grid, Messbach catchment of 2.13 km², Germany) evaluated every configuration "against the high-resolution benchmark run with 1 m mesh resolution and 0.25 m underlying subgrid". — [David & Schmalz 2021, Water](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/water/water-13-02346/article_deploy/water-13-02346.pdf)
- [Result] The SFINCS sub-grid paper (van Ormondt, Leijnse, de Goede, Nederhoff, van Dongeren) used three kinds of reference. For conceptual straight and meandering channels at 5 to 500 m it used **the analytical uniform-flow discharge**. For the St. Johns River tide and the Hurricane Harvey pluvial case it used **observations** (11 tide stations; USGS gauges and high-water marks), not a fine-grid run. — [van Ormondt et al., EGUsphere preprint 2024-1839](https://egusphere.copernicus.org/preprints/2024/egusphere-2024-1839/egusphere-2024-1839.pdf)
- [Result] Kitts, Syme, Gao, Collecutt and Ryan (River Flow 2020) used Manning's uniform-flow solution for a 100 m wide channel at 5, 10, 25 and 50 m cells, and measured flume levels (De Vriend 1978 U-bend) at 0.05, 0.10, 0.17 and 0.34 m cells. — [Kitts et al. 2020, IAHR River Flow](https://tuflow.com/media/5022/2020-mesh-orientation-and-cell-size-sensitivity-in-2d-swe-solvers-kitts-et-al-iahr-river-flow-delft.pdf)
- [Result] A 2026 calibrated HEC-RAS / SMS-TUFLOW Classic comparison states that its mesh tests were not a strict asymptotic-convergence analysis. No grid finer than 15 ft was run, and neither Richardson extrapolation nor a GCI was applied, so it reports "relative stability" over 15 to 55 ft rather than mesh independence. (From the search-result summary; the page itself returned 403.) — [MDPI Sci 8(8):219, 2026](https://www.mdpi.com/2413-4155/8/8/219)
- [Result] Porosity and upscaled shallow-water papers compare the coarse model against a high-resolution reference simulation of the classical equations. Examples: Özgen, Liang and Hinkelmann (Appl. Math. Model. 2016) report "good agreement" with a high-resolution reference at about three orders of magnitude less run time. Guinot et al. compute "a reference solution … by a high-resolution shallow water model on a 1 m by 1 m structured computational grid". — [Bruwier et al. search summary, ORBi](https://orbi.uliege.be/handle/2268/214640); [Guinot et al., escholarship](https://escholarship.org/content/qt7b8336tr/qt7b8336tr_noSplash_b2c4df1e72accffb988f70e5a505929b.pdf)
- [Result] Horritt & Bates (J. Hydrol. 253, 2001, LISFLOOD-FP, River Severn) judged resolution against **observed** inundated area. Performance peaked at 100 m for extent. Secondary summaries add that 500 m was adequate for water levels, while flood-wave travel time stayed strongly resolution-dependent. — [Univ. Bristol record](https://research-information.bris.ac.uk/en/publications/effects-of-spatial-resolution-on-a-raster-based-model-of-flood-fl/)

### Inferences
- [Likely] "The finest mesh run" is the de facto reference in flood practice. That treats the finest run's own discretisation error as zero, so a coarse mesh can look converged against a fine run that is itself still drifting. The Wadi2D comparison (aligned 20 m against squares 20 m) is weaker still. Its reference is a mesh of the **same** size, so the "within 3 %" rule measures the **difference of two discretisation errors**, not the error of either mesh.
- [Likely] Analytic cases (uniform flow, Ritter, MacDonald) are the only references free of discretisation error. Observed data carry model-form and input error, and fine runs carry their own residual discretisation error. Published practice uses all three, but for different questions.

### Gaps
- I found no flood-modelling paper or guideline that names the Richardson-extrapolated value as the reference for judging a coarse or alternative mesh. This may exist in the dam-break or CFD-hydraulics literature (Hardy, Lane et al. used GCI for 3D open-channel CFD), but nothing surfaced in this search.

## 2. Has the Grid Convergence Index (Roache 1994/1998; ASME V&V 20; Celik et al. 2008) been applied to 2D flood or shallow-water models, and what GCI values were deemed acceptable?

### Takeaway
The GCI method is well defined: Fs = 1.25 for three or more grids and 3.0 for two, refinement ratio r > 1.3 (Celik) or ≥ 1.1 (NPARC), plus an asymptotic-range check. However, **neither the method's authors nor the sources found here state an acceptable GCI value**: the GCI is an error band, not a pass mark. **I found no 2D flood study that applies a GCI and states what value it accepts.** The 2026 HEC-RAS / TUFLOW comparison explicitly did not apply one, and a grid-convergence study of the integral-porosity SWE (Özgen-Xian et al. 2018) is the closest shallow-water example found.

### Cited Findings
- [Method] Roache introduced the GCI in "Perspective: A Method for Uniform Reporting of Grid Refinement Studies", J. Fluids Eng. 116, 1994. — [sciencegate record, DOI 10.1115/1.2910291](https://next.sciencegate.app/document/10.1115/1.2910291)
- [Method] NASA NPARC's tutorial on Roache's method gives Fs = 3.0 for two-grid comparisons and 1.25 for three or more grids. It says "each grid level [must] yield solutions that are in the asymptotic range", checked by "observing two GCI values as computed over three grids" (GCI23 ≈ r^p GCI12; the worked example gives a ratio of about 1.002). It gives a minimum refinement ratio r ≥ 1.1 and allows non-integer ratios because halving "may put the solution out of the asymptotic range". **No numeric acceptance threshold is given**, only that "a small value of GCI indicates that the computation is within the asymptotic range". — [NASA GRC NPARC, Examining Spatial (Grid) Convergence](https://www.grc.nasa.gov/www/wind/valid/tutorial/spatconv.html)
- [Method] Celik, Ghia, Roache, Freitas, Coleman and Raad, "Procedure for estimation and reporting of uncertainty due to discretization in CFD applications", J. Fluids Eng. 130(7), 2008 (DOI 10.1115/1.2960953). It is a five-step procedure: observed order p, extrapolated value, approximate relative error, and fine-grid GCI = 1.25·e_a/(r^p − 1). It recommends r > 1.3, a value "based on experience and not on formal derivation". Iterative convergence must be shown first, and a negative observed order signals oscillatory convergence. The procedure "cannot possibly encompass all possible scenarios" and does not account for modelling errors. (Details from secondary summaries; the ASME text was not fetched.) — [Celik et al. 2008 via scribd copy](https://es.scribd.com/document/288804583/Procedure-for-Estimation-and-Reporting-of-Uncertainty-Due-to-Discretization-in-CFD-Applications); [Volupe GCI calculator summary](https://volupe.com/support/grid-convergence-index-calculator/)
- [Result] Özgen-Xian, Liang and Hinkelmann (2018) ran a grid-convergence study of the integral-porosity shallow-water model on unstructured triangles. Results converged to the conventional SWE results where non-hydrostatic effects were absent, but capturing those effects needed resolutions similar to the conventional model. (No GCI value surfaced in the snippet.) — [EasyChair, A Grid Convergence Study for the Integral Porosity SWM](https://easychair.org/publications/paper/XCvj)
- [Result, outside flood work] A landslide-tsunami study reports the GCI falling from 17.18 % to 1.94 % with mesh refinement. It illustrates magnitudes, not an acceptance rule. — [ResearchGate table](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Grid-convergence-index-GCI-calculation_tbl1_336125702)
- [Result] The 2026 HEC-RAS / SMS-TUFLOW paper applied "neither Richardson extrapolation nor a GCI" and calls its result relative stability, not mesh independence (search-result summary). — [MDPI Sci 8(8):219](https://www.mdpi.com/2413-4155/8/8/219)

### Inferences
- [Likely] A worked GCI on the project's own Faydh 01 numbers, the dam-axis peak on squares as recorded in the project state file, is 1,835 (20 m), 1,871 (10 m) and 1,887 m3/s (5 m), with r = 2.
  - The error ratio is e32/e21 = 36/16 = 2.25, so convergence is monotone and p = ln 2.25 / ln 2 = 1.17.
  - The extrapolated peak is 1,887 + 16/1.25 ≈ **1,900 m3/s**.
  - GCI at 5 m = 1.25 × (16/1,887)/1.25 = **0.85 %**; at 10 m = **1.9 %**.
  - Errors against the extrapolated value: 20 m squares **−3.4 %**, 10 m −1.5 %, 5 m −0.7 %.
  - With three grids and p computed from the same three grids, the asymptotic check GCI23/(r^p GCI12) reduces to f1/f2 ≈ 1.009, so it says nothing new. A fourth grid would be needed for a real check.
  - The brief's "3.6 % below the converged (5 m) answer" does not match these figures (2.8 % against 5 m, 3.4 % against the extrapolation). The numbers should be re-read from the run record before this is quoted.
- [Likely] The aligned (centre) mesh has only two grids: 1,987 (20 m) and 1,919 m3/s (10 m). With two grids the GCI needs Fs = 3 and an assumed order, and the extrapolated answer depends strongly on that order:
  - p = 1: extrapolates to 1,851; GCI(10 m) = 10.6 %.
  - p = 1.17, borrowed from the squares: extrapolates to 1,865; GCI = 8.5 %.
  - p = 2: extrapolates to 1,896; GCI = 3.5 %.

  Only p ≈ 2 brings the aligned series to the same limit as the squares (about 1,900). Against 1,900 the aligned 20 m reads **+4.6 %** and the squares 20 m **−3.4 %**. The "+8.3 % against the squares" is these two errors added, on opposite sides of the converged value.
- [Guessing] The 5 m centre mesh is recorded as broken, so a third aligned grid at r = 2 is not available. Celik's r > 1.3 allows a non-integer series instead, for example about 28, 20 and 14 m (r ≈ 1.41), or 40, 20 and 10 m. That would give the aligned mesh its own observed order and its own extrapolated limit, which would show whether both constructions converge to the same answer.

### Gaps
- I found no 2D flood or shallow-water paper that reports a GCI on peak level or discharge together with an accepted GCI value. I also found no flood-specific statement of what GCI is acceptable. The CFD sources give none either (NPARC: "a small value"). **No consensus threshold exists in the material found.**
- ASME V&V 20-2009 was not retrieved. Its specific wording (validation uncertainty u_val combining numerical, input and data uncertainty) is not quoted here.

## 3. Published mesh sensitivity studies for 2D flood models (HEC-RAS 2D, TUFLOW, LISFLOOD-FP, Iber, sub-grid models): what changes in peak depth, level, discharge or extent were reported as acceptable or "converged"?

### Takeaway
Reported changes span a wide range: a few centimetres in confined channels at adequate resolution, decimetres to more than 100 % of depth for shallow sheet flow on coarse cells. Peak flow is typically less mesh-sensitive than arrival time and extent. **The only explicit numeric acceptance criteria found are study-specific**: David & Schmalz's thresholds (NSE ≥ 0.8, ΔWSE 0.03 / 0.05, volume deficit 5 %) and TUFLOW's illustrative "95 % of results within a tolerance". Most papers write "acceptable" or "converged" without a number.

### Cited Findings
- [Result] David & Schmalz 2021 (HEC-RAS rain-on-grid; benchmark 1 m mesh on a 0.25 m subgrid):
  - In the stream, the maximum water-level deviation was 0 m up to 5 m mesh for DEMs of 0.25 to 4 m, and 0.03 m on the 5 m DEM. It rose from **0.1 m at 10 m to 0.6 m at 30 m** mesh, at a benchmark depth of 0.43 m. At 20 to 30 m "the deviations are over 100% of the total water depth".
  - On the village road (benchmark depth 0.10 m), fine meshes deviated 0.003 to 0.037 m and 3 to 5 m meshes about 0.03 m. Coarser meshes deviated up to 0.66 m (10 m mesh, 1 m DEM).
  - For their catchment they recommend a 3 to 5 m mesh on a 0.25 to 1 m DEM.

  — [David & Schmalz 2021](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/water/water-13-02346/article_deploy/water-13-02346.pdf)
- [Criterion] The same paper filtered configurations with thresholds "set in such a way that they contain an acceptable deviation from the benchmark run": NSE (village and outlet) = 0.8, ΔWSE_road = 0.03, ΔWSE_stream = 0.05, volume deficit VD = 5 %, volume balance VB = 95 %, flooded-area fit F2 = 0.28. F2 "had to be reduced due to the poor agreement with the benchmark". **The paper prints the ΔWSE thresholds as "cm", but its results are reported in metres (0.03 m), so metres is probably meant. Verify before quoting.** — [David & Schmalz 2021](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/water/water-13-02346/article_deploy/water-13-02346.pdf)
- [Result] The same paper found that Manning's n ±10 % moved the peak discharge by about 5 to 8 %, and ±20 % by about 10 to 20 %. The parameter band is therefore comparable to or larger than the mesh band at adequate resolution. — [David & Schmalz 2021](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/water/water-13-02346/article_deploy/water-13-02346.pdf)
- [Result] TUFLOW HPC: the EA Test 5 valley converged at ≥ 5 elements across the valley. Without enough cells across, a bend's super-elevation (outside 4.4 m against inside 3.7 m in the slide's example) is not reproduced, because "each 2D cell only calculates a single velocity per cell face". For the urban case the recommendation is under 6 m. — [Huxley, TUFLOW FMA 2018](https://tuflow.com/media/4968/201809-2d-model-design-fundamentals-what-2d-mesh-resolution-is-necessary-fma-nevada-usa.pdf)
- [Result] TUFLOW manual worked example: 40 m is consistent and 60 m and coarser are not. The judgement is stated to be "subjective" and to depend on "the purpose of the modelling and specifically what the 'essential results' are". — [TUFLOW Manual 2026.0 §3.2](https://docs.tuflow.com/classic-hpc/manual/2026.0/ModelResolutionDiscretisation-2.html)
- [Result] Kitts et al. 2020 (TUFLOW HPC):
  - Without sub-grid sampling, a misaligned 100 m wide channel departs further from Manning as cells coarsen (5 to 50 m). With SGS it shows "excellent convergence to theoretical water level … for the full range of cell sizes".
  - In the U-bend flume, without SGS "none of the cell sizes reproduced the measurements", all overpredicting upstream level. With SGS the results "correlated well … for all cell sizes".
  - The conclusion speaks of "acceptable changes in results" with SGS, against "sometimes unacceptable" ones without, **with no number given**.

  — [Kitts et al. 2020](https://tuflow.com/media/5022/2020-mesh-orientation-and-cell-size-sensitivity-in-2d-swe-solvers-kitts-et-al-iahr-river-flow-delft.pdf)
- [Result] Shustikova et al. (Hydrol. Sci. J., 2019), HEC-RAS against LISFLOOD-FP on complex topography: grids of 25 to 100 m over a 1 m sub-grid. A 100 m mesh with sub-grid terrain gave acceptable accuracy at much lower run time, judged against observations (search-result summary; the page returned 403). — [Shustikova et al. 2019, HSJ](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02626667.2019.1671982)
- [Result] Teton Dam (1976) in GeoClaw and HEC-RAS: the mesh controlled **peak arrival time** but produced similar **peak flows** (search-result summary). — [arXiv 2206.00766](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.00766)
- [Result] Bomers, Schielen and Hulscher (Env. Fluid Mech. 19:1273–1294, 2019; D-Flow FM, river Waal) found the spread in simulated water levels across grid shapes and sizes "rather large". They attribute it to bathymetry accuracy and numerical friction (grid size) and numerical viscosity (grid shape), and judge a hybrid curvilinear-channel / triangular-floodplain grid practical. — [Springer, DOI 10.1007/s10652-019-09670-4](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s10652-019-09670-4)
- [Result] Horritt & Bates 2001 (LISFLOOD-FP): extent performance peaks at 100 m, water levels adequate at 500 m, travel times "strongly dependent on model resolution". — [Bristol record](https://research-information.bris.ac.uk/en/publications/effects-of-spatial-resolution-on-a-raster-based-model-of-flood-fl/)
- [Opinion, practitioner] Kleinschmidt's RAS Solution blog: HEC-RAS is "fairly insensitive to grid size in general when the grids are properly aligned with key terrain", with little difference over a reasonable range of sizes for a dam breach. This is anecdotal, not a convergence study. — [Kleinschmidt, HEC-RAS Subgrid Bathymetry](https://therassolution.kleinschmidtgroup.com/ras-post/hec-ras-subgrid-bathymetry-theory-and-application/)
- [Result] The 2026 HEC-RAS / SMS-TUFLOW comparison found HEC-RAS showed stronger depth convergence than TUFLOW Classic over 15 to 55 ft, with extent most mesh-sensitive for the frequent, low-magnitude event (search-result summary). — [MDPI Sci 8(8):219](https://www.mdpi.com/2413-4155/8/8/219)

### Inferences
- [Likely] For a confined wadi peak, the comparable published numbers are the TUFLOW "≥ 5 cells across" rule and the HEC-RAS/TUFLOW observation that peaks move less than timing. The Wadi2D finding is that the 20 m aligned pair has only two cells across a 20 m wide, 4.6 m deep bend channel. That sits below every cells-across rule found: TUFLOW HPC 3 to 4 with SGS, 6 to 8 without, and ARR ≥ 5. So a resolution-dependent excess there is consistent with published practice, not an anomaly.
- [Likely] The published tolerances that exist are absolute level differences of a few centimetres in channels: David & Schmalz 0.03 to 0.05 m; sub-grid papers within a few cm against observations (question 4). A percentage on peak discharge is a different measure. Through a rating of Q ∝ h^(5/3) (wide channel), 3 % on Q is about 1.8 % on depth, roughly 8 cm at 4.6 m of depth.

- Iber mesh-sensitivity numbers (for example Caviedes-Voullième et al. 2012, J. Hydrol.) were not found in this pass.

## 4. How do sub-grid model papers judge a coarse sub-grid mesh against a fine reference?

### Takeaway
Sub-grid papers mostly judge the coarse sub-grid run against **analytic uniform flow** or **observations**, and report "the same accuracy as" a finer non-sub-grid run. The cleanest number found is SFINCS sub-grid: **discharge within 2 % of the analytic value at every grid size from 5 to 500 m in a straight channel**. When the meanders are unresolved, the 500 m sub-grid run **overestimates discharge by about 51 %**, because sinuosity inside a cell is read as a wide straight channel. That failure is the closest published analogue to the Wadi2D bend deficit. Volp et al. (2013) and PRIMo (2019) state qualitative agreement. Their numbers were not retrieved.

### Cited Findings
- [Result] SFINCS sub-grid (van Ormondt et al. 2024/2025):
  - **Straight channel** (5 km long, 100 m wide, 1:5 side slopes, n = 0.02, levels +0.25 / −0.25 m) at Δx = 5, 10, 20, 50, 100, 200 and 500 m on a 1 m sub-grid. Regular models stay close to the analytic answer up to 50 m and then lose discharge. "The discharges computed by the subgrid models are within 2% of the analytical solution across all grid resolutions."
  - **Meandering channel** (sinuosity 1.32): the sub-grid models "show very good accuracy at resolutions up to 50 m". The 500 m model computes 473 m³/s, "an overestimation of the analytical discharge by ~51%". The coarse mesh schematises the channel as straight (overestimating the water-level slope), and "meanders inside a grid cell result in a larger wet fraction, which the model 'interprets' as a wide channel".

  — [van Ormondt et al., EGUsphere 2024-1839](https://egusphere.copernicus.org/preprints/2024/egusphere-2024-1839/egusphere-2024-1839.pdf)
- [Result] SFINCS sub-grid against observations:
  - **St. Johns River tide**: at 100 m, the median M2 amplitude error fell from 2.6 to 0.4 cm and the phase error from 4.1 to 2.1 degrees with sub-grid. Regular-mode overall RMSE grew from about 8 cm (25 to 100 m) to about 20 cm (1,000 m); with sub-grid it "remains stable around this value of 8 cm".
  - The table gives regular RMSE of 7.7, 7.8, 8.0, 12.0, 16.1 and 20.1 cm at 25, 50, 100, 200, 500 and 1,000 m, and 8.7 cm for sub-grid at 25 m.
  - **Harvey pluvial**: at 25 m the NSE went from 0.35 (regular) to 0.58 (sub-grid). The 100 m sub-grid run ran 35 times faster than the 25 m regular run "while maintaining a similar level of accuracy".

  — [van Ormondt et al. 2024](https://egusphere.copernicus.org/preprints/2024/egusphere-2024-1839/egusphere-2024-1839.pdf)
- [Result] The SFINCS authors cite Sehili et al. (2014): a sub-grid approach "could save an order of magnitude of computational cost without major accuracy loss" in estuarine modelling. They cite Woodruff et al. (2021): a speed-up of ADCIRC by 10 to 50 with sub-grid corrections. — [van Ormondt et al. 2024](https://egusphere.copernicus.org/preprints/2024/egusphere-2024-1839/egusphere-2024-1839.pdf)
- [Result, qualitative] Volp, van Prooijen and Stelling, "A finite volume approach for shallow water flow accounting for high-resolution bathymetry and roughness data", WRR 49:4126–4135, 2013. Sub-grid information enters by assuming a uniform flow direction and friction slope over part of each coarse cell. On two examples, "coarse-grid results approach high-resolution results at much lower computational cost" (abstract-level). — [TU Delft repository](https://repository.tudelft.nl/record/uuid:9e495f41-5233-420f-8bb1-8b109eddbaed); [Wiley abstract via Zendy](https://zendy.io/title/10.1002/wrcr.20324)
- [Result, qualitative] Sanders & Schubert, "PRIMo: Parallel raster inundation model", Adv. Water Res. 126:79–95, 2019: the shallow-water equations are solved on an upscaled grid far coarser than the raster DTM, with sub-grid recovery of fine-scale detail. Benchmarks are presented. (Abstract-level; the upscaling error metrics were not retrieved.) — [PRIMo record](https://katalog.lib.cas.cz/KNAV/EdsRecord/edsair,edsair.doi...........a8c40cc9779a5306609e3149f3833e7f)
- [Result] Bruwier et al. (J. Hydrol. 554, 2017), anisotropic porosity with cell merging on Cartesian grids: speed-ups of 10 to 100 "while the errors on water depths remain around few percent" against reference fine simulations, over five test cases. — [ORBi 2268/214640](https://orbi.uliege.be/handle/2268/214640)
- [Result] TUFLOW SGS (Kitts et al. 2020): Manning's level reproduced at 5 to 50 m on a misaligned channel, and flume levels at all four cell sizes, both judged visually against theory and measurements. — [Kitts et al. 2020](https://tuflow.com/media/5022/2020-mesh-orientation-and-cell-size-sensitivity-in-2d-swe-solvers-kitts-et-al-iahr-river-flow-delft.pdf)
- [Criterion, vendor] The TUFLOW manual on SGS: SGS "has a significant beneficial impact on cell size convergence", and "a model using SGS may meet the required convergence criteria with a cell size that is much larger". Recommended minimum cells across a flow path (Table 3.1): about 4 to 6 for Classic, 6 to 8 for HPC without SGS, 3 to 4 for HPC with SGS (terrain at least 2× finer). — [TUFLOW Manual 2026.0 §3.2](https://docs.tuflow.com/classic-hpc/manual/2026.0/ModelResolutionDiscretisation-2.html)
- [Criterion, vendor] TUFLOW wiki: higher-resolution velocity or velocity-derived outputs (such as hazard) "must be achieved by reducing the 2D cell size, not by adjusting the SGS sampling settings". — [TUFLOW Wiki, Quadtree and Sub-Grid Sampling FAQ](https://wiki.tuflow.com/Quadtree_and_Sub-Grid_Sampling_FAQ)

### Inferences
- [Likely] The sub-grid literature's "within 2 % of analytic" and "same accuracy as finer non-sub-grid" are the closest published counterparts to a "within 3 %" rule. Both are judged against an error-free or observed reference, not against another coarse mesh. A Wadi2D gate in the same spirit would compare each mesh against the analytic case or the extrapolated converged value.
- [Likely] SFINCS's meander result shows that sub-grid models fail when the planform curvature sits inside a cell: good up to 50 m in a 100 m wide channel, about +51 % at 500 m. Wadi2D's bend case is a different mechanism (two aligned cells across a channel narrower than the pair, reading level low rather than flux high), but the principle carries over. A sub-grid model is only mesh-independent while the features that set resistance or flux are resolved by cells, not by tables.

### Gaps
- Volp et al. 2013 and PRIMo 2019 coarse-against-fine error figures (cm or %) were not retrieved; the abstracts give none. Casulli (2009), Stelling (2012, 3Di quadtree) and Kennedy et al. (2019) were not checked for numbers.

## 5. Any vendor or guidance statement of a convergence threshold (TUFLOW, HEC-RAS, ARR Project 15, FHWA, EA benchmarks)?

### Takeaway
**No vendor or guideline found here gives a numeric convergence threshold.** TUFLOW explicitly calls the judgement "subjective" and purpose-dependent, and offers only a placeholder ("95 % of results within <some tolerance>") and the "penultimate halving" rule. The guidance that is numeric concerns **cells across a flow path** (ARR 2012: ≥ 5; TUFLOW: 3 to 8 depending on solver and SGS; FHWA: 5 to 7 elements per bridge span), not result tolerances.

### Cited Findings
- [Criterion] TUFLOW manual: "The decision as to whether or not the model results are 'converged' is subjective and will depend on the purpose of the modelling and specifically what the 'essential results' are." Testing should be documented, and stakeholders may need to be involved. The histogram method's example criterion is 95 % of results within a tolerance of the finest-size results, with the tolerance left as a placeholder. The Wu viscosity formulation (HPC default) is said to reduce cell-size sensitivity relative to Smagorinsky. — [TUFLOW Manual 2026.0 §3.2.4](https://docs.tuflow.com/classic-hpc/manual/2026.0/ModelResolutionDiscretisation-2.html)
- [Criterion] ARR 2012 Project 15 ("Two Dimensional Modelling in Urban and Rural Floodplains", P15/S1/009, Nov 2012), as tabulated by TUFLOW: flow in channel "≥ 5 grid/mesh elements laterally across the channel"; urban overland 2 to 5 m; floodplain 10 to 50 m. ### Inferences
- [Likely] Because no guideline fixes a number, any tolerance (3 % on peak flow, 5 cm on level) is a project decision. Under TUFLOW's own wording it should be justified by the "essential results" and documented. A defensible form for a design-flow tool is a stated tolerance on the essential result (dam-axis peak) **against the converged or extrapolated reference**, alongside a reported GCI band. The tolerance is then placed in relation to the other uncertainties: Manning ±10 % moves the peak 5 to 8 % (David & Schmalz 2021), and the Wadi2D project's own record has Manning 0.05 to 0.1 moving the Bidah peak 25 %.
- [Likely] By TUFLOW's "penultimate halving" rule, the squares on Faydh 01 would count as converged at 10 m if the tolerance is 1 % (10 m against 5 m: 0.85 %) and at 20 m only if it is about 2 % (20 m against 10 m: 1.9 %). By GCI the 20 m squares carry about 3.4 % error against the extrapolated peak.

- No numeric threshold was found in TUFLOW, HEC-RAS, ARR P15 (PDF 403), the FHWA documents (only the checklist was seen) or the EA benchmark (404). Whether ARR P15 or FHWA-HIF-19-061 state a level or flow tolerance for convergence remains unverified.
- FEMA's 2D modelling guidance and the HEC-RAS 6.x 2D User's Manual primary text were not checked.

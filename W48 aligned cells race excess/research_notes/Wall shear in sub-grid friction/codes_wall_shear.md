# Wall shear in sub-grid face friction: what production 2D codes compute at a face, and what their vendors say about it

Scope: how each production 2D flood code turns a terrain profile under a cell face (or a velocity point) into friction or conveyance, and whether shear on steep channel walls enters through a wetted perimeter or a hydraulic radius, or whether friction acts on plan width only. Read on 2026-11-08. Tags: [Certain] = read verbatim in a primary source this session; [Likely] = primary source read only through a search snippet or an earlier project note, or a secondary source; [Inference] = my reasoning or my own arithmetic, not a source.

The gorge case the parent gave, used only to read the formulas against (12 m flat bed, walls rising 7 m over 1.5 m each side, 300 m3/s, n 0.05, slope 1 %). I computed uniform-flow normal depths for each documented formula (continuous integration across the section, one water level, planar walls):

| Face friction form | Code(s) that document it | Normal depth |
|---|---|---|
| Pixel sum, K = sum (1/n) d^(5/3) dy (wide-channel, plan width, no wall shear) | SFINCS subgrid; 3Di 2D (per pixel depth); TUFLOW "SGS Materials" (2020-10 beta, disabled 2023-03); the parent's solver | 4.40 m |
| Single section with resistance radius R = A/W (flow area over flow width) | TUFLOW HPC SGS default | 4.47 m |
| Pixel sum with each pixel's own A/P (slope factor (1+s^2)^(1/3) on wall pixels) | The fine-face limit of HEC-RAS's per-face A/P [Inference] | 4.49 m |
| Single section with hydraulic radius R = A/P | HEC-RAS 2D per face (and HEC-RAS 1D main channel); TUFLOW "SGS Radius Approach == Hydraulic" (under development); D-Flow FM Conveyance2D = 1 | 5.39 m |

[Inference] The parent's solver reads 4.40 m at every cell size because 4.40 m is the analytic normal depth of its own pixel-sum formula. HEC-RAS at 20 m (5.28 m) sits 2 % under the single-section A/P answer (5.39 m), and at 5 m (4.70 m) it has moved most of the way toward the per-face limit (4.49 m). That is the cell-size drift you would expect if each face is one cross section with one A/P, so the walls' perimeter is charged to the whole section when one face spans the gorge, and only to the wall faces when several faces divide it.

## HEC-RAS 2D: how the face hydraulic radius and wetted perimeter are built from the face profile, and whether HEC documents cell-size dependence

### Takeaway
HEC-RAS treats every face as a cross section. It stores elevation against area, wetted perimeter (the arc length of the wetted ground profile, walls included) and n, and uses one hydraulic radius R = A/P per face in the conveyance (DWE) and in the friction (SWE). So wall shear enters, but it is lumped over the whole face. The documentation never says that results depend on cell size through this. Its only related guidance is 5 to 7 or more cells across a channel for 2D effects, faces aligned along the channel, and the remark that coarse cells move water through a channel "in a 1D-type of mode".

### Cited Findings
- [Certain] The 2D User's Manual page "Creating Hydraulic Property Tables for 2D Flow Areas" (the version selector shows 7.0 as "latest") says "each cell face is like a detailed cross section" and "the faces of the cells are preprocessed into tables of elevation versus area, wetted perimeter, and roughness"; "the details of the channel cross sections are contained within the cell faces". — [HEC-RAS 2D UM, hydraulic property tables](https://www.hec.usace.army.mil/confluence/rasdocs/r2dum/latest/development-of-a-2d-or-combined-1d-2d-model/creating-hydraulic-property-tables-for-2d-flow-areas)
- [Certain] The Hydraulic Reference Manual page "Subgrid Bathymetry" (latest, 7.0) defines the face area A_f(z_s) = integral of W_f(z) dz from z_b to z_s and the face wetted perimeter without porosity P_f(z_s) = integral sqrt(1 + (dx/dy)^2) dy along the wetted profile, i.e. the arc length of the ground under the water, so a steep wall contributes nearly its full height. "Porosity is applied to the face wetted perimeter similarly to the face wetted area." "From these variables other hydraulic variables such as the hydraulic radius and conveyance can be easily computed." — [HEC-RAS TRM, Subgrid Bathymetry](https://www.hec.usace.army.mil/confluence/rasdocs/ras1dtechref/latest/theoretical-basis-for-one-dimensional-and-two-dimensional-hydrodynamic-calculations/2d-unsteady-flow-hydrodynamics/subgrid-bathymetry)
- [Certain] The same TRM page gives the justification for coarse cells: "the free water surface is smoother than the bathymetry; therefore, a coarser grid can effectively be used to compute the spatial variability in free surface elevation." — [HEC-RAS TRM, Subgrid Bathymetry](https://www.hec.usace.army.mil/confluence/rasdocs/ras1dtechref/latest/theoretical-basis-for-one-dimensional-and-two-dimensional-hydrodynamic-calculations/2d-unsteady-flow-hydrodynamics/subgrid-bathymetry)
- [Likely, read in the project's note N03, not re-read this session] The Diffusion Wave solver uses the face conveyance K_k = A_k R_k^(2/3)/n_k with R = A/P from the face table. The 5.0 Reference Manual (p. 2-69) says the hydraulic radius "is obtained from the sub-grid bathymetry data. The solver must maintain a table relating the hydraulic radius to the water elevation". The SWE-ELM solver's implicit bottom friction is c_f = n^2 g |V| / R^(4/3), with R and n interpolated from the face tables at every iteration. — [HEC-RAS TRM 6.4, DWE solver](https://www.hec.usace.army.mil/confluence/rasdocs/ras1dtechref/6.4/theoretical-basis-for-one-dimensional-and-two-dimensional-hydrodynamic-calculations/2d-unsteady-flow-hydrodynamics/numerical-methods/diffusion-wave-equation-solver); [HEC-RAS 5.0 Reference Manual](https://www.hec.usace.army.mil/software/hec-ras/documentation/HEC-RAS%205.0%20Reference%20Manual.pdf); [TRM, momentum conservation](https://www.hec.usace.army.mil/confluence/rasdocs/ras1dtechref/latest/theoretical-basis-for-one-dimensional-and-two-dimensional-hydrodynamic-calculations/2d-unsteady-flow-hydrodynamics/hydraulic-equations/momentum-conservation)
- [Likely, N03, from pyHMT2D's reader] The stored face table has four columns: elevation, area, wetted perimeter, Manning's n. Conveyance is not stored; it is derived. — [pyHMT2D RAS_2D_Data.py](https://github.com/psu-efd/pyHMT2D/blob/main/pyHMT2D/Hydraulic_Models_Data/RAS_2D/RAS_2D_Data.py)
- [Certain] Face Manning's n has two methods. The default takes "a value from the Land Cover and Calibration Region at the centroid of every face". "Spatially Varied Manning's N" "computes a composite Manning's n values as a function of elevation ... in the same manner as for 1D channels", weighting each segment's n_i^(3/2) by its local wetted perimeter P_i ("P_i : local wetted perimeter corresponding to each segment along a face"); n is constant once the face is fully wet. HEC warns that "the large n value will dominate the equation resulting in a very high n value for the entire cell face" (buildings, n 0.25 to 0.5). — [HEC-RAS 2D UM, hydraulic property tables](https://www.hec.usace.army.mil/confluence/rasdocs/r2dum/latest/development-of-a-2d-or-combined-1d-2d-model/creating-hydraulic-property-tables-for-2d-flow-areas)
- [Certain] Table thinning: "Face Profile Filter Tol" reduces "the number of points that get extracted from the detailed terrain for each face", and "Face Conveyance Tol Ratio" "reduces the error in computing hydraulic properties, and therefore conveyance"; "A higher tolerance will result in fewer points in the hydraulic property tables of the cell faces, but less hydraulic accuracy". — [HEC-RAS 2D UM, Development of the 2D Computational Mesh (latest)](https://www.hec.usace.army.mil/confluence/rasdocs/r2dum/latest/development-of-a-2d-or-combined-1d-2d-model/development-of-the-2d-computational-mesh). [Likely, N03, 6.0 page] The defaults are 0.01 ft for the filter tolerances and 0.02 (2 %) for the conveyance ratio, with a midpoint point inserted wherever the conveyance departs from linear interpolation by more than 2 %. — [HEC-RAS 2D UM 6.0, mesh](https://www.hec.usace.army.mil/confluence/rasdocs/r2dum/6.0/development-of-a-2d-or-combined-1d-2d-model/development-of-the-2d-computational-mesh)
- [Certain] HEC's guidance on cells across a channel: "a cell size in that would allow 5 to 7 (or more) cells across the channel is needed"; "The smaller cell size will allow the model to capture the two dimensional flow effects within the channel itself"; with large cells "flow can move through a channel in a 1D-type of mode ... This type of model is not a detailed channel model, but it does allow water to move through the channels in a 1D fashion"; "A detailed model would align faces along the channel and use smaller cell sizes in the channel". The worked example is 500 x 500 ft cells on a 2 x 2 ft terrain. — [HEC-RAS 2D UM, hydraulic property tables](https://www.hec.usace.army.mil/confluence/rasdocs/r2dum/latest/development-of-a-2d-or-combined-1d-2d-model/creating-hydraulic-property-tables-for-2d-flow-areas)
- [Certain] The mesh page ties cell size to the water surface, not to the section: "the cell size should be based on the slope of the water surface in a given area"; "Where the water surface slope is flat and not changing rapidly, larger grid cell sizes are appropriate". It asks users to "align the faces of the 2D mesh for areas that are barriers to flow". It gives no number for how results change with cell size and no warning that one face spanning channel and walls (or channel and overbank) gets a single hydraulic radius. — [HEC-RAS 2D UM, Development of the 2D Computational Mesh (latest)](https://www.hec.usace.army.mil/confluence/rasdocs/r2dum/latest/development-of-a-2d-or-combined-1d-2d-model/development-of-the-2d-computational-mesh)
- [Likely, secondary] CivilGEO's knowledge base describes each face in the dual grid as "a virtual cross section" from which flow area, wetted perimeter, hydraulic radius and conveyance are computed, and recommends aligning cell edges along channel banks, especially perched banks. — [CivilGEO, 2D HEC-RAS modeling recommendations](https://knowledge.civilgeo.com/2d-hec-ras-modeling-recommendations); [CivilGEO, grid and subgrid computations](https://knowledge.civilgeo.com/hec-ras-2d-grid-subgrid-computations)

### Inferences
- [Inference] HEC-RAS 2D does carry wall shear, as the wetted perimeter of the face profile, but its form is "one face = one cross section = one A/P". When a single face spans the whole gorge, the walls' perimeter lowers R for the whole section, and the answer is the 1D single-section Manning answer (5.39 m for the parent's gorge; HEC-RAS 2D at 20 m reads 5.28 m). When faces divide the section, the middle faces see R ≈ h with no wall, and only the wall faces carry the wall perimeter. The model then tends toward a divided channel with a slope factor on wall pixels (4.49 m in the limit; HEC-RAS at 5 m reads 4.70 m). So HEC-RAS's cell-size dependence in the gorge follows from its documented formula, not from a defect. The direction is that coarser faces are more resistive.
- [Inference] HEC's own wording ("1D-type of mode", "not a detailed channel model", "5 to 7 (or more) cells across") is the nearest the documentation comes to admitting this dependence. It frames the coarse face as a 1D-like channel, not as a source of stage error. I found no HEC statement that quantifies the effect or that names wall shear.
- [Inference] The composite-n option weights by local wetted perimeter, exactly as in 1D. That confirms HEC thinks of the face as a 1D section in every respect, n included.

### Gaps
- No HEC page read states that face hydraulic radius makes stages depend on cell size, or gives guidance specific to steep-walled channels. Where HEC is silent it is recorded as silent, not as a denial.
- Whether 7.0 (now "latest" on Confluence) changed the face property method: the 7.0 pages read here carry the same text as the 6.x pages cited in N03; no 7.0 release note was read.
- Whether the SWE-EM solver uses R = A/P or a depth in its friction term was not re-read this session (N03 covers SWE-ELM; EM's advection uses h = volume/wetted area).
- HEC-RAS 1D's rule that the main channel is not subdivided for conveyance (only the overbanks at n breaks) is from my knowledge of the 1D Hydraulic Reference Manual, not re-read this session; it underpins the 5.39 m "1D answer" above.

## TUFLOW HPC with SGS: how the face conveyance curve is built, the radius options, and any wall or side-slope treatment

### Takeaway
TUFLOW HPC with SGS (since 2020-01) builds per-face flow-area and flow-width curves from samples along the face and uses one radius per face. The default is the resistance radius R = A/W (flow width, plan, no walls), which TUFLOW calls "traditionally the approach used by 2D solvers". An option `SGS Radius Approach == Hydraulic` (R = A/P) exists to include side-wall friction, but TUFLOW flagged it as under development, pending checks on time-stepping and cell-size sensitivity. A pixel-sum "parallel channel" conveyance (SGS Materials) was released as a 2020-10 beta with resistance radius only, then disabled in 2023-03. No reading found any current statement on steep sides.

### Cited Findings
- [Certain] Without SGS (Classic and HPC), "The cell face radius value (as used in Manning's equation) is set to the depth (i.e. this is the Resistance Radius approach, which uses the flow width rather than the wetted perimeter)." — [TUFLOW Release Notes 2020-01-AB s.3.2.2](https://downloads.tuflow.com/_archive/TUFLOW/Releases/2020-01/Doc/TUFLOW%20Release%20Notes.2020-01-AB.pdf)
- [Certain] With SGS, "The radius, as used in Manning's equation, is a non-linear function of elevation. There is a choice of utilising either the Resistance Radius or Hydraulic Radius approach via the command 'SGS Radius Approach == {Resistance} | Hydraulic'." "For Resistance Radius the radius value is equal to the flow area divided by the flow width. This is traditionally the approach used by 2D solvers and is the default setting for the TUFLOW 2020-01 release." "For Hydraulic Radius the radius value is calculated as the flow area divided by the wetted perimeter. As the Hydraulic Radius approach considers side wall friction it should be slightly more resistive than the Resistance Radius approach. Please note that the Hydraulic Radius approach has not undergone extensive testing at the time of the 2020-01-AA release and should be treated as under-development. In particular we will be checking for any effects on time-stepping and cell size sensitivity." — [TUFLOW Release Notes 2020-01-AB s.3.2.3](https://downloads.tuflow.com/_archive/TUFLOW/Releases/2020-01/Doc/TUFLOW%20Release%20Notes.2020-01-AB.pdf). The 2020-10-AD release notes repeat the same text unchanged. — [TUFLOW Release Notes 2020-10-AD](https://downloads.tuflow.com/TUFLOW/Releases/2020-10/TUFLOW%20Release%20Notes.2020-10-AD.pdf)
- [Certain] Example sampling given there: a 10 m cell with a 2 m SGS Sample Distance uses 25 points for the cell volume curve and 5 points for each face's area-elevation curve. — [TUFLOW Release Notes 2020-01-AB s.3.2.4](https://downloads.tuflow.com/_archive/TUFLOW/Releases/2020-01/Doc/TUFLOW%20Release%20Notes.2020-01-AB.pdf)
- [Certain] SGS Materials (Method C, 2020-10 beta, on by default with Method C). "The elevations and Manning's n values are sampled at the same locations along a cell face and are used to carry out a parallel channel analysis in a similar manner to that carried out for a 1D cross-section". The conveyance is K(H) = sum_i A_i d_i^(2/3) / n_i, "where A is the flow area and d is the flow depth of each sampling segment". The equivalent n is n(H) = A_total d_avg^(2/3) / K(H), with d_avg the total flow area over the wetted width. "For the radius formulation, the resistance radius is used. Support for hydraulic radius using 'SGS Radius Approach ==' ... is not yet supported." Depth-varying n uses each sample's own depth, "not the lowest elevation as is used by the other SGS methods". Update of June 2022: depth-varying n with Materials ON "can produce unexpected .flow results"; the advice is to set "SGS Materials == OFF". — [TUFLOW Release Notes 2020-10-AD s.3.7.3](https://downloads.tuflow.com/TUFLOW/Releases/2020-10/TUFLOW%20Release%20Notes.2020-10-AD.pdf) (equations read from a text extraction of the PDF, symbols partly lost; the form matches N02's reading)
- [Likely, N02 reading of the 2023-03 release notes s.2.2.4] "For the 2023-03 Release the sub-grid sampling of materials (land-use) has been disabled", pending research and benchmarking. — [project note N02, citing TUFLOW 2023-03 release notes](https://downloads.tuflow.com/TUFLOW/Releases/2020-10/TUFLOW%20Release%20Notes.2020-10-AD.pdf)
- [Certain] Current manual (2025.0, s.7.4.3): across each cell side "the flow area and flow width are calculated based on sampled elevations" to give a conveyance-versus-elevation curve, and the flow-area curve gives the depth- and width-averaged face velocity. The default is 11 samples per face, capped at 31 by default, with a hard limit of 127. SGS gives "little or no benefit" if the DEM is "coarser or similar to the 2D cell size". "Including gully lines can overestimate conveyance in these channels." Method C has been the default since 2023-03 (Method B before). Breaklines may be needed against "leaks" through levees. — [TUFLOW Classic/HPC Manual 2025.0, Section 7](https://docs.tuflow.com/classic-hpc/manual/2025.0/TwoD-Domains-1.html)
- [Certain] In the 2025.0 manual chunks read, no text mentions `SGS Radius Approach`, hydraulic radius in SGS, side-wall friction for SGS faces, or steep sides. Side-wall friction appears only for bridge-deck layered flow constrictions ("Including side wall friction ('BC' FC_Type only)"), which raise "the wetted perimeter at the cell mid-side by a factor equal to (2.*Bed_n)/FC_n". — [TUFLOW Manual 2025.0, Section 7](https://docs.tuflow.com/classic-hpc/manual/2025.0/TwoD-Domains-1.html)
- [Certain] The manual's general claim is that SGS "enables the use of much larger cell sizes without loss of hydraulic conveyance accuracy" and "results in less output sensitivity to the grid orientation". — [TUFLOW Manual 2025.0, s.7.2.3](https://docs.tuflow.com/classic-hpc/manual/2025.0/TwoD-Domains-1.html)

- [Certain] "SGS also provides an improved definition of the wetted perimeter and hydraulic radius at each cell face compared to the Traditional approach", in a passage about artificial depression storage in rain-on-grid. It is a generic statement, not a radius formula. — [Huxley, Syme, Ryan and Collecutt 2022, HWRS](https://www.tuflow.com/media/7555/2022-hydraulic-modelling-2d-cell-size-result-convergence-huxley-et-al-hwrs.pdf)
- [Likely, secondary blog, M. ### Inferences
- [Inference] TUFLOW's default face friction is a single-section resistance radius A/W: mean depth over the wetted width, plan width, no wall shear. It is not a pixel sum. For the gorge it sits close to the pixel sum (4.47 m against 4.40 m), because the A/W lumping costs little when the bed is flat. Both are far from the A/P answer (5.39 m).
- [Inference] TUFLOW said in writing that the A/P option could bring cell-size sensitivity, and it never published the outcome. That is the closest any vendor comes to naming the effect seen in HEC-RAS. Its validation cases (a 100 m wide rectangle; a 1.7 m flume at 0.05 to 0.34 m cells) have width-to-depth ratios at which wall shear is negligible or resolved, so they cannot show it.
- [Inference] TUFLOW's own pixel-sum conveyance (SGS Materials) was paired with the resistance radius only. TUFLOW never shipped a pixel sum with a slope or wall factor.

### Gaps
- The 2025 status of `SGS Radius Approach` (still present? still "under development"? default still Resistance?) was not found in the 2025.0 manual text read. Neither was the status of SGS Materials after its 2023-03 disabling.
- Whether Method C's default conveyance curve is K = A (A/W)^(2/3)/n over the whole face or something else is not stated in so many words. The release notes say "radius ... equal to the flow area divided by the flow width", which implies the single-section form.
- No TUFLOW benchmark or paper read tests a deep narrow steep-walled channel.

## 3Di / Rana: what the "divided-channel" friction is, and whether walls enter

### Takeaway
3Di (now documented as Rana) computes 2D friction "using the flow depth at each subgrid pixel in the momentum domain", with Chezy or Manning. This is the Volp et al. (2013) subgrid friction, which assumes a uniform flow direction and a uniform friction slope across the momentum domain, i.e. a divided channel of pixel strips. No wetted perimeter or wall term is documented for 2D. Rana states plainly that on sloping terrain its default "wet cross-sectional area will be overestimated, and the friction will be underestimated", and it offers limiters for this.

### Cited Findings
- [Certain] "In the 2D domain, the friction is calculated using the flow depth at each subgrid pixel in the momentum domain. The formulations of Chézy or Manning can be used." — [Rana docs, 2D surface flow](https://docs.ranawaterintelligence.com/h_surfaceflow.html) (old address docs.3di.lizard.net redirects here)
- [Certain] "Calculating 2D flow on sloping terrain requires some special attention. If the default approach is used, the wet cross-sectional area will be overestimated, and the friction will be underestimated. The Rana hydrodynamic computational core offers elegant solutions for this, in the form of limiters." The 2D cross-section of a velocity point is built from the pixel rows either side of the cell boundary: "For each pixel pair, the maximum value is used to describe the 2D cross-section." — [Rana docs, 2D surface flow](https://docs.ranawaterintelligence.com/h_surfaceflow.html)
- [Certain] The subgrid tables hold "Friction parameters per waterlevel (2D)"; friction tables are an exception to coarse table steps because of "the non-linear friction profile"; "The storage capacity in the 2D surface and groundwater domains are grid size independent." References: Casulli 2009; Casulli and Stelling 2011; Stelling, "Quadtree flood simulations with sub-grid digital elevation models", Water Management 165, 567-580 (listed as 2015 on the page; that volume is the 2012 Proc. ICE paper); Volp, Van Prooijen and Stelling 2013. — [Rana docs, Subgrid method](https://docs.ranawaterintelligence.com/h_subgrid.html)
- [Certain] Settings that act on friction depth (Rana simulation settings): `limiter_slope_crossectional_area_2d`, default 0 (uniform water level); 1 "activated in case the downstream water depth is zero"; 2 "It is assumed that the flow behaves as a thin sheet flow" (depth = upstream volume over the maximum surface area of the upstream cell); 3 a smooth transition that needs `thin_layer_definition`. `limiter_slope_friction_2d`, default 0, "determines the effective frictional depth" and "is obligatory in combination with the limiter_slope_crossectional_area_2d". `frict_avg`, default 0: "The roughness coefficient will be averaged within one cell." `friction_shallow_water_correction`, default 0: when on, "the friction is determined both in the classical way and based on averaged values" and the maximum is used; 1 "will use the maximum depth at the edge of the cell"; 2 and 3 "define the depth similar to the cross-sectional area limiter". — [Rana docs, simulation settings](https://docs.ranawaterintelligence.com/h_simulation_settings.html)
- [Certain] The SFINCS authors summarise Volp et al. (2013) as having "extended Casulli's approach to finite volumes and incorporated subgrid corrections to compute advection and bottom friction under the assumptions of locally uniform flow direction and friction slope". — [van Ormondt et al. 2025, GMD 18, 843-861](https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/18/843/2025/)
- [Likely, search snippet only] Volp et al. 2013 (WRR 49, 4126-4135, doi:10.1002/wrcr.20324) applies a uniform flow direction and a uniform friction slope within part of a coarse cell. — [TU Delft repository record](https://repository.tudelft.nl/record/uuid:9e495f41-5233-420f-8bb1-8b109eddbaed)
- [Likely, search snippet of the 1D page only] 3Di's 1D friction has "Chézy/Manning" and "Chézy/Manning with conveyance" types; the latter divides the cross-section into sub-sections by depth. The docs warn that assuming a uniform ratio of wetted perimeter to area can understate capacity where depth varies strongly across a section. So wetted perimeter is a 1D concept in 3Di. — [Rana/3Di docs, 1D flow](https://docs.3di.lizard.net/h_onedee_flow.html)

### Inferences
- [Inference] 3Di's 2D friction is the pixel-sum (divided-channel) form: each pixel strip carries Manning or Chezy flow on its own depth under a common friction slope, and the strips are summed. That is the same family as the parent's solver and SFINCS. Walls enter only as pixels of small depth (plan width). No pixel carries a slope factor or a wetted-perimeter term in any 2D text read.
- [Inference] Rana's admission that friction is underestimated on sloping terrain concerns the thin-sheet regime (the cross-sectional-area overestimate, with depth taken from the maximum of each pixel pair). Its limiters change the depth used, not the perimeter. It is the nearest documented acknowledgement that plan-depth pixel friction under-resists on slopes, but it is aimed at hillslope sheet flow, not at deep flow against a gorge wall.

### Gaps
- The exact equations of 3Di's 2D friction (Volp 2013's per-pixel integral, and how `limiter_slope_friction_2d` sets the "effective frictional depth") were not read: Volp 2013 and Stelling 2012 full texts were not opened this session.
- "Stelling 2012: friction by a divided-channel method", as the parent quotes it, was not verified in the paper itself.
- No Rana statement on cell-size sensitivity of friction in narrow deep channels was found.

## SFINCS, D-Flow FM, MIKE 21 FM, InfoWorks ICM 2D, LISFLOOD-FP: does any carry a wetted-perimeter or slope factor on bed shear?

### Takeaway
Among these five, only D-Flow FM documents an R = A/P option (`Conveyance2D = 1`), alongside analytic 1D and 2D conveyance options; its default is R = HU, the face depth. SFINCS's subgrid friction is explicitly the pixel sum of H^(5/3)/n with no perimeter. LISFLOOD-FP takes the wide-channel assumption (wetted perimeter equal to width). No primary formula was found for MIKE 21 FM or ICM 2D.

### Cited Findings
- [Certain] SFINCS (paper results from the Dollerup release, v2.1.1, November 2023): the subgrid corrections adjust "the conveyance depth, hu, and Manning's roughness, n ... so that the unit discharge, qu, through a cell interface equals the average of the unit discharge of the subgrid pixels within the considered velocity point. An important assumption here is that the water level within the velocity point is constant". ## HEC-RAS 1D against 2D in confined channels: documented differences in stage for the same n, and HEC's statements on why

### Takeaway
I found no HEC statement that 2D and 1D give different stages for the same n in a confined channel, nor any HEC advice to adjust 2D n. HEC's only relevant words are its mesh guidance (cells across, faces along the channel, coarse cells as "1D-type"). Secondary studies report that HEC-RAS 2D gives higher stages than 1D for the same n on real reaches, and that practitioners lower 2D n by 15 to 34 %. They attribute this to losses resolved or added by 2D, not to the face friction formula. In the parent's gorge the 2D model at coarse cells reproduces its own 1D answer, so the question there is the fine-cell drift, not a 1D/2D offset.

### Cited Findings
- [Certain] HEC: "flow can move through a channel in a 1D-type of mode ... it does allow water to move through the channels in a 1D fashion"; "a cell size in that would allow 5 to 7 (or more) cells across the channel is needed" to "capture the two dimensional flow effects within the channel itself"; composite face n "computed in the same manner as for 1D channels". — [HEC-RAS 2D UM, hydraulic property tables](https://www.hec.usace.army.mil/confluence/rasdocs/r2dum/latest/development-of-a-2d-or-combined-1d-2d-model/creating-hydraulic-property-tables-for-2d-flow-areas)
- [Likely, search snippet of a CSU Sacramento thesis] Napa River through St. Helena: the 1D model needed Manning's n values 25 to 34 % higher than the 2D model for the same calibration; with like n, 1D water surfaces were more than 2 ft lower than 2D in places. — [A comparison of 1D and 2D HEC-RAS models of the Napa River](https://scholars.csus.edu/esploro/outputs/graduate/A-comparison-of-1D-and-2D/99257831153901671)
- [Likely, secondary blog via snippet] On four stream reaches without structures and with identical n, "The HEC-RAS 2D models generated higher water surface elevations", and the engineers lowered 2D n to match. A Michael Baker presentation, cited secondhand, suggests reducing a 1D n by 15 to 30 % for HEC-RAS 2D. — [SurfCity H2O, Selecting Manning's n values for 2D HEC-RAS models](https://surfcityh2o.com/selecting-mannings-n-values-for-2d-hec-ras-models/)
- [Likely, conference paper via snippet] A HEC-RAS 1D/2D comparison (CSCE 2019) found that one 2D n maps to a range of 1D n values, and that on a curved channel the "Manning's 1D coefficients obtained are higher than the 2D ones". — [CSCE 2019 paper](https://legacy.csce.ca/elf/apps/CONFERENCEVIEWER/conferences/2019/pdfs/PaperPDFversion_17_0228014648.pdf)
- [Likely, via snippet] A dam-safety reference says adapting n for 2D is "an evolving practice", that 2D n values are generally expected to be lower than 1D, and that unmodified a 2D model "will overcount energy losses"; the reduction depends on the terrain and mesh. A National Academies (TRB) report separates "resolved" Manning's n (captured by terrain and mesh) from the unresolved part. — [ASDSO, Manning n value](https://damsafety.org/reference/manning-n-value); [National Academies, OpenBook 27730 ch. 2](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/27730/chapter/2)

### Inferences
- [Inference] The literature's 1D/2D offset (2D higher for the same n) is explained there by losses that 2D represents and 1D folds into n (lateral shear, secondary currents, expansion). In the parent's straight gorge none of those apply. The coarse-cell HEC-RAS 2D answer (5.28 m) is within 2 % of the single-section 1D Manning answer (5.39 m, computed here), so HEC-RAS 2D at 20 m behaves as HEC's own "1D-type mode". The open question is the other end: as cells shrink, HEC-RAS 2D drops toward a divided-channel answer (4.70 m at 5 m cells; 4.49 m as the per-face limit, computed here). Neither HEC nor the secondary studies discuss this.
- [Inference] For the report the useful distinction is this. The pixel sum (parent's solver, SFINCS, 3Di, TUFLOW Materials) carries no wall shear and is cell-size independent by construction. The single-section A/W (TUFLOW default) also carries no wall shear and is nearly cell-size independent on a flat bed. Only the per-face A/P (HEC-RAS, the TUFLOW A/P option, D-Flow FM Conveyance2D = 1) carries wall shear, and that is exactly the form that makes the answer depend on how many faces divide the section. TUFLOW named that risk in 2020 and HEC never did.
- [Inference] A physically complete pixel form would be K = sum (1/n) d^(5/3) dy / (1 + s^2)^(1/3), each pixel's own A/P with its local bed slope s. It keeps wall shear and stays cell-size independent. No code read documents it; it gives 4.49 m on the gorge against 4.40 m (pixel sum) and 5.39 m (single-section A/P). The single-section A/P is higher because it spreads the walls' shear over the whole section's mean velocity, so the fast centre is slowed by walls it does not touch. The per-pixel form charges wall shear only to the slow wall strips. Which is closer to a real gorge is a lateral-shear question that no code read here settles.

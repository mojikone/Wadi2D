# GPU speed per cell by mesh type for explicit finite-volume shallow-water solvers, and the cost of square tiles plus breakline-aligned strips

Date: 2026-11-07. Scope: hard numbers on GPU cost per cell for uniform, block-uniform quadtree (BUQ) and unstructured meshes; what makes unstructured kernels slow; hybrid and cut-cell meshes on the GPU; the explicit time-step penalty of thin or skewed cells and what local time stepping (LTS) recovers; launch overhead at 0.1 to 1 M cells; and an expected cost per option for Wadi2D against its measured 0.6 ns per cell step (RTX 5080, uniform 32 x 32 tiles). It builds on `W1 research and design note/research/raw/N05_riemann_muscl_gpu_codes.md` (schemes, CFL numbers, SERGHEI and Brodtkorb profiles, Hu et al. LTS) and does not repeat it; N05 is cited by finding number where it carries the evidence.

Tags: [Certain] = read in a primary source in this session (PDF text, paper, official page); [Likely] = abstract, search snippet, secondary description, or a strong inference; [Guessing] = gap filled by arithmetic on assumed values or general knowledge. Several key PDFs refused the fetch (Vacondio et al. 2017 BUQ, Dazzi et al. 2018 LTS and Hu et al. 2022 all returned HTTP 403; Lacasta et al. 2014/2015 are paywalled), so their numbers are [Likely] from abstracts or search snippets.

Normalisation used below: the flux and update kernels of these solvers are memory-bound (SERGHEI profile, N05 finding 6), so per-cell times are scaled between GPUs by DRAM bandwidth. Bandwidths are vendor specifications from memory, not fetched [Likely]: GTX 260 112 GB/s, GTX 280 142 GB/s, GTX 480 177 GB/s, RTX 2070 448 GB/s, RTX 4060 272 GB/s, P100 732 GB/s (STREAM about 495-497 GB/s, measured in the Volna and Sulyok papers), V100 900 GB/s (STREAM 742 GB/s), RTX 5080 960 GB/s. Bandwidth scaling ignores cache growth across generations, which favours unstructured codes on newer cards [Likely].

## 1. Published GPU throughput by mesh type, and the unstructured-to-structured factor on the same GPU with the same scheme

### Takeaway
Almost no source publishes ns per cell step; most give speed-ups against a CPU, which hides the GPU cost. The only same-group, same-scheme, same-card comparison found (Castro et al. 2011, first-order Roe-type, GTX 260, single precision) puts unstructured triangles at about 1.5 to 2 x the per-cell cost of structured cells; independent evidence (Volna-OP2 kernel bandwidths, Sanders' group accuracy-per-cost study, Lacasta's ordering study) puts the realistic range at 1.5 to 3 x for first order, more for second order. Wadi2D's 0.6 ns per cell step on an RTX 5080 is in the same class as the best published structured GPU code once scaled by bandwidth.

### Inferences
- Bandwidth-scaled to an RTX 5080 (x 960/177 = 5.4), Brodtkorb's structured second-order code is 0.26 ns (early exit) to 0.35 ns (Malpasset mean) and 0.53 ns (no early exit) per cell step; Wadi2D's 0.6 ns sits next to the no-early-exit figure while carrying sub-grid tables per cell and face, so it is not a slow structured code [Guessing: bandwidth scaling across 15 years of cache changes].
- Volna's two indirect kernels (gradients, applyFluxes) hold 70 % of its run time at 27 % and 41 % of the card's bandwidth, while its direct kernels reach 69-77 %. Had they run at direct-kernel efficiency the step would be about 1.7 x faster; that is the indirect-access penalty of a well-engineered unstructured code, before counting the index arrays that inflate "achieved" bandwidth [Guessing, arithmetic on Certain numbers].
- Converging evidence gives a raw per-cell-step factor of unstructured over structured, same scheme and GPU, of 1.5-2 x (Castro, first order), about 1.7 x from indirect access alone (Volna), with up to 2 x more lost to a bad cell order (Lacasta) and 2-3 x in accuracy per cost (Kim, CPU) [Likely]. For a second-order or staggered scheme, which needs gradient or face-neighbour reconstructions (Volna's gradients kernel is half its run time), 2-4 x per cell step is the defensible range [Likely].
- Wadi2D's own 1.6 x from the quadtree at 4.8 M cells implies the mesh plus LTS costs about 1.04 x the uniform grid per cell per simulated hour ((603 / 4.8) / (977 / 8.1)) [Certain arithmetic]; per cell step it is higher, because LTS lets coarse tiles take fewer steps, and the split cannot be made without the step counts [Gap].

- No 2020-2026 source runs one GPU code on Cartesian and triangular meshes with the same scheme and reports ns per cell step; the extended search returned none. Castro 2011 (GTX 260 era) is the only like-for-like measurement found.
- No published ns per cell step was found for TRITON, HiPIMS, LISFLOOD-FP 8 GPU, TUFLOW HPC, Iber+, RiverFlow2D GPU or TUFLOW FV; they publish speed-ups or wall times without step counts. RiverFlow2D was not found at all. The "HyPAR" and "Hydro-UFO" codes named in the brief were not found as GPU shallow-water codes [Gap; HyPAR is, from memory, a structured finite-difference conservation-law code, Guessing].

## 2. What makes unstructured GPU kernels slow, and what recovers the speed

### Takeaway
The cost is indirect addressing: gathers through cell-to-cell or edge-to-cell maps and scatters of face fluxes into cells, which defeat coalescing, invite data races and cut cache reuse. Reordering (bandwidth-reducing or partitioning), structure-of-arrays storage, hierarchical colouring with shared memory, and choosing the loop mode per kernel recover 15 % to 2 x, but published tuned unstructured kernels still sit at 30-60 % of STREAM bandwidth against 70-85 % for direct kernels on the same card.

### Cited Findings
- The key difficulty is "the indirect accesses that lead to data-races when parallelized" [Certain] — [Sulyok, Reguly, Mudalige et al. 2019, JPDC (arXiv 1802.03749)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1802.03749).
- Sulyok et al., P100 (STREAM 495 GB/s) and V100 (742 GB/s): Airfoil res_calc (2.8 M cells), global colouring 94 GB/s (4.65 ms), hierarchical colouring with shared memory 211-215 GB/s (about 2.05 ms), plus partitioning 233-239 GB/s (1.83-1.92 ms), reuse factor 2 to 3.6; Volna SpaceDiscretization (3.59 M edges) 119 GB/s (0.93 ms) to 146 GB/s (0.77 ms), about 30 % of P100 peak, and 462 GB/s (62 %) on the V100; LULESH: atomics were 17 % faster than the best hierarchical colouring on the P100 and about 2 x on the V100 (561 GB/s, 76 % of peak); miniAero face fluxes: hierarchical colouring 75 % faster than atomics on the P100 and 158 % on the V100; headline 1.1-1.75 x over the state of the art [Certain] — [arXiv 1802.03749](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1802.03749). The authors report no single verdict on atomics against colouring; more reuse brings more colours and more synchronisation stalls (13 % to 36-39 % in LULESH).
- Weng et al. 2021, second-order unstructured FV CFD on V100 and K80: face loops give the best data locality but are not always fastest; a cell loop wins when a kernel only reads cell data indirectly and needs no atomics; choosing the loop mode per kernel raised whole-code performance by 15-20 % [Likely, abstract via search] — [Advances in Aerodynamics 2021](https://aia.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s42774-021-00073-y).
- Ma and Lian 2024, unstructured shallow water: the edge loop was 18.34 % and 24.29 % faster than the cell loop in their two cases [Likely, search snippet; GPU model not seen] — [Water Resources Management 38(2)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/waterr/v38y2024i2d10.1007_s11269-023-03696-6.html).
- de la Asunción, Mantas and Castro 2012: renumbering triangle cells by bandwidth-reducing orders helps more the more expensive the scheme [Likely, search snippet] — [hgpu record](https://hgpu.org/?p=7785).
- Castro et al. 2011: on triangles "we must compute the whole numerical scheme and we use a single kernel for processing all the edges", each edge writing to per-volume accumulators (three accumulators per cell, one per edge slot) so no two threads write the same slot; on the GTX 480 a 48 KB L1 / 16 KB shared split gave better times for the edge kernel [Certain] — [Castro et al. 2011](https://comptes-rendus.academie-sciences.fr/mecanique/item/10.1016/j.crme.2010.12.004.pdf).
- VOLNA-OP2 renumbers the mesh at load and uses OP2's two-level colouring (mini-partitions coloured, then threads within a block coloured); applyFluxes, the edge-to-cell increment, is "limited by the irregular access", 204 GB/s against 342-382 GB/s for the direct stages [Certain] — [GMD 11, 4621](https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/11/4621/2018/).
- A Z-order (Morton) curve gives coalesced access in a GPU quadtree, and a parallel tree traversal with stored neighbour indices replaces the sequential depth-first traversal [Certain] — [Chowdhury et al. 2023](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/205706/1/jh0251210.pdf).
- SERGHEI chose Cartesian grids "to avoid issues with memory mapping, coalescence and cache misses in GPUs" (N05 finding 5) [Certain].

### Inferences
- For a strip of aligned cells, the ordering problem largely disappears: a strip is a chain along the line, so storing its cells and faces in along-line order gives near-ideal locality without RCM, Hilbert or Morton machinery [Likely].
- Atomics give a nondeterministic float32 summation order. Wadi2D's GPU runs are bitwise repeatable today (W31: two GPU runs bitwise identical; W33: resume byte for byte), so a strip coupling that scatters interface fluxes with atomics would break those guarantees; a gather in a fixed face order per cell, or Castro's fixed accumulator slots, keeps them [Likely].
- The staggered scheme (level per cell, momentum per face) adds a constraint that collocated Riemann codes do not have: on non-rectangular cells the face momentum needs face-normal pressure gradients (orthogonality between the centre-to-centre line and the face) and a reconstruction of the tangential velocity for advection, as D-Flow FM and UnTRIM-type codes do; that is extra indirect work per face and an accuracy risk at skewed transition cells [Likely, from general knowledge; not checked in a source this session].

### Gaps
- No source measured the same SWE kernel in AoS against SoA on a GPU; Sulyok's mixed results (AoS better for Airfoil and Volna on the P100, SoA better for LULESH) say the layout is kernel-dependent.
- Block-structured unstructured meshes (quadrilateral blocks holding structured triangles) exist as a mesh-generation idea [Likely, search snippet — [Springer 2019](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1134/S0965542519120182)], but no GPU SWE timing for them was found.

## 3. Hybrid, multi-block, overset and cut-cell meshes on the GPU: interface handling and overhead

### Takeaway
No shallow-water code was found that runs square tiles at structured GPU speed with separate breakline-aligned strips. The production ways to get resolution or alignment at lines are: a block-uniform quadtree (same kernel per block, interface lookups only at block edges, about 20 % per-cell overhead in TUFLOW's implementation); a fully unstructured or flexible mesh with aligned cells (HEC-RAS on the CPU, TUFLOW FV and mixed tri/quad codes on the GPU), which pays the unstructured price everywhere; nested Cartesian grids (Tsunami-HySEA); and a zero-width embedded barrier in a Cartesian grid stabilised by state redistribution or cell merging, reported to save about five-fold in time steps against refining at the barrier (CPU).

### Cited Findings
- BUQ: blocks of uniform cells whose resolution may differ from block to block, with a data structure that keeps GPU efficiency "with minimum overheads"; the multi-GPU version exchanges partition borders by MPI, and border communication is "completely maskable by GPU calculations" in weak scaling [Likely, abstracts] — [Vacondio et al. 2017](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1364815216309252), [Turchetto, Vacondio, Dal Palù 2018, HIC](https://easychair.org/publications/paper/9Qpz).
- TUFLOW Quadtree: single-level Quadtree identical to the HPC grid is on average 20 % slower; the smallest cells' "smaller minimum timestep ... gets applied to the whole model"; a change of cell size inside a channel "can locally change the effective flow width" and cause recirculation or bumpy levels, so transitions belong on the floodplain; Quadtree stores only active cells and uses less GPU memory than HPC [Certain] — [TUFLOW FAQ](https://wiki.tuflow.com/Quadtree_and_Sub-Grid_Sampling_FAQ).
- HEC-RAS 2D (CPU, implicit): breaklines make cell faces follow the line, with a cell-centre spacing along it, and RAS redraws the mesh around the line; a breakline spacing much smaller than the mesh produces cells with more than 8 faces, which RAS will not run; collinear faces run but can affect the computation [Likely, practitioner sources, not the manual] — [Kleinschmidt, 2D area breaklines](https://kleinschmidtgroup.com/ras-post/2d-mesh-leaking-part-2-2d-area-breaklines), [RAS Solution forum](https://therassolution.kleinschmidtgroup.com/the-ras-solution-forum/topic/breakline-and-cell-spacign-techniques-at-roadslinear-strutures-in-2d/). This is the "square cells plus aligned cells" pattern, but in one unstructured data structure on the CPU.
- Ryoo and Mandli (GeoClaw lineage, CPU): sea walls as a zero-width embedded boundary in a Cartesian grid, acting as a flux interface, with state redistribution (SRD) for the small cut cells; conservative and "geometrically easy to implement"; about five-fold fewer time steps than a same-resolution run with adaptive refinement at the barrier; a companion paper uses cell merging and shows second-order convergence on a linear and a V-shaped barrier [Likely, abstracts and snippets] — [arXiv 2112.09696](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09696), [arXiv 2112.09566](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.09566).
- The Forrer-Jeltsch cut-cell variant avoids the small-cell problem because fluxes are still computed at the Cartesian faces and the time integration is done over the whole Cartesian cell volume [Likely, snippet] — [arXiv 2601.04099](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.04099).
- Cut cells on CUDA exist for the Euler equations (a Cambridge thesis: split Euler solver with piecewise-linear cut cells, compressed embedded-boundary data structures, scaling to 32 GPUs) [Likely, abstract] — [Cambridge repository](https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/handle/1810/298318). AMReX runs embedded boundaries on GPUs with extra arrays for face apertures, normals and volume fractions plus masks, and disables GPU tiling by default to cut launch overhead; no isolated EB overhead was found [Likely] — [NERSC AMReX GPU case study](https://docs.nersc.gov/performance/case-studies/amrex-gpu), [AMReX JOSS](https://joss.theoj.org/papers/10.21105/joss.01370.pdf).
- Nested grids: Tsunami-HySEA (CUDA, MPI multi-GPU) uses a nested grid system to reduce load; for the FUNWAVE-TVD Boussinesq model, the two-way nesting interface "involves a significant programming effort" and a GPU version of the interface was left as future work [Likely, snippets] — [arXiv 2408.07609](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.07609), [FUNWAVE nesting preprint](https://egusphere.copernicus.org/preprints/2022/egusphere-2022-35/egusphere-2022-35.pdf).
- Mixed triangle and quadrilateral unstructured meshes run on multiple GPUs (MPI + OpenACC), "improves the flexibility of mesh generation" [Likely, snippet] — [EMS 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1364815224002020).
- Adaptive quadtree on the GPU: GPU-HWFV1 pays its adaptivity overhead back only on deep hierarchies (L >= 9) and loses when terrain forces over-refinement [Certain] — [Chowdhury et al. 2023](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/205706/1/jh0251210.pdf).

### Inferences
- How a strip should couple to the tiles, from the lessons above [Likely]:
  1. Do not touch the tile kernel. Tile cells under a strip are masked inactive, and the tile faces they would have used are closed; the strip owns its cells, its interface faces and every irregular transition polygon, so no tile inherits a small cell.
  2. Interface faces as a separate list (tile cell index, strip cell index, normal, length, sub-grid face table) processed by one face kernel; each tile cell next to a strip gets its interface fluxes by gather from a fixed per-cell list (or fixed accumulator slots, as Castro did), never by atomics, to keep bitwise repeatability.
  3. Strip cells and faces in structure-of-arrays, ordered along the line; one cell kernel and one face kernel for all strips, launched once per (sub)step, not once per strip.
  4. Keep transitions out of channels (TUFLOW's advice) where the line allows, and merge slivers rather than keep them (HEC-RAS's more-than-8-face limit and SRD/merging both say small cells are the trouble).
- The zero-width embedded barrier (a cut face inside a square tile cell, SRD or merging for the small piece) is the low-cost alternative to a strip: it keeps the structured kernel, carries the exact crest geometry and does not shrink the step; what it does not give is cells aligned with flow along the line, which matters for the 45-degree channel error Wadi2D measured (W3, W6) [Likely]. It deserves a place beside the strip in the option list.
- Wadi2D's own W3 result that snapped faces oscillated to +29 % on banks (project CLAUDE.md) is a warning for any interface where face geometry changes abruptly; the strip-to-tile interface is such a place, so the oscillation test belongs in the strip prototype's gate [Likely].

### Gaps
- No shallow-water GPU code was found that combines a structured fast path with unstructured or aligned strips; no measured interface overhead for structured-unstructured coupling on a GPU was found in any field searched (overset and Chimera GPU CFD were not reached within the search budget).
- Whether D-Flow FM, MIKE 21 FM or TUFLOW FV meshes with quad strips along channels are used on the GPU with a separate structured path was not checked [Gap; TUFLOW FV GPU is a single flexible-mesh solver, Likely].

## 4. The time-step penalty of thin or skewed aligned cells, and what local time stepping recovers

### Takeaway
With one global step, the smallest effective cell length in fast flow sets the step for the whole domain, and the cost scales as cells divided by that length: a strip half or a quarter of the tile width in the limiting flow makes the whole run 1.6 to 4 x dearer. Power-of-two LTS contains it, but published GPU gains from LTS are modest (1.03 to 1.56 x in the one recent GPU study read, 2.8 x for block-level LTS on BUQ, up to 5 x in Hu et al.), and block-level LTS like Wadi2D's penalises a whole tile for one small cell inside it. The fix is structural: strip and transition cells in their own LTS class, sized so that their effective length times the ratio of wave speeds is not below the tile size.

### Cited Findings
- Water 2025 (unstructured triangles, power-of-two LTS, NVIDIA RTX 4060): each cell's level m_i = min(int(log2(dt_i/dt_min)), m_user), an edge takes the smaller level of its two cells, a cell is capped by its neighbours' levels, and dt_i = 2^m dt_min; with m_user = 2 a global step is four sub-steps, level 0 updated in all four, level 1 in two, level 2 in one [Certain] — [Water 17(18) 2662](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4441/17/18/2662).
- Same paper, Table 1, three-hump dam break, serial base model cost and speed-ups: uniform 129,486 cells at 0.2 m, 2.63 min, LTS 1.29 x, GPU 15.8 x, all combined 16.21 x; refined 243,790 cells, minimum 0.1 m, 11.34 min, LTS 1.36 x, GPU 38.45 x, combined 41.58 x; refined 474,276 cells, minimum 0.05 m, 39.92 min, LTS 1.56 x, GPU 49.06 x, combined 62.98 x [Certain; the text gives 173,080 elements for both refined meshes, which conflicts with the table]. Field case, 87,342 triangles of 33-300 m: CPU 46 min, LTS 34 min (1.35 x), GPU 1.1 min (41.8 x), all combined about 40 s (69 x) [Certain].
- Arithmetic on that table: serial cost per cell is (39.92 / 474,276) / (2.63 / 129,486) = 4.1 x higher on the mesh whose minimum edge is 4 x smaller, so the smallest element set the global step for every cell [Certain arithmetic]. On the GPU, LTS (m_user = 2) added only 62.98 / 49.06 = 1.28 x on that mesh and 1.03 x on the uniform one [Certain arithmetic].
- Dazzi et al. 2018: LTS on BUQ grids on the GPU with one time step per block; reported about 2.8 x extra; criticised because "each block takes the smallest allowable step among its cells", so extra cost remains where step limits vary within a block [Likely, secondhand via the Hu et al. 2022 abstract and a search summary; the PDF refused the fetch] — [Dazzi et al. record](https://air.unipr.it/handle/11381/2836093), [Hu et al. 2022](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4441/14/6/922).
- Hu et al. 2022: per-cell LTS on an unstructured HLLC GPU code, up to 5 x on the GPU (Tesla K80) and 10 x on the CPU (N05 finding 35) [Certain].
- TUFLOW Quadtree has no LTS: the smaller step of refined cells "gets applied to the whole model" [Certain] — [TUFLOW FAQ](https://wiki.tuflow.com/Quadtree_and_Sub-Grid_Sampling_FAQ).
- Wadi2D (W10, decision 22): tile LTS saves nothing on a uniform 2 M-cell grid (161.98 s uniform, 190.73 s at buffer 1.0, 160.25 s at buffer 0.5, though tile steps halve), and gains on the mesh at scale [Certain] — [project CLAUDE.md](file:///D:/Mojtaba/Renardet/Wadi2D/CLAUDE.md).
- CFL length measures used for non-square cells: inscribed-circle radius (Delmas, CFL 0.9) and minimum centroid-to-face distance (Hu, Cr 0.9) (N05 finding 34) [Certain].
- An embedded barrier with SRD reduces the time-step count about five-fold against refining at the barrier [Likely, abstract] — [arXiv 2112.09696](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09696).

### Inferences
- The penalty, for a strip cell of width w across the strip and length a along it, inside tiles of size dx [Certain arithmetic]:
  - with a directional condition dt = C min(dx/(|u|+c), dy/(|v|+c)), the factor on the whole run is dx/w: 2 x at w = dx/2, 4 x at w = dx/4;
  - with the 2D staggered gravity-wave bound dt <= C / (sqrt(gh) sqrt(1/w^2 + 1/a^2)) and a = dx, the factor is sqrt((dx^2/w^2 + 1)/2): 1.58 x at w = dx/2, 2.92 x at w = dx/4;
  - skew: for a parallelogram transition cell with angle theta, the distance between opposite faces is side x sin(theta), so a 30-degree cell halves its length and its step.
  Which formula applies depends on Wadi2D's own CFL expression; with sub-grid tables the effective length of a partly wet cell is wet plan area over wet face width, which a thin strip cell with long faces makes small [Likely].
- The strip only bites if its cells are in the flow that limits the step. The minimum strip width that leaves a global step unchanged is w_min = dx_lim x (|u|+c)_strip / (|u|+c)_lim, where dx_lim and (|u|+c)_lim belong to the cell that limits the step today [Certain arithmetic]. Example [Guessing on the flow values]: a gorge limiting cell of 10 m at 3 m depth and 5 m/s (|u|+c about 10.4 m/s) and a dyke strip on the plain overtopped at 0.5 m and 1.5 m/s (about 3.7 m/s) give w_min about 3.6 m; the same strip along a wadi bank in the gorge flow needs w >= 10 m.
- Wadi2D's tile-level LTS has Dazzi's weakness: if transition polygons lived inside tiles, every tile crossed by a line (about 1.4 tiles per 320 m of line at 10 m cells, more on diagonals) would drop to the strip's level. A 30 km line at 10 m crosses roughly 100-140 tiles, 100-140 k cells forced to 2-4 x the steps, about 1-2 % of an 8 M-cell domain per line [Guessing]. Keeping strip and transition cells in their own class avoids it entirely [Likely].
- Rule of thumb for Wadi2D [Likely]: strip cells no thinner than dx/2 across the strip, transition slivers merged until their effective length is at least dx/2, and the strip in its own power-of-two class one level below the tiles; then the tiles keep their step and the strip does 2 sub-steps per tile step at a cell count of about 1 % of the domain.

### Gaps
- The full Dazzi 2018 and Hu 2022 papers were not readable here (403); the share of cells in the finest class and the per-sub-step overhead on the GPU are therefore not quoted.
- No source quantifies the step penalty of strips specifically; all figures above are arithmetic on the CFL condition.

## 5. Launch overhead and occupancy for small domains (0.1 to 1 M cells)

### Takeaway
Below about 1 M cells a GPU step is a few hundred microseconds or less, and per-kernel launch costs of 2.5 to 10 microseconds (more from Python) become a large share; every published GPU speed-up curve collapses below 0.1 M cells. Any design that adds kernels per step (quadtree levels, LTS sub-steps, a strip) pays most on the small domains, which is why Wadi2D's mesh loses below about 1 M cells. CUDA Graphs or kernel fusion are the known cure.

### Cited Findings
- NVIDIA: with CUDA Graphs a sequence of kernels is launched in one operation; in the blog example the time per kernel including overheads was 3.8 µs against 2.9 µs of execution [Likely, search summary of the blog] — [NVIDIA blog, Getting started with CUDA Graphs](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/cuda-graphs).
- NVIDIA, A100: kernels of 2-8 µs, Graphs gave 16-26 % speed-up depending on size and method [Likely, search summary] — [Employing CUDA Graphs in a dynamic environment](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/employing-cuda-graphs-in-a-dynamic-environment/).
- NVIDIA documentation: launch overhead comes from several software layers, and the language-transition stage alone is put at 10-100 µs; the GPU idles waiting for the CPU when kernels last only a few µs [Likely, search summary] — [NVIDIA CUDA Graph basics](https://docs.nvidia.com/dl-cuda-graph/cuda-graph-basics/introduction.html).
- A user report on the NVIDIA forum: minimum launch overhead about 2.5-3 µs on recent hardware, down from about 5 µs [Guessing grade: forum claim] — [NVIDIA forum](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/launch-of-many-small-kernels-10x-slower-compared-to-one-kernel/350194).
- TUFLOW HPC GPU against Classic by model size: 7,500 wet cells 4 x; 31,000 5 x; 125,000 21 x; 3.1 M 76 x; 12.5 M 78 x; "In smaller models the communication overhead dominates the overall simulation time" [Certain; hardware not stated] — [TUFLOW wiki](https://wiki.tuflow.com/Hardware_Benchmarking_Topic_HPC_on_CPU_vs_GPU).
- TUFLOW FV on a V100 against one core: 1,375 cells 3.3 x, 5,500 10.5 x, 22,000 31.4 x, 88,000 71.4 x, 352,000 103.2 x [Certain] — [TUFLOW FV benchmarking](https://tuflow.com/insights/2021_04-tuflow-fv-speed-benchmarking/).
- RTX 4060, unstructured: GPU speed-up 15.8 x at 129,486 cells, 38.45 x at 243,790, 49.06 x at 474,276 [Certain] — [Water 17(18) 2662](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4441/17/18/2662).
- GPU adaptivity overhead pays back only from a 512 x 512 finest grid upward (RTX 2070) [Certain] — [Chowdhury et al. 2023](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/205706/1/jh0251210.pdf).
- AMReX turns GPU tiling off by default to reduce kernel launch overhead [Likely] — [NERSC AMReX case study](https://docs.nersc.gov/performance/case-studies/amrex-gpu).
- Brodtkorb: the dry-block early exit doubled throughput (N05 finding 40) [Certain]; Wadi2D's tile mesh is slower than uniform on Bidah's 90 tiles because launches swamp the kernels (decision 22) [Certain].

### Inferences
- At 0.6 ns per cell step, a 100 k-cell domain computes a step in about 60 µs and a 1 M-cell domain in about 600 µs. With about 10 kernel launches per step at 3-10 µs from C++ (more through CuPy from Python), launch time is 30-100 µs per step: comparable to the compute at 0.1 M cells and 5-15 % at 1 M cells [Guessing: Wadi2D's kernel count and its Python launch cost were not measured here]. Block 2 at 10 m (about 4.9 ms per step) is untouched by it.
- Every extra kernel a strip brings (cell kernel, face kernel, interface kernel, a sub-step of LTS) costs a fixed 3-10 µs per launch regardless of the strip's size, so on Bidah-sized domains the strip's cost is launches, not cells. Capturing the whole step, strip and LTS sub-steps included, in one CUDA Graph is the expected cure; CuPy exposes stream capture into graphs [Likely, not verified against the CuPy version Wadi2D pins].
- Occupancy: a strip of 10-50 k cells is too small to fill an RTX 5080 (84 SMs [Likely]) on its own; launching it concurrently with the tile kernels on a second stream, or folding strip cells into the tail of the tile kernel's grid, avoids a half-empty GPU during the strip kernels [Likely].

### Gaps
- No source measured CuPy or NVRTC launch overhead specifically, and Wadi2D's per-step kernel count and launch time were not profiled for this note. A profile of one Bidah step (kernels, launch gaps) is the cheapest way to close it.

## 6. Expected cost of each mesh option for Wadi2D

### Takeaway
Per cell step: uniform tiles 1.0 (0.6 ns, measured); the quadtree 1.05-1.3; a fully unstructured mesh 2-4; tiles plus aligned strips 1.01-1.1 averaged over the domain, because the strips are about 1 % of the cells. The strip's real risk is not its per-cell cost but the time step: with one global step a strip thinner than the tile cells in the limiting flow multiplies the whole run by 1.6-4; with the strip in its own power-of-two class it costs 1.02-1.2 on large domains and more on small ones through launches.

### Inferences
- Speed ranking per cell: uniform tiles, then tiles plus strips (within 10 % of uniform if the strip sits in its own LTS class and its transition cells are merged), then the quadtree, then fully unstructured, which is the only option that costs a factor of 2-4 on every cell [Likely].
- The decisive engineering choices for the strip are, in order: its own LTS class (or a width rule w >= w_min) so it never sets the global step; transition polygons owned by the strip, slivers merged; gather in fixed order, no atomics; all strips in one cell kernel and one face kernel; the whole step in one CUDA Graph for small domains [Likely].
- A sixth option outside the brief deserves a measurement beside the strip: a zero-width cut face inside the square cells (SRD or merging), which costs about 1.0-1.1 per cell and nothing in time step [Guessing on the factor], but does not align cells with flow along the line [Likely].
- None of these factors covers accuracy; the strip's accuracy at the tile interface (orthogonality, the +29 % oscillation seen with snapped faces in W3) is the gate it must pass before its speed matters [Likely].

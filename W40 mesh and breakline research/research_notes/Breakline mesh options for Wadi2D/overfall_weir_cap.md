# Free overfall, submergence and along-line flow on crest faces: theory, practice, and a face law for Wadi2D

Date: 2026-11-07. Scope: why an explicit staggered (or Godunov) 2D shallow-water face over-carries a free overfall at an abrupt crest; how production codes cap it and switch to submerged flow; submergence laws; validation data; how to make a cap orientation-free on a staircase or an aligned strip without throttling flow that runs along a line or through a drowned crest. Builds on `W1 research and design note/research/raw/N09_boundaries_injection_weirs.md` (N09), which already holds the TUFLOW, HEC-RAS, Iber, LISFLOOD-FP, Rana 1D-weir material, the Villemonte and TUFLOW coefficient tables and the HLL dry-step arithmetic; those are cited here as "N09 #n" and not repeated.

Tags: [Certain] read in a primary source (or a project record / code read directly); [Certain, arithmetic] my own derivation, checkable; [Likely] strong inference, or read only in an abstract or search summary; [Guessing] filling a gap. Quotes are kept to a minimum; equations are transcribed.

Notation used throughout: face invert z_c (crest); upstream side U and downstream side D by the sign of the flow; e_U = eta_U - z_c and e_D = eta_D - z_c the levels over the invert; E = e_U + u^2/(2g) a specific energy over the invert; q per unit face width; q_max(E) = sqrt(g) (2/3 E)^1.5 = 1.7046 E^1.5 (SI), the largest discharge per unit width that a specific energy E can pass (critical flow); r = e_D / E_U the submergence ratio; n the unit normal of the true line (crest polyline), t its tangent, e_f the normal of a lattice face.

## 1. Why momentum conservation at an abrupt bed step over-predicts discharge (staggered and Godunov schemes)

### Takeaway
A free overfall is choked: the specific-energy theorem caps q at q_max(E_U) whatever happens downstream, and a shallow-water face has no critical section of its own. In a staggered face the area is taken at the upstream depth while the velocity is driven by the whole level difference across the face, so the face converts the full head to velocity and keeps the full depth: q = H sqrt(2gH), exactly 2.598 x critical, which is W36's 2.62 at a 1.5 m head and is independent of the cell size. Collocated Godunov schemes with hydrostatic reconstruction choke correctly in the steady state (Fr = 1 in the last wet cell) but overshoot transiently and are ambiguous at the step itself, where the literature splits between energy-conserving and momentum-conserving closures; the consistent physics is energy (Bernoulli) through a contraction, momentum through an expansion, which is Stelling and Duinmeijer's rule.

- **Valiani and Caleffi, "Momentum balance in the shallow water equations on bottom discontinuities" (Adv. Water Resour.; arXiv 1912.05465)** [Certain, read]:

  - the depth Y_S that makes momentum agree with energy always lies between the depths just upstream and downstream of the step;
  - the fixed-depth closures used by Bernetti et al., Rosatti and Fraccarollo, Rosatti and Begnudelli, Cozzolino et al.   - choked backward-facing step: the upstream state becomes critical; the fixed-depth closure then dissipates a head of relative order 1e-1, growing with the step;
  - recommendation: total-head-conserving schemes; real losses added as xi U^2/(2g).

### Inferences
- [Certain, arithmetic] **The 2.6 asymptote.** If a face carries the full upstream depth H over the crest at the velocity that converts the full head, sqrt(2gH), then q = sqrt(2) sqrt(g) H^1.5, and q / q_max(H) = sqrt(2) / (2/3)^1.5 = 2.598. W36 measured 2.62 at 1.5 m. Bernoulli forbids both at once: depth and velocity head share E, and the maximum of q at fixed E is at h = (2/3)E. The staggered face double-counts the head.
- [Likely] **Why it does not depend on dx.** In the steady state the face momentum balance u du/dx = -g d(eta)/dx integrates to u^2/2 = g (Delta eta) over the face whatever dx is, so refining the mesh cannot create a critical section: the face has none. The larger ratios at low heads (3.8 at 0.3 m) fit a drive larger than e_U (W36: drive = max(e_up, min(drop, |eL - eR|))): ratio ~ 2.6 sqrt(drive / e_U), i.e. drive about 2.1 e_U at 0.3 m [Guessing on the exact split between drive and friction].
- [Certain, arithmetic, first-order 1D model] **Collocated Godunov with hydrostatic reconstruction chokes correctly in the steady state.** With the downstream level below the crest, the face sees (h, u) against a dry state. Exact Riemann solution: the sonic state at x/t = 0 gives q = ((u + 2c)/3)^3 / g; steady q = h u gives F = (F + 2)^3 / 27, so F = 1. HLL with S_L = u - c, S_R = u + 2c gives a mass flux h (u + 2c)/3; steady gives u = c, F = 1. So the last wet cell is at critical depth and q = q_max(E_cell). The 1.22 overshoot of N09 #34 is the first instant only. This is why Godunov codes (Iber, HiPIMS) rarely report a free-overfall over-carry, while staggered and diffusion-wave codes (TUFLOW Classic before the weir switch, HEC-RAS 2D, Wadi2D breaklines) do.
- [Likely] **Why momentum conservation at a step-up (contraction) over-predicts.** Across a contraction, momentum is not conserved locally: an unknown reaction acts on the riser. A momentum-conservative discretisation fixes that reaction by assuming a hydrostatic thrust at some depth; if the depth is wrong it can create head (Valiani and Caleffi's paradox), and created head is created discharge. Bernoulli through the contraction (lossless acceleration toward the crest) plus momentum through the expansion (the jump or the plunge losses) is the closure that cannot create energy, which is the Stelling-Duinmeijer and D-Flow FM rule.
- [Likely] **HEC-RAS's 4.6 x has the same structure.** The face velocity comes from the water-surface gradient over the cell spacing (including the drop below the crest) and the face area from the higher water surface, with no critical limit; a 0.25 m pool passing 4.6 x critical is the same double count with a larger drive. HEC's documentation concedes the over-prediction (N09 #22).
- [Likely] **D-Flow FM's fix is the minimal one and works by changing the drive, not by capping.** Replacing zeta_R in the pressure gradient by max(zeta_R, z_C + (2/3)E) and the face depth by max(zeta_R - z_C, (2/3)E) makes the steady face pass q_max(E) in free flow and, when drowned, the energy-based drowned weir law q = e_D sqrt(2g (E - e_D)) (Section 3), with one continuous switch at e_D = (2/3)E and no coefficient. It needs Bernoulli advection on the weir face.

### Gaps
- Stelling and Duinmeijer 2003 and Kramer and Stelling 2008 full texts were not read; whether they test a free overfall against lab data, and what their weir result is, is unconfirmed. D-Flow FM's documented scheme stands in for their method here.
- Audusse et al. 2004's bibliographic details are from memory; its own statements on bed steps were not read.
- No source found that quantifies the steady-state free-overfall discharge of a second-order (MUSCL) hydrostatic-reconstruction scheme at a step much larger than the depth; my F = 1 derivation is first order and 1D.

## 2. How production codes cap it: free/submerged switch and orientation

### Takeaway
Every production code that handles road and dyke overtopping well puts an explicit control on the crest face: D-Flow FM by an energy-conserving drive with a critical-depth floor (or empirical energy losses fitted to flumes), TUFLOW HPC and Iber by a weir equation with a Villemonte-type factor, MIKE by an internal discharge boundary, HEC-RAS only on SA/2D connections and lateral structures, never on breaklines. Only 3Di/Rana relies on the momentum equation alone (with an undocumented gradient limiter at steep steps). Orientation is handled explicitly only by D-Flow FM (width projected by |n_face . n_weir|, with an admission that oblique weirs are not understood) and by HEC's length proportioning; TUFLOW uses the face length.

### Cited Findings
- **D-Flow FM, three sub-grid fixed-weir approaches** (Sect. 6.7) [Certain]: numerical (FixedWeirScheme = 6: Bernoulli/momentum, Section 1 above), and two empirical energy-loss laws taken from SIMONA/WAQUA, Tabellenboek (8) and Villemonte (9), fitted to flume data; a weir is a face exactly on the weir, no cell is cut by it; continuity is untouched once the face areas carry the crest; bl1, bl2 raised to max(z_c, bl). — [D-Flow FM TRM, pp. 95-96](https://content.oss.deltares.nl/delft3dfm2d3d/D-Flow_FM_Technical_Reference_Manual.pdf)
- **D-Flow FM empirical laws enter as an energy loss, not a cap**: a term -g Delta E/dx in the momentum equation, i.e. a level jump Delta E at the weir. In critical flow a loss is prescribed so the discharge converges over a short time to the empirical discharge; in subcritical flow the loss equals that of a 1D steady flow with the given discharge (2D effects may keep the discharge from the empirical value). Delta E is under-relaxed, Delta E_new = (1 - alpha) Delta E_new + alpha Delta E_old, default alpha 0.6, raised for stability. [Certain] — [D-Flow FM TRM, Sect. 6.7.5, pp. 101-102](https://content.oss.deltares.nl/delft3dfm2d3d/D-Flow_FM_Technical_Reference_Manual.pdf)

### Inferences
- [Likely] Two families exist: **drive modification** (D-Flow scheme 6: the downstream level seen by the face cannot fall below the critical level on the crest; empirical schemes 8 and 9: an added head loss) and **discharge replacement or cap** (TUFLOW HPC, Iber, MIKE, HEC SA/2D, Wadi2D today). The first keeps the face a momentum face and switches continuously; the second needs a regime test and can jump at the switch.
- [Likely] No code documents a rule that leaves tangential (along-line) flow alone. D-Flow's projected width w_u and MIKE's "perpendicular width" apply to the whole face, so they reduce along-line conveyance through the same faces, the effect W36 suspects in Wadi2D's 0.71.
- [Likely] The Dutch abandoning scheme 6 for an empirical law in production river models is a warning that the pure Bernoulli/critical-floor face, while exact for a single isolated crest, is less robust where weirs crowd cells, which is Wadi2D's staircase situation.

## 3. Submergence: Villemonte, roadway curves, HDS-1/HDS-5, broad-crested data

### Takeaway
For a broad crest the ideal energy-based drowned law q = e_D sqrt(2g(E_U - e_D)) is free up to r = 2/3, continuous there, and matches TUFLOW's fitted broad-crested Villemonte curve within 0.01; road embankments and embankment weirs hold modular flow longer (Bradley/HDS-1 roadway curve flat to about 0.85), and sharp crests drown earliest. D-Flow FM's production law is a Villemonte form written in energy heights with the crest length, ramp slopes and ground height inside it, and uses only the velocity components normal to the weir.

### Cited Findings
- **Energy-based (ideal) broad-crested weir, free and drowned**: free q = sqrt(g) ((2/3)E)^1.5; drowned q = e_D sqrt(2g (E - e_D)); both equal at e_D = (2/3)E. [Certain, N09 E4, from Rana 1D weirs and Iber] — N09 #27, #29
- **D-Flow FM energy-height Villemonte (Sieben 2011)**: Q = Q(E1, E2) with E1 = h1 + U1^2/(2g), E2 = h2 + U2^2/(2g), U1 the upstream velocity component toward the weir and U2 the downstream component away from it; Qc(E1) = (2/3)E1 sqrt((2/3) g E1) (6.240); Cd0 from vegetation, ramp slopes m1, m2 (default 4), crest length L_crest through w = exp(-(1/2) E1/L_crest) (6.244), calibration c1 (default 1.0 reproduces the Tabellenboek data); power p from Cd0, ramps and downstream ground height d1, with c2 = 10 for smooth weirs and 30 to 50 for rough ones; even critical flow differs from Qc by the factor Cd0. [Certain for the structure and parameters; equation layout partly garbled] — [D-Flow FM TRM, Sect. 6.7.6, pp. 102-104](https://content.oss.deltares.nl/delft3dfm2d3d/D-Flow_FM_Technical_Reference_Manual.pdf)
- **Villemonte (1947)** Q_sub/Q_free = [1 - (h2/h1)^n]^0.385, n = 1.5 for a rectangular sharp crest. [Likely, N09 #26]; full reference: Villemonte, J., 1947, "Submerged weir discharge studies", Engineering News-Record. [Certain, bibliographic in D-Flow FM TRM references]

- [Certain, arithmetic] **The drowned law is marginal for uniform flow across a submerged crest**: with e_D = e_U = e and E = e + u_n^2/(2g), e_D sqrt(2g(E - e_D)) = e |u_n|, exactly the actual flux. ## 4. Validation datasets for overtopping in 2D

### Takeaway
There is no single 2D overtopping benchmark with measured truth: the EA tests check that momentum overtops a low obstruction and that final levels sit at the lowest crest, and Test 7 shows packages disagreeing on bank overtopping with no exact answer. The quantitative checks have to be 1D flume laws transposed to 2D (free critical flow, the drowned curve, embankment and oblique-weir data, the free-overfall end depth) plus Wadi2D's own flume, bank runs and the Bidah crest against HEC-RAS's SA/2D weir. ## 5. Making the cap orientation-free on a staircase or an aligned strip

### Takeaway
The flux across a straight line is partitioned exactly onto staircase faces by the projection |n . e_f| (D-Flow FM's w_u), and the head that drives the crossing is the line-normal specific energy E_n = e_U + (V_U . n)^2/(2g), not the full energy (tangential momentum crosses a crest unchanged). Wadi2D's present factor is uniform per segment, 1/(|n_x| + |n_y|), which equals the projection only at 0 and 45 degrees and gives the right total only when every face of the line binds. A cap written on the line-normal component only, with the projection factor, reduces to the ordinary face cap on an aligned strip (n = e_f, factor 1) and leaves tangential flow untouched in both cases.

### Cited Findings
- D-Flow FM projects weir width onto the face: w_u = c dx_face |n_j . n_wj| (6.216), equivalently cos(alpha) ||edge|| (6.247); its Bernoulli face is valid only for flow perpendicular to the weir or uniform along it (Remark 6.7.3); its Villemonte law uses the velocity components toward and away from the weir. [Certain] — [D-Flow FM TRM, pp. 96-97, 102-106](https://content.oss.deltares.nl/delft3dfm2d3d/D-Flow_FM_Technical_Reference_Manual.pdf)
- MIKE's 2D weir width is the width perpendicular to the flow direction. [Certain] — [MIKE+ 2D Weirs](https://userdoc.mike-plus.mikepoweredbydhi.com/en/2026/2DOverland/2DOverland_HydraulicModelling/2D_Weirs/)
- HEC-RAS proportions face lengths to the true structure length; TUFLOW uses the face length. [Certain/Likely, N09 #18, #24]
- **Wadi2D's staircase factor**: each corner of the lattice walk carries a distance along the true line, distributed evenly over the steps of each polyline segment, and each edge's factor is its true length over dx. [Certain, code] — `wadi2d/terrain/crest.py` (`_walk`, `staircase`). For a straight segment with |di| + |dj| steps this is factor = sqrt(di^2 + dj^2) / (|di| + |dj|) = 1/(|n_x| + |n_y|) on every edge. [Certain, arithmetic]
- **W36**: for plain momentum faces no factor is needed, because a staircase's face fluxes sum exactly to the flow across the true line; the factor exists only for the weir law, whose discharge per metre does not know the direction; 0.71 on every face of a 45-degree crest line also cuts what flows along it. [Certain for the statement; the along-line effect tagged Likely there] — W36 README
- Oblique weir data: at low head the discharge gain tends to the crest-length ratio. [Likely] — Section 4

### Inferences
- [Certain, arithmetic] **Exact partition.** For a straight line with unit normal n, the staircase's x-faces total |n_x| ds and its y-faces |n_y| ds per length ds of line. A crossing flux q n per unit line length puts q n_x on each x-face and q n_y on each y-face (per unit face width), and the sum is q ds. The weir law per face is therefore C |n . e_f| q_max(E_n), with the factor the projection, not a uniform ratio.
- [Certain, arithmetic] **Uniform versus projection.** At 15 degrees the uniform factor is 0.816 on every face, the projection 0.966 and 0.259; at 30 degrees 0.732 against 0.866 and 0.500. When every face binds (free overfall, everywhere over-carrying) the total is right (sum = q ds) but the outflow direction is wrong; when only some faces bind, the faces nearest the line normal are throttled to 0.84 (15 degrees) or 0.85 (30 degrees) of their share. This is a small, fixable error; the projection costs one float (or an angle) per crest face.
- [Certain, arithmetic] **Tier-0 bound with no factor over-carries on a staircase in free overfall by at most cos(theta) + sin(theta)**: 1.225 at 15 degrees, 1.366 at 30, 1.414 at 45 (each face passing critical flow over its own width: the zigzag behaves like a labyrinth weir, which is also what the oblique-weir data show at low head). So a face-frame bound alone is not enough on a staircase; the line-normal law is.
- [Likely] **Line-normal head, not full energy.** Across a crest there is no force tangential to the line (friction aside), so tangential velocity crosses unchanged and its kinetic energy is not available to drive the crossing; the head for the normal flux is E_n. This is De Marchi's side-weir assumption (lateral outflow driven by the depth over the crest, the main-channel kinetic energy retained) and D-Flow FM's (velocity components toward the weir). Using the full energy over-allows lateral outflow by ((1 + (F_t^2 + F_n^2)/2) / (1 + F_n^2/2))^1.5, e.g. 1.07 and 1.28 at a tangential Froude number of 0.3 and 0.6 with no normal approach [Certain, arithmetic].
- [Likely] **Decomposition on a staircase face.** With the face velocity u_f (along e_f) and the transverse velocity v_f already formed for friction, V = u_f e_f + v_f e_f_perp; V_n = V . n, V_t = V . t; the face flux is A (V_n (n . e_f) + V_t (t . e_f)). Capping |V_n| e <= C q_max(E_n) and reassembling u_f = V_n (n . e_f) + V_t (t . e_f) caps only the crossing part. On an aligned face n = e_f, t . e_f = 0, and this is the ordinary face cap; on a future aligned strip it is exact and the tangential question disappears, because along-strip flow does not cross the strip's line faces.
- [Likely] **Upstream velocity must exclude the face itself** (D-Flow's Algorithm 45): if E uses the face's own over-carried velocity the bound becomes an identity and never binds. In Wadi2D the cell-centre discharge Qc over the cell's mean area is the continuity estimate D-Flow uses [Guessing on whether Qc already contains enough of the face to bias it].

### Gaps
- No source states how a weir law should treat flow tangential to an oblique crest on a staircase; the decomposition is my construction.
- Whether the transverse velocity at a staircase face (interpolated from y-faces that may lie across the line) is a fair estimate of V_t is untested.

## 6. The bank-line loss: does capping every obliquely crossed staircase face throttle the flood? Side-weir and oblique-weir theory

### Takeaway
Yes, and the arithmetic says why: Wadi2D's crest-line cap is depth-based (1.705 e^1.5, no velocity head), so it binds on any face whose normal Froude number exceeds (2/3)^1.5 = 0.544, which steep wadi floods exceed routinely; along a 45-degree staircase a drowned along-line flow zigzags through the line's faces at 0.71 of its speed, and the 0.71 factor cuts the cap by the same ratio, so supercritical and near-critical along-line flow is throttled even with the bank under water. An energy-head cap with the normal head cannot bind on uniform flow of any Froude number (equality only at Fr_n = 1), and side-weir theory gives the right head for lateral overflow.

### Cited Findings
- **W26 (bank lines used as crest lines)**: Bidah -9.7 % at 10 m and -21.7 % at 20 m, Faydh 01 -30.1 % and -35.5 %, 1.6 to 3.3 x the steps; the same lines a metre below the ground still cost 7.8 %, so three quarters is the crest face itself, its critical-flow cap and its step on every face a gorge flood crosses obliquely. [Certain, project record] — project `CLAUDE.md`, W26 entry
- **W36 (breaklines, no weir law)**: -1.3 / -2.2 % (Bidah 10 / 20 m), -2.4 / -11.1 % (Faydh 01); keeping the natural-crest cap on those faces: -1.8, -2.6, -5.8, -13.0 %; lifting a pixel only on a true ridge: within 0.3 % in all four. [Certain, project record] — W36 README
- **Design note**: crest lines and natural crests carry the depth-based limit |Q| <= cw A sqrt(A/W), cw 1.705; drowning follows from the momentum balance and the limit stops binding near r = 0.85. [Certain] — `design_note.md` Sect. 2
- **Side-weir theory**: De Marchi's constant specific energy; Froude-dependent coefficients (Subramanya and Awasthy 1972; Borghei et al. 1999; Hager 1987, J. Hydraul. Eng. 113(4):491-504). [Likely] — Section 4 links
- **Oblique weirs**: discharge gain tends to the length ratio at low h/P and falls with head. [Likely] — Section 4 links

### Inferences
- [Certain, arithmetic] **A depth-based cap binds on uniform flow** whenever h u_n > (2/3)^1.5 sqrt(g) h^1.5, i.e. Fr_n > 0.544. An energy-based cap with E_n = h + u_n^2/(2g) gives h u_n / q_max(E_n) = Fr_n / ((2/3)(1 + Fr_n^2/2))^1.5: 0.770 at Fr_n 0.5, 1.000 at 1.0, 0.890 at 1.5, 0.707 at 2.0. It never binds on a state that already exists upstream; it binds only on a face that tries to pass more than its upstream energy allows, which is the over-carry.
- [Likely] **The zigzag.** On a 45-degree staircase, along-line flow on either side can avoid the line's faces only by turning at every cell; when the line is drowned, x-momentum advected into a line cell pushes flow through the line's x-face to the far side and back through the next y-face. Each line face then carries about e V / sqrt(2) per unit width in alternating directions, net zero across the line. With the depth-based cap times 0.71 that flux is capped when V/sqrt(g e) > about 0.55: in a gorge at Fr 1.5 the zigzag faces pass about 36 % of what they carry, and the flood is forced to the cells either side [Certain, arithmetic for the ratio; Likely for the zigzag occurring in the solution]. This is a candidate for W26's "three quarters is the crest face itself" and for the lines-below-ground 7.8 %; it is a prediction to test, not a measurement.
- [Likely] **Banks need no weir law at all**: W36's ridge-only lift shows the bank losses vanish when the faces only carry the ground, and HEC's own table says natural high ground does not pass through critical (N09 #23). The universal energy bound (tier 0 below) is harmless there, the line law must not be applied to banks.
- [Likely] **Lateral overflow from a wadi over a low bank into a lower plain is a side weir**: the right head is the depth over the bank plus the line-normal velocity head, not the main-channel energy. Using full energy over-allows lateral outflow; using depth only under-allows normal approach flow. E_n is the one quantity right in both limits.

### Gaps
- The zigzag mechanism is inferred, not observed: no face-flux field along a W26 bank line has been inspected.
- No 2D study found that measures a weir law's effect on flow running along an oblique crest; the side-weir literature is 1D.

## 7. Recommended face law and its tests

### Takeaway
Three tiers, all evaluated per face from the upstream cell's state and orientation-free by construction: (0) on every face, a coefficient-free bound q_f <= q_max(E_f) from the upstream face-normal specific energy, which removes the 2.6 to 3.8 x over-carry and cannot throttle any flow that already exists upstream; (1) on every line face (crest line, road, dam, breakline used as a crest), the critical cap applied to the line-normal component only, C |n . e_f| q_max(E_n), always on, with the tangential component passed untouched, so free overfall is exact at any angle and drowned or along-line flow is never capped; (2) on structures with a user coefficient, tier 1 times C_d and a drowned factor S(r), off above r = 0.98. Banks stay plain breaklines (tier 0 only).

### Cited Findings
- Specific-energy maximum q_max(E) = (2/3)E sqrt((2/3) g E) as the free (critical) discharge; free when zeta_R <= z_C + (2/3)E. [Certain] — [D-Flow FM TRM eqs 6.231-6.234, 6.240](https://content.oss.deltares.nl/delft3dfm2d3d/D-Flow_FM_Technical_Reference_Manual.pdf)
- Far-field velocity estimated without the weir face, by continuity. [Certain] — D-Flow FM TRM eq. 6.224, Algorithm 45
- Width by projection |n_face . n_weir|. [Certain] — D-Flow FM TRM eqs 6.216, 6.247
- Energy head (not water level) as the default weir head in TUFLOW HPC (Method B Energy). [Certain] — [TUFLOW 2023-03](https://docs.tuflow.com/classic-hpc/release/2023-03-AF/TwoDSolv-1.html)
- Drowned curves: energy-based ideal, TUFLOW broad-crested and roadway fits; HEC 0.98 switch and oscillation at 95-100 %; D-Flow under-relaxation 0.6. [Certain] — N09 #21, #23, #25; D-Flow FM TRM eq. 6.236
- Wadi2D measured: flume 2.6 to 3.8 x with no cap, 0.977-0.997 with the cap; W8 drowning releases the cap near r = 0.85; the W26/W36 bank losses. [Certain, project records]

### Inferences

**The law.** For a face f with normal e_f (x- or y-face, or an aligned face), flow from U to D:

1. Upstream state, from the upwind cell, never from the face itself: e_U = eta_U - z_f (level over the face invert z_f); velocity vector V_U from the cell-centre discharges over the cell's mean area (continuity estimate, D-Flow 6.224). If e_U <= 0 the face is dry as now.

2. **Tier 0, every face, any regime, C = 1:**
   E_f = e_U + (V_U . e_f)^2 / (2g);  |q_f| <= q_max(E_f) = 1.7046 E_f^1.5 (per unit face width; times W for the face, A/e_U for a sub-grid section [Guessing on using A/e_U as the effective width]).
   Replaces the depth-based natural-crest cap. It cannot bind on uniform or gradually varied flow (Section 6 arithmetic) and on thin sheet flow the sub-grid level over the face invert makes it loose [Likely]; it binds only on over-carry. On an aligned face it is the full free-overfall control; on a staircase it leaves at most cos(theta) + sin(theta) of over-carry (labyrinth effect), which tier 1 removes where a line exists.

3. **Tier 1, faces of a drawn line with stored normal n (crest lines, roads, dam crests, dykes):**
   V = u_f e_f + v_f e_f_perp at the face (v_f as formed for friction); V_n = V . n, V_t = V . t;
   E_n = e_U + (V_U . n)^2 / (2g);
   cap |V_n| e_f_depth <= C q_max(E_n), with C = 1 on crest lines; then u_f = V_n (n . e_f) + V_t (t . e_f).
   The per-face share is the projection |n . e_f| (it appears through the reassembly; equivalently cap the face-normal part of the flux at C |n . e_f| q_max(E_n)). Replace the uniform 1/(|n_x|+|n_y|) factor by the projection.
   **Free-to-submerged switch: none explicit.** The cap is a ceiling on the normal part: in free flow the momentum face over-carries and the cap binds (critical flow); as the tailwater rises the momentum face's normal flux falls below the ceiling and the cap releases continuously (Wadi2D already measured release near r = 0.85 with the depth-based cap, W8). Tangential (along-line, zigzag) flow is never capped, drowned or not. If T2 below shows the released momentum face exceeding the energy-based drowned curve by more than 5 % between r = 0.67 and 0.9, add S(r) to tier 1 as in tier 2 [Guessing on whether it will].

4. **Tier 2, structures with a user coefficient (spillway, road with HDS/Bradley curve, dam crest):** tier 1 with C = C_d (spillway C/1.7046; roadway Cd 0.542 x (2/3) sqrt(2g) / 1.7046 = 0.94 of ideal [Certain, arithmetic from N09 #21]) and the drowned factor S(r), r = e_D / E_n: energy-based 2.598 r sqrt(1 - r) for r > 2/3 by default, or (1 - r^a)^b with the TUFLOW pairs (broad 8.55/0.556, roadway 21.15/0.627, ogee 6.992/0.648); the weir cap switched off above r = 0.98 (HEC), and Delta of the cap under-relaxed (alpha 0.6, D-Flow) only if T2 oscillates.

5. **Staircase versus aligned faces.** Staircase: n from the polyline segment, projection share, decomposition as above. Aligned face or future aligned strip: n = e_f, share 1, V_t not crossing; tiers 1 and 2 reduce to the standard face weir with face-normal energy head. Mesh refinement faces: the same law per sub-face.

6. **Alternative to keep in reserve (D-Flow scheme 6):** on line faces only, use the downwind crest depth h_f = max(e_D, (2/3)E_n) for the area and floor the downstream level in the drive at z_c + (2/3)E_n, with Bernoulli advection on that face.

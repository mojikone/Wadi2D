## Q1. Model by model: mesh type, how breaklines enter, face elevation and properties, overtopping, flow along the line, known weaknesses

### Takeaway
Two families. Aligners (HEC-RAS, ICM, ANUGA, TUFLOW FV with SMS meshes, MIKE FM meshes) put cell faces on the line, so the face profile is the crest itself. Lattice models (TUFLOW Classic/HPC, 3Di, SFINCS, D-Flow FM fixed weirs on Cartesian nets, MIKE dikes on any mesh) leave the mesh alone. They pick the faces whose centre-to-centre link crosses the line and raise each one to the line's elevation, which gives the same half-cell staircase Wadi2D has. Only D-Flow FM publishes both a correction for oblique crest width and a staggered-scheme treatment of the crest that floors the face depth at critical depth.

### Cited Findings

#### HEC-RAS 6.x 2D (unstructured, up to 8 faces per cell, implicit solvers)
- [Certain] Breaklines go "to any location that is a barrier to flow, or controls flow/direction" (levees, roads, natural berms). Mesh generation "tries to snap" faces to breaklines, but cells "may not always have cell faces that are aligned perfectly". The fix is *Enforce Break line*, which deletes the cell centres in a buffer around the line and regenerates cells along it. — [HEC-RAS 2D User's Manual, Development of the 2D computational mesh](https://www.hec.usace.army.mil/confluence/rasdocs/r2dum/latest/development-of-a-2d-or-combined-1d-2d-model/development-of-the-2d-computational-mesh)
- [Certain] Per-breakline attributes: *Near Spacing* (cell size on both sides of the line), *Near Repeats* (extra rows of that size; the example, 50 ft with 2 repeats, gives two extra rows each side), *Far Spacing* (the size grows outward, "roughly doubles", to the nominal size), and *Enforce 1 Cell Protection Radius* (stops later breaklines moving or deleting the cells next to this one). By default the cell size along a breakline is the 2D area's nominal size. — same page
- [Certain] Enforcement is sequential and order-dependent: "Cells are created along break lines one at a time". In the manual's example the bank lines overrode a centreline, and the fix was to enforce the centreline last. Refinement-region perimeters act "much like a break line", with points "offset by ½ of the spacing value". Cell Spacing Y is "[NOT IMPLEMENTED YET]". — same page
- [Certain] Mesh failures caused by breaklines: more than 8 faces per cell, highlighted red. Collinear faces, usually from "placing two or more break lines parallel to each other, and close together". The manual's verdict on those: "the software will run, but the computation across cell faces that are like this will not be correct". Short faces are collapsed by *Min Face Length Ratio* (default 0.05; 0.1 to 0.15 "may give better results"). — same page
- [Certain, from N03 findings 10-11] Once faces are aligned, the barrier is the terrain profile along the face. There is no separate crest object: the face minimum elevation blocks flow until it is exceeded.
- [Likely] Practitioner reports: a misaligned face lets a levee "leak" before it is overtopped. The remedy is to enforce the breakline and reduce its spacing step by step, re-enforcing each time, until every face lies on the line. — [Kleinschmidt, 2D Mesh "Leaking" Part 2](https://kleinschmidtgroup.com/ras-post/2d-mesh-leaking-part-2-2d-area-breaklines); [CivilGEO, Troubleshooting 2D fragmented inundation](https://knowledge.civilgeo.com/troubleshooting-2d-fragmented-inundation)
- [Certain, from N09 findings 22-24] Overtopping across a breakline face is computed by the 2D equations, with no weir law. A weir law applies only where an SA/2D connection is drawn. One method is chosen per structure, with no automatic switch. HEC warns that the 2D equations "over-predict" free-fall flow.
- [Likely] HEC-RAS 2025 (new interface) has a new mesher built from arcs and regions, like SMS. An arc inside a region becomes a breakline, and each region can be meshed with quadrilateral or triangular cells. It also has an explicit solver. Read only in a conference poster summary; the poster itself was too large to fetch. — [CWEMF 2024, RAS 2025 Mesh Generation / Explicit Solver](https://cwemf.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/18.6-RAS-2025-Mesh-Gneration-Explicit-Solver.pdf)

#### TUFLOW Classic and HPC (square grid, optional quadtree since 2020, SGS)
- [Certain] Cell selection for lines uses a cross-hair test: cells are selected "only if an input GIS line intersects these imaginary cross-hairs" (mid-side to mid-side). Quadtree uses a "spider web" test (centre to each connected face) to avoid gaps at changes of level, and it "will look identical to the cross-hair approach" where the cell size is uniform. — [TUFLOW Manual 2026.3, s.7.2.5.1-7.2.5.2](https://docs.tuflow.com/classic-hpc/manual/2026.3/06-2D-Domains.html)
- [Certain] A Zpt takes its elevation from "the perpendicular intersection of that Zpt with the line", or from the nearest vertex if there is none. Thin lines modify ZH, ZU, ZV. Thick lines (up to 1.5 cells wide) also modify ZC. Wide lines set every Zpt in a buffer of half the width. Z shape polygons set every Zpt inside by TIN interpolation. Polygons are processed first, then wide lines, then thin and thick lines. — same manual, s.7.2.6.2 and 7.2.6.2.1
- [Likely, inference from the rule above] A face up to half a cell from the line therefore receives the line's elevation, whatever the ground under that face. The documented RIDGE/MAX option only prevents lowering. This is structurally Wadi2D's gorge problem (1): TUFLOW would put a bank line's high-wall elevation onto faces inside a narrow gorge in the same way.
- [Certain] Layered flow constrictions (2d_lfcsh) act by "adjusting the flow width of the 2D cell". Each of three layers has its own blockage and form loss (FLC). For thin lines, FLC "is applied to the cell faces unchanged"; for thick lines it "is divided by two". A 100 % blockage keeps "a minimum cell face flow width factor of 0.001". — same manual, s.7.2.9.3, Table 7.22
- [Certain] For TUFLOW Classic, weir flow only occurs "if the bed slope is adverse" to the water-surface slope when Supercritical is ON (the default). The regime is picked by comparing upstream- and downstream-controlled flows, preferring the lower, plus a Froude check. Free Overfall ON allows weir flow on both slopes. — [TUFLOW Manual 2025.2, 7.4 Classic Specific](https://docs.tuflow.com/classic-hpc/manual/2025.2/CLASpecific-2.html) (read in search excerpt; full rules in N09 finding 19)
- [Certain] HPC: the full weir equation on thin and thick breaklines is the default from 2023-03. Method A is the old water-level-gradient limiter; Method B uses the water level; Method B Energy, the default, uses the upstream energy level. Separate thin and thick parameter sets, defaults 0.577, 1.5, 8.55, 0.556. "WrF is not equivalent to Cf." — TUFLOW Manual 2026.3 s.7.3.4 (URL above). The manual text read contains no definition of W beyond N02 finding 33 ("the length of the weir face") and no statement on diagonal crests.
- [Certain] HPC's improvements listed for the EA benchmarks: mid-side elevation points allow "the specification of thin breaklines (i.e. same as TUFLOW Classic)", and "TUFLOW GPU, TUFLOW FV and other 2D schemes typically use one elevation per element". HPC also switches automatically to upstream-controlled regimes (weir or supercritical) across cell mid-sides. — [TUFLOW EA benchmark update 2019, p.91-92](https://tuflow.com/media/5019/2019-uk-ea-2d-benchmarking-results-update-latest-tuflow-solvers.pdf)
- [Likely] A 2012 TUFLOW demonstration tested a 2D weir at 45 degrees to the grid against the broad-crested formula. The 2D result "could be corrected using a weir coefficient", i.e. the staircase error was absorbed by calibration rather than geometry. Read in search summary only; the PDF exceeds the fetch limit. — [TUFLOW 2012 FMA demonstration session](https://www.tuflow.com/media/4946/201209-tuflow-special-demonstration-session-fma-usa.pdf)
- [Likely] TUFLOW recommends Z Shapes over the older Z Line layer, whose development has ceased (read in a search summary of the TUFLOW wiki). — [TUFLOW wiki, Tutorial M02](https://wiki.tuflow.com/Tutorial_M02)

#### TUFLOW FV (flexible mesh, usually built in SMS)
- [Certain] Structures are attached to nodestrings: "each polyline is snapped to the nearest cell faces along its path and converted to a nodestring" (checked in `_ns_check_L`). The default is "the broad crested weir equation with submergence", with C = 1.705 and Ex = 1.5, i.e. ideal critical flow in SI. *Walls* block selected cell faces, with "levees, flood barriers and solid training walls" as the example uses. Culverts on a nodestring do "not allow embankment overtopping in the 2D domain"; with linked nodestrings "the bathymetry between the nodestrings may be raised to represent the embankment". — [TUFLOW FV Manual 2026.0, 5.17 Hydraulic Structures](https://docs.tuflow.com/fv/manual/2026.0/HD2D-HS-2.html)
- [Likely] Weir width defaults to the nodestring length for nodestring structures. A user width B is available since 2025.1, for linked nodestrings only. — [TUFLOW FV 2025.1 release notes](https://docs.tuflow.com/fv/release/2025.1.0/HD-1.html) (search excerpt)
- [Certain] TUFLOW FV, like most flexible-mesh schemes, uses "one elevation per element", so a crest must be resolved by mesh edges or by a structure. — TUFLOW EA benchmark update 2019 (URL above)

#### MIKE 21 FM / MIKE+ 2D overland (flexible triangles and quads, explicit)
- [Certain] A dike is a polyline with a crest Z per point, treated as "an internal discharge boundary across a section of faces". A face is included "when the line between the two element centres of the faces crosses" the dike. Each face uses a weir expression reduced by the Villemonte formula. The upstream level is the higher of the two element levels. Default C = 1.838. The continuity flux is "corrected to conserve mass". A *dampening delta depth* (default 0.01 m) damps the discharge gradient at small head differences, and should be raised if the structure oscillates. Time-varying crests come from a dfs1. — [MIKE+ 2026, 2D Dikes](https://userdoc.mike-plus.mikepoweredbydhi.com/en/2026/2DOverland/2DOverland_HydraulicModelling/2D_Dikes/)
- [Certain] DHI: the program "aligns the structure to the nearest cell faces" and "derives the flow across the dike by the water levels in the two adjacent elements". A dike section that falls on a boundary face "will not come into action". — [DHI KA-01021](https://support.dhigroup.com/knowledgebase/article/KA-01021/en-us)
- [Certain, arithmetic] 1.838 in SI equals 3.33 US x 0.5521: the Francis sharp-crested value, 8 % above ideal critical flow (1.705). The exponent sits in an equation image that was not read; 1.5 is [Likely].
- [Likely] MIKE+ 2D weirs offer Broad Crested, Villemonte and Honma. Honma uses the mean level of the wet elements beside the weir faces. — [MIKE+ 2026, 2D Weirs](https://userdoc.mike-plus.mikepoweredbydhi.com/en/2026/2DOverland/2DOverland_HydraulicModelling/2D_Weirs/) (search excerpt)

#### InfoWorks ICM 2D (Shewchuk Triangle mesh, aggregated elements, optional subgrid sampling since 2026)
- [Certain] "Base linear structure, building, porous wall and porous polygon lines are always enforced as mesh element edges". General lines with a category act as permeable break lines. Mesh-zone and mesh-level-zone boundaries are enforced as break lines. Element ground level is "the average of the sample point levels" over sub-triangles down to the minimum element area or the DEM resolution. Terrain-sensitive meshing splits a triangle when its height range exceeds a limit, but with at most 64 samples per triangle it may "not detect narrow linear features such as embankments or channels". Clip meshing is about 4 x faster than classic and stable to local edits. — [ICM 2027, 2D Mesh Generation Methodology](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2027/ENU/ICM-Help/files/GUID-24EB7F2A-778D-4815-8F54-4DE299B8D532.htm)
- [Certain] Base Linear Structure (2D) types are Wall (optional porosity, infinite crest, collapse trigger), Weir (broad-crested or thin-plate, Cd default 0.85 "corresponds to a weir of width equal to that of the approach channel", typical 0.2 to 3.0) and User control (head-unit-flow table). The crest is Infinite, Height, Level or Irregular (X, Y, Z section). With *Height*, the crest "is the height above the highest of the ground levels of the two computational elements" either side. There is a lateral friction coefficient for flow along the structure. — [ICM 2026, Base Linear Structure (2D) Data Fields](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2026/ENU/ICMLIVE-Help/files/GUID-1178A2A1-7294-41D3-B939-000A2CBD494B.htm)
- [Likely] Flow is computed face by face: the unblocked part of a face by "a standard weir equation based on the crest level of the face", the blocked part as a wall. The 1D broad-crested weir is deemed drowned above a modular ratio of 66 %. — [ICM 2027, Base Linear Structures (2D)](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2027/ENU/ICM-Help/files/GUID-28633F80-A9FE-472D-B912-E9AE5B4C5B04.htm) (search excerpt)
- [Certain] Subgrid Sampling (clip meshing only) builds a volume-level table per element. It "only accounts for topographic barriers on the boundaries of the elements"; a crest through an element centre lets flow "tunnel". The advice is breaklines "along any significant topographic crests", subgrid triangles at least ten times smaller than the element, and elements smaller than the river bed. Reported speed: GPU "about 5-10 times faster", CPU "40-80 times". It "can sometimes lead to over-rapid propagation speeds" for unimpeded downhill flow; for rainfall on hills, use it only in a mesh zone. With SGS, a height-based structure's crest is measured from the higher of the face ground level and the adjacent table minimum. — [ICM 2027, Using Subgrid Sampling](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2027/ENU/ICM-Help/files/GUID-0AB4AD8A-99DD-4AB1-9E27-083F538B3AE0.htm)

#### 3Di / Rana (quadtree, pixel subgrid, Stelling-type staggered scheme)
- [Certain] The face cross-section and exchange level come from the pixel rows either side of the edge: "For each pixel pair, the maximum value is used". A narrow obstacle not on an edge "will not affect the cross-section and exchange level", so water passes. Blocking elements are detected only "when these are located at the cell edges". — [Rana docs, 2D surface flow](https://docs.ranawaterintelligence.com/h_surfaceflow.html)
- [Certain] Explicit obstacles are lines with a crest level. The grid builder finds the 2D flowlines they intersect, and "the obstacle's crest level is used as exchange level, overriding" the DEM value. An obstacle not on an edge "affects the exchange level of the nearest cell edge". Flags `affects_2d`, `affects_1d2d_open_water` and `affects_1d2d_closed` set which link types are raised. Grid refinement is offered as the alternative, at the cost of more cells. A limiter for the water-level gradient "may be used" at steep steps. — same page; [Rana docs, 2D objects](https://docs.ranawaterintelligence.com/d_2d_objects.html)
- [Certain] Refinement areas and lines: level 1 is the minimum cell size and "each increase by 1 doubles the cell size". Refinement lines are better buffered into areas. — 2D objects page (URL above)
- [Certain, from N09 finding 28] No 2D weir law: the momentum equation carries the flow over a raised exchange level.
- [Likely] Stelling's quadtree subgrid method is in Stelling (2012), Proc. ICE Water Management 165(10) 567-580. A later Stelling paper notes that "flow blocking obstacles, like thin floodwalls, may be overlooked by subgrid methods". Both read only as citations or search excerpts. — [doi:10.1680/wama.12.00018](https://doi.org/10.1680/wama.12.00018)

#### D-Flow FM / D-HYDRO (unstructured orthogonal mixed meshes, staggered: levels at cells, normal velocity at faces)
- [Certain] "Weirs are discretely represented along mesh lines ... no computational cells are cut by a weir". A fixed weir is subgrid: "a weir is not 'modelled on the grid', but ... a parametrization is applied". There are three options: `fixedweirscheme=6` numerical, `=8` Tabellenboek, `=9` Villemonte, the last two "taken from the Simona software". — [D-Flow FM Technical Reference Manual, s.6.7](https://content.oss.deltares.nl/delft3dfm2d3d/D-Flow_FM_Technical_Reference_Manual.pdf)
- [Certain] Grid snapping: "If a thin dam or fixed weir intersects with a flow link, then this object will be snapped to the corresponding net link". The crest level is interpolated along the polyline. Where several weirs hit a link, the highest crest wins. A crest below the bed is raised to the bed. The lowest toe level (*teenhoogte*) is kept each side, and the ground height (crest minus toe) is the weir height. — same, s.6.7.7
- [Certain] Oblique weirs: the face width is projected, wu_j = c Δx_j |n_j · nw_j| (eq. 6.216), equivalently wu_j = cos(α)·|Edge|_j (eq. 6.247) with α the angle between weir and edge. The wet area is reduced by the same factor. c is *FixedWeirContraction* (pillars and the like). Deltares' own remark: "Oblique weirs are not fully understood at this moment." — same, s.6.7.1 and 6.7.7
- [Certain] Numerical scheme 6. The face bed levels are raised to the crest (bl = max(z_c, bl)). Faces of neighbouring cells get advection type 4, which uses only inflowing velocities. On the weir face, Bernoulli is imposed from the upstream cell to the face, with "a downwind approximation" for the depth on the crest: hu_j = max(ζ_R − z_c, 2/3 E_j), where E_j = ζ_L − z_c + u_in²/2g. This adds −(g/Δx)·max(0, 2/3 E_j − (ζ_R − z_c)) to the face momentum equation. The text states that the contraction upstream "is governed by energy conservation" and the expansion downstream "by momentum conservation". The expression is valid only for flow "perpendicular to the weir (1D flows) or ... uniform along both sides". Scheme 4 projects the far-field velocity on the weir normal; scheme 5 drops the kinetic term. — same, s.6.7.2-6.7.3, eq. 6.217-6.223, Algorithms 41-44
- [Certain] Empirical schemes 8 and 9 add an energy loss −gΔE/Δx to the face momentum. Tabellenboek uses Carnot below 0.25 m/s on the crest, table interpolation above 0.50 m/s, and a blend between. Villemonte: Q = Cd0·Qc(E1)·f(E2/E1, p), with Qc = (2/3)E1·sqrt((2/3) g E1), ramp slopes m1 = m2 = 4 by default, a vegetation term, and calibration c1 = 1. In critical flow the loss is set so that the discharge "converge[s] to the empirical discharge over a small period of time". *FixedweirRelaxationcoef*, default 0.6, blends the new loss with the old for stability. The input carries dikes, groynes and steps "sometimes" in more than a million lines (Rijkswaterstaat models). — same, s.6.7.5-6.7.7
- [Certain] Moving from WAQUA to D-Flow FM, the change in weir formulation alone raised Waal water levels by several centimetres; a test without weirs closed the gap to about 2 cm. — [ten Hagen, U. Twente MSc thesis](https://essay.utwente.nl/fileshare/file/66056/HagenEDMten_1009230_openbaar.pdf)
- [Likely] Stelling and Duinmeijer (2003) is the origin of this staggered treatment: "in flow expansions, the approximation is consistent with the momentum principle, while in contractions it is consistent with the Bernoulli equation". Kramer and Stelling (2008) extended it to unstructured meshes, and that extension underlies the HEC-RAS SWE-EM advection (N03 finding 17). Abstract only. — [Stelling and Duinmeijer 2003, IJNMF 43(12), doi:10.1002/fld.537](https://cir.nii.ac.jp/crid/1362262945010575872)

#### SFINCS (regular or quadtree staggered grid, local inertial, subgrid since v2.1)
- [Certain] The interface depth in the base scheme is "the difference between the maximum water level in the two adjacent cells and the maximum bed level in these cells". With subgrid, uv-point tables of mean depth, roughness and wet fraction use pixels from both sides A and B of the face, and "no water is allowed to flow when z_u drops below z_min". Narrow blocking features go in as a "subgrid weir formulation" that must be created in pre-processing; "to date, these features have been manually identified". A straight channel is within 2 % at 5 to 500 m cells. An unresolved meander runs +51 % at 500 m (Q_m/Q_r ≈ Ω^1.5). Speed-up 35 to 50 x at 38 to 128 % extra cost per cell. — [van Ormondt et al. 2025, GMD 18, 843](https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/18/843/2025/gmd-18-843-2025.html)
- [Certain] Thin dams snap to faces and zero only the normal flux. Weirs (x, y, z, Cd per point) are free when h2 <= 2/3 h1, with q = Cd·1.7049·h1^1.5, and submerged otherwise, with q = Cd·h2·sqrt(2g(h1 − h2)). Cd "0.6 is a typical value". SFINCS "multiplies by the length of the weir segment inside each grid cell", with at most 2 structure u/v points per cell. The crest can be taken from the DEM (HydroMT `dep`) plus an offset `dz`. — [SFINCS docs, Structures](https://sfincs.readthedocs.io/en/latest/input_structures.html)
- [Certain, arithmetic] The two SFINCS branches meet at h2 = 2/3 h1: Cd·(2/3)h1·sqrt(2g h1/3) = Cd·1.705 h1^1.5.

#### ANUGA (unstructured triangles, finite volume)
- [Certain] A riverwall is "an infinitely-thin wall placed along mesh edges". Its polylines are passed as breaklines when the mesh is built, which "forces triangle edges to align exactly with the wall". The crest is interpolated linearly per segment, one crest per edge. On riverwall edges the solver swaps the Riemann flux for a Villemonte weir blended with the shallow-water flux, with s = tailwater head / headwater head and h = tailwater head / weir height. The weir dominates for s < s1 and h < h1; the shallow-water solution takes over for s > s2 or h > h2. Defaults: Qfactor 1.0, s1 0.9, s2 0.95, h1 1.0, h2 1.5. Optional seepage below the crest (Cd_through, default 0). Discontinuous-elevation algorithms only. No accuracy figures are given. — [ANUGA docs, Riverwalls](https://anuga.readthedocs.io/en/develop/setup_anuga_script/riverwalls.html)

#### Iber / Iber+ (unstructured finite volume, Roe-type, GPU in Iber+)
- [Certain] Internal weir conditions use Q = Cdw·B·(Zu − Zw)^1.5 with a submerged switch at 0.67 (N09 finding 27).
- [Certain] Flume test, 194 runs on 9 weirs, Iber at 4.31 cm² elements near the weir. With the weir as topography, mean relative error in head is 5.0 to 22.9 % (MAE 2.9 to 11.4 mm). With the weir as an internal condition it is 0.9 to 5.6 % (MAE 0.4 to 2.4 mm). As topography, the model "tends to overestimate the headwater depth" in free flow and underestimates it once the weir is submerged. Neither approach reproduces the local surface pattern at oblique weirs. — [García-Alén et al. 2021, Water 13, 2152](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/water/water-13-02152/article_deploy/water-13-02152.pdf)

#### SRH-2D, TELEMAC-2D, BASEMENT, RiverFlow2D, Flood Modeller 2D (lighter evidence)
- [Likely] SRH-2D: a weir is two arcs (upstream and downstream faces). The cells between them are disabled and the flow is passed by a weir equation with a submergence factor; no reverse flow in the weir tutorial. Quadrilateral rows are recommended immediately up- and downstream. — [xmswiki, SMS:SRH-2D Structures](https://xmswiki.com/wiki/SMS:SRH_Gate); [SRH-2D error message tables](https://www.xmswiki.com/wiki/User:Jcreer/SRH-2D_Error_Messages_Tables) (search excerpts)
- [Likely] TELEMAC-2D: the weir "is not constructed directly in the grid". The discharge is Poleni's (subroutine `clsing.f`), and normal velocities either side are derived from it. A 2023 HR Wallingford method defines all obstacles from georeferenced lines (sharp or broad crest, V-shaped, tilted, submerged, reversible) without node numbers. — [TELEMAC user conference paper](https://core.ac.uk/download/477676123.pdf); [HR Wallingford, simplified approach to obstacles](https://eprints.hrwallingford.com/1597) (search excerpts)
- [Likely] BASEMENT: BASEmesh takes one line shapefile of boundaries and internal breaklines, which must snap and close. Nothing found on weirs at element edges. — [hydro-informatics, BASEmesh pre-processing](https://hydro-informatics.github.io/numerics/pre-qgis-bm.html)
- [Likely] RiverFlow2D group (Echeverribar et al., Zaragoza): levees as internal boundary conditions on a weir law avoid "very fine meshes or small time steps". "Each pair of cells sharing an edge on that internal line" becomes internal-boundary cells, so the levee follows edges. — [Echeverribar et al., Use of internal boundary conditions for levees representation](https://zaguan.unizar.es/record/86508) (search excerpt; page refused fetch)
- [Likely] Flood Modeller 2D (ADI, TVD, FAST, GPU): Z-Lines set elevations along a polyline from points snapped to its vertices. — [Flood Modeller help, Z-Lines](https://help.floodmodeller.com/docs/z-lines) (search excerpt)
- [Likely] PARFLOOD (Parma; GPU, block-uniform quadtree, the precedent for Wadi2D's tiles): internal boundary conditions for bridges and flow-through dams, at a physical-to-computational time ratio of about 20 to 30 on about 10^6 cells. Full text refused fetch. — [Dazzi, Vacondio, Mignosa 2020, Adv. Water Resources 137, 103525](https://air.unipr.it/retrieve/c5eec033-554f-4cac-b0e6-090cd0bbf5a6/paper_IBC_SWEGPU_REV_V01_REV_ACCETTATE.pdf)

### Inferences
- [Likely] MIKE (element-centre line crossing), 3Di (intersected flowlines), D-Flow FM (intersected flow links), SFINCS (snapped u/v points) and TUFLOW (cross-hair, perpendicular projection) all use one selection rule in effect: a face is raised when its dual link crosses the line. On a square lattice this gives the same staircase within half a cell that Wadi2D uses. Wadi2D's rasterisation is not the outlier; its gorge loss (problem 1) is the known cost of the lattice family.
- [Likely] None of the lattice models documents sampling the local ground under a face instead of the line's elevation. Two documented mechanisms come closest to a "ridge-only" rule. ICM's Height crest is measured from the higher of the two adjacent element grounds, so it is always a ridge relative to both sides. D-Flow FM's toe and ground height define the weir only where the crest stands above its toes. Both match Wadi2D's W36 diagnostic, in which a ridge-only lift brought all four cases within 0.3 %.

### Gaps
- Exact selection of ZU/ZV sides for an oblique thin Z line in TUFLOW, and the definition of W in HPC on a staircase. The manual excerpt read has neither, and the 2012 45-degree weir test could not be read (PDF over the size limit).
- HEC-RAS 2025: details of the new mesher and the explicit solver (poster not fetched).
- BASEMENT and Flood Modeller 2D weir and crest handling at cell edges: no primary text found.
- MIKE's dike exponent and whether each face's width is projected for oblique dikes: equation images not readable.

## Q2. On a square or quadtree grid, how does each model make a diagonal barrier watertight without a staircase error in the flood section, and what accuracy does it report?

### Takeaway
Making the barrier watertight is solved the same way everywhere: raise every face whose link crosses the line. The staircase length error has three published fixes: D-Flow FM projects each face width onto the weir normal, SFINCS uses the weir segment length inside each cell, and HEC proportions face lengths to the true length (N09 finding 24). TUFLOW absorbs the error into a calibration coefficient. No vendor publishes an accuracy figure for a diagonal crest on a lattice.

### Cited Findings
- [Certain] D-Flow FM: wu_j = c Δx_j |n_j · nw_j|, and "Oblique weirs are not fully understood at this moment." — [D-Flow FM TRM s.6.7.1](https://content.oss.deltares.nl/delft3dfm2d3d/D-Flow_FM_Technical_Reference_Manual.pdf)
- [Certain, arithmetic] For a straight crest of length L at angle θ on a square lattice, the staircase holds x-normal faces of total length L|sin θ| and y-normal faces of total length L|cos θ|. Against the weir normal (−sin θ, cos θ), the projected widths add to L sin²θ + L cos²θ = L exactly, at every angle. So the D-Flow FM projection removes the 1.414 overcount at 45 degrees (N09 finding 35) with no geometry beyond the line's direction per face.
- [Certain] SFINCS computes q per unit width and "multiplies by the length of the weir segment inside each grid cell", with at most two structure u/v points per cell. — [SFINCS docs, Structures](https://sfincs.readthedocs.io/en/latest/input_structures.html)
- [Likely] TUFLOW: 45-degree 2D weir corrected "using a weir coefficient" (2012 demonstration, search excerpt). — [TUFLOW 2012](https://www.tuflow.com/media/4946/201209-tuflow-special-demonstration-session-fma-usa.pdf)
- [Certain] 3Di raises exchange levels on intersected flowlines and has no width or length correction in the documentation read. — [Rana docs, 2D surface flow](https://docs.ranawaterintelligence.com/h_surfaceflow.html)
- [Certain] Watertightness failures documented: ICM subgrid "tunnelling" when a crest passes through an element centre ([ICM 2027 SGS](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2027/ENU/ICM-Help/files/GUID-0AB4AD8A-99DD-4AB1-9E27-083F538B3AE0.htm)); 3Di narrow obstacles off the edges do not block ([Rana](https://docs.ranawaterintelligence.com/h_surfaceflow.html)); MIKE dike sections on boundary faces are inactive ([DHI KA-01021](https://support.dhigroup.com/knowledgebase/article/KA-01021/en-us)); TUFLOW SGS crests not on faces sample lower ground (N02 finding 31; N09 finding 20).
- [Certain] EA Test 7 on a 20 m grid: the final level behind a bank should equal its lowest point, 12.13 m. Two packages hit it and others ran "up to ~12cm too high". The low point "was localised and between two higher points ~70m apart (a small distance compared to the grid resolution of 20m)". — [EA 2010, SC080035/SR2, Test 7](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a75765c40f0b6360e4744dd/scho0510bsno-e-e.pdf)

### Inferences
- [Likely] The projection fixes the crossing flow but, if applied to the conveyance of the same face, also throttles any flow component along the line. D-Flow FM reduces the wet area by the same factor, and states its crest formula is valid only for flow perpendicular to the weir. That is the mechanism of Wadi2D problem (3): a bank line runs parallel to the flood, so the flood's along-channel component crosses staircase faces carrying the 0.71 factor and the weir cap. The projection belongs on the weir (critical) branch of a face, never on its normal conveyance.
- [Likely] TUFLOW's W = face length leaves a diagonal crest over-carrying by up to 41 % when free, but does not throttle along-line flow. D-Flow FM's projection is exact across the line but throttles along it. Neither is right for a line that is sometimes crossed and sometimes followed. Wadi2D must decide per face, by the local flow direction or by line purpose (crest against bank).

### Gaps
- No published test of a 45-degree crest on a lattice against a resolved crest, from any vendor, was found. The cheapest discriminating test remains Wadi2D's own: the same crest at 0, 22.5 and 45 degrees, free and submerged.

## Q3. Which models align faces to breaklines and which keep a lattice and adjust face elevations, and what does each approach cost in run time and time step?

### Takeaway
Aligners: HEC-RAS (enforced breaklines), ICM (structures always mesh edges), ANUGA (breaklines force edges), MIKE FM and TUFLOW FV (meshed in the mesher, structures snapped to the nearest faces), D-Flow FM (weirs "along mesh lines"; orthogonal meshes drawn to follow dikes). Lattice: TUFLOW Classic/HPC, 3Di, SFINCS, LISFLOOD-FP. Aligning costs mesh complexity, short faces and small cells. Lattice crest objects cost nothing in cells or time step; refinement costs both.

### Cited Findings
- [Certain] HEC-RAS: near spacing and repeats put the line's cell size on each side; collinear faces "will not be correct"; Min Face Length Ratio 0.05 merges short faces. — [HEC-RAS mesh page](https://www.hec.usace.army.mil/confluence/rasdocs/r2dum/latest/development-of-a-2d-or-combined-1d-2d-model/development-of-the-2d-computational-mesh)
- [Certain, from N03 finding 21] HEC-RAS Courant guidance: SWE-ELM up to 3, SWE-EM up to 1, Diffusion Wave up to 5. Small cells along breaklines cost time chiefly under SWE-EM.
- [Certain] ICM aggregates triangles below the minimum element area. Clip meshing avoids Delaunay near nearly coincident objects and aggregates the small triangles instead. — [ICM 2027 mesh methodology](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2027/ENU/ICM-Help/files/GUID-24EB7F2A-778D-4815-8F54-4DE299B8D532.htm)
- [Certain] 3Di: refining instead of using an obstacle means "more cells, so this may slow down the simulation". — [Rana docs](https://docs.ranawaterintelligence.com/h_surfaceflow.html)
- [Likely] Levee internal boundary conditions avoid "very fine meshes or small time steps" (RiverFlow2D group). — [Echeverribar et al.](https://zaguan.unizar.es/record/86508)
- [Certain] TUFLOW thin lines change only face and corner Zpts, with no cells added and cell storage unchanged (N02 finding 29). TUFLOW quadtree costs about 20 % at a single level and pays from three levels (N02 finding 46).
- [Certain] SFINCS subgrid costs 38 to 128 % per cell but runs 35 to 50 x faster through coarser cells. — [van Ormondt et al. 2025](https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/18/843/2025/gmd-18-843-2025.html)
- [Certain] ICM SGS: GPU about 5 to 10 x, CPU 40 to 80 x faster than non-subgrid at equal triangle resolution. — [ICM 2027 SGS](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2027/ENU/ICM-Help/files/GUID-0AB4AD8A-99DD-4AB1-9E27-083F538B3AE0.htm)
- [Certain] D-Flow FM: no cell is cut by a weir, so continuity is unchanged; only the face geometry and momentum change. — [D-Flow FM TRM s.6.7](https://content.oss.deltares.nl/delft3dfm2d3d/D-Flow_FM_Technical_Reference_Manual.pdf)

### Inferences
- [Likely] For an explicit solver, dt scales with the smallest cell, so an aligned mesh with half-size cells along every bank costs about 2 x in step count on top of the cell count. A lattice with face-crest objects costs nothing in dt unless the crest treatment itself creates fast shallow flow. Wadi2D's tripled step count with weir-capped bank lines (problem 3) is not explained by any source read. [Guessing] It is likely critical-velocity jets on faces with almost no depth.
- [Likely] An aligned mesh is incompatible with Wadi2D's 32 x 32 tiles without a new engine (decision 4, W3 rejected snapped faces at +29 %); the published evidence does not argue for changing that.

### Gaps
- No source quantifies the run-time penalty of enforced breaklines in HEC-RAS or of edge-aligned meshes in MIKE or ICM on a real model.

## Q4. How does each model stop a free overfall from over-carrying at an abrupt crest, and how does it switch to submerged flow?

### Takeaway
Four strategies. (a) An explicit weir law with a submergence function and a threshold or blend: TUFLOW HPC, MIKE, ICM, SFINCS, ANUGA, Iber, TELEMAC, SRH-2D, HEC SA/2D connections. (b) A critical-depth floor inside the staggered face momentum: D-Flow FM scheme 6, after Stelling and Duinmeijer. (c) A gradient limiter: TUFLOW HPC Method A (superseded) and 3Di. (d) Nothing: HEC-RAS breakline faces, which HEC says over-predict free fall. For a staggered solver, (b) is the documented route that keeps the momentum equation and needs no switch.

### Cited Findings
- [Certain] D-Flow FM scheme 6: hu = max(ζ_R − z_c, 2/3 E). The downstream level "should have no effect on the fixed weir in supercritical conditions", and in that case the crest depth "reached its minimum value of 2/3 E". — [D-Flow FM TRM eq. 6.217-6.221](https://content.oss.deltares.nl/delft3dfm2d3d/D-Flow_FM_Technical_Reference_Manual.pdf)
- [Certain, arithmetic] In steady free flow, Bernoulli from the upstream cell to the face with hu = 2/3 E gives u² = 2gE/3, so q = (2/3)E·sqrt(2gE/3) = sqrt(g)(2E/3)^1.5 = 1.705 E^1.5 (SI), exactly critical flow. When ζ_R − z_c exceeds 2/3 E the floor drops out, the face reverts to the ordinary pressure gradient, and submergence is continuous with no threshold to tune.
- [Certain] TUFLOW HPC Method B Energy: weir equation on upstream energy head, with Villemonte-type submergence (N02 finding 32, N09 finding 18). Method A, the earlier water-level-gradient limiter, was replaced.
- [Certain] ANUGA blends a Villemonte weir and the shallow-water flux, with weights from s (0.9 to 0.95) and h (1.0 to 1.5). — [ANUGA Riverwalls](https://anuga.readthedocs.io/en/develop/setup_anuga_script/riverwalls.html)
- [Certain] MIKE dikes: Villemonte-reduced weir, plus a dampening depth (0.01 m) against oscillation. — [MIKE+ 2D Dikes](https://userdoc.mike-plus.mikepoweredbydhi.com/en/2026/2DOverland/2DOverland_HydraulicModelling/2D_Dikes/)
- [Certain] SFINCS: free and submerged branches switch at h2 = 2/3 h1 and are continuous there. — [SFINCS Structures](https://sfincs.readthedocs.io/en/latest/input_structures.html)
- [Certain] D-Flow FM empirical schemes damp the switch with *FixedweirRelaxationcoef* 0.6. — [D-Flow FM TRM s.6.7.5](https://content.oss.deltares.nl/delft3dfm2d3d/D-Flow_FM_Technical_Reference_Manual.pdf)
- [Certain] With topographic weirs (no law), a collocated scheme (Iber) over-estimated head in free flow, i.e. under-discharged, by 5 to 23 %, while the internal law was within 0.9 to 5.6 %. — [García-Alén et al. 2021](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/water/water-13-02152/article_deploy/water-13-02152.pdf)
- [Certain, from N09 finding 22] HEC-RAS: the 2D equations "will substantially over-predict velocities" in free fall over SA/2D connections.

### Inferences
- [Likely] The two failures point in opposite directions. Iber's collocated Riemann scheme at a resolved crest under-discharges. HEC's face-based implicit scheme, and Wadi2D's staggered faces (2.6 to 3.8 x critical), over-discharge at an unresolved drop, because the face pressure gradient sees the full head across the drop. D-Flow FM's floor replaces ζ_R by max(ζ_R, z_c + 2/3 E) in exactly that gradient, which addresses Wadi2D problem (2) at its cause.
- [Likely] The floor needs only the upstream energy head and the crest elevation per face. Wadi2D already holds both (crest faces, energy-head option in decision 23), so it is a kernel change of a few lines rather than a new structure type.

### Gaps
- No published validation of D-Flow FM scheme 6 against flume data was read. Deltares calls oblique weirs "not fully understood". The Gerritsen et al. 2007 Delft3D weir validation, which the Twente thesis cites ("within 5%" for one scheme), was not read.

## Q5. Benchmarks comparing these approaches

### Takeaway
The only multi-package levee benchmark (EA Test 7) found that between-package spread came mostly from how crests were implemented and how overtopping was parameterised. Errors were comparable to the overtopping depths (about 0.1 m), and the EA concluded that robust techniques "do not exist". No paper comparing HEC-RAS breaklines with TUFLOW Z lines was found.

### Cited Findings
- [Certain] EA Test 7 (20 m grid, Upton-upon-Severn): peak floodplain levels 13.45 m (InfoWorks), about 13.3 m (MIKE, TUFLOW, ISIS) and 13.15 m (SOBEK), against embankments at about 13.1 to 12.8 m. The differences come from "a) model overtopping ... b) the implementation of the embankment crest elevations, and c) modelling of the 10m opening". Participants "were not always able to implement the correct structure dimensions or the correct elevations", with errors "often comparable to, if not larger than overtopping depths", which "are often as small as ~0.1m". Robust techniques "with appropriate guidance on parameterisation do not exist". — [EA 2010 SC080035/SR2, s.4.7 and Appendix C](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a75765c40f0b6360e4744dd/scho0510bsno-e-e.pdf)
- [Certain] Test 7 approaches: InfoWorks reduced element size near the breaklines "to ensure that the elevations were correctly represented". SOBEK turned the breaklines into "elevated 2D gridcells" on a 10 m grid. MIKE FLOOD used lateral weir coupling. ISIS reviewed crest points into 1D units. — same, Appendix C
- [Certain] The 2013 update (SC120002) supersedes the 2010 report. Its Test 7 detail was not read. — [GOV.UK, Benchmarking the latest generation](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/benchmarking-the-latest-generation-of-2d-hydraulic-flood-modelling-packages)
- [Certain] EA Test 3: a sill between two depressions at 5 m cells and n 0.01, with "some" water expected over the sill only if inertia is modelled. Test 1 settles to the sill level, 10.25 m. Both test sill handling, not oblique crests. — [TUFLOW EA benchmark update 2019](https://tuflow.com/media/5019/2019-uk-ea-2d-benchmarking-results-update-latest-tuflow-solvers.pdf)
- [Certain] Flume weirs (García-Alén 2021): the open dataset of 194 runs (Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.5062775) includes oblique weirs and is reusable for Wadi2D's crest-face tests. — [García-Alén et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/w13162152)

### Inferences
- [Likely] The benchmark evidence says crest elevation errors, not the overtopping law, dominate real-world spread. For Wadi2D that ranks problem (1), wrong ground on faces in a gorge, above problems (2) and (3).

### Gaps
- No paper comparing HEC-RAS breakline and TUFLOW Z-line results on the same embankment. No embankment-overtopping validation of a lattice crest at an oblique angle. No gorge-scale test of breakline placement in any model.

## What this means for a staggered square-tile solver

### Takeaway
Keep the lattice and the staircase. Fix the three problems face by face, with techniques that are published, cost no cells and need no time-step change: a ridge-only crest lift, a critical-depth floor in the face momentum, and a projected weir width applied only to the weir branch. Face alignment and refinement along every bank are the costly alternatives, and the evidence does not demand them.

### Cited Findings
- [Certain] (A) Raise faces whose link crosses the line, at the line's elevation. Watertight, zero cells, zero dt cost. Evidence: TUFLOW, 3Di, D-Flow FM, MIKE and SFINCS all do it (sources in Q1). Weakness: a half-cell offset puts the line's elevation on faces with lower ground, which is Wadi2D problem (1) and the EA Test 7 crest errors. — [EA 2010](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a75765c40f0b6360e4744dd/scho0510bsno-e-e.pdf)
- [Certain] (B) Ridge-only lift: lift a face only where the line stands above the ground on both sides. Precedents: ICM's crest "above the highest of the ground levels of the two computational elements"; D-Flow FM's toe level and ground height; 3Di's pixel-pair maximum, which only ever raises from the real pixels. Cost: one comparison per face at table build. Evidence in Wadi2D: W36 diagnostic, four cases within 0.3 %. — [ICM 2026 linear structure](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2026/ENU/ICMLIVE-Help/files/GUID-1178A2A1-7294-41D3-B939-000A2CBD494B.htm); [D-Flow FM TRM s.6.7.7](https://content.oss.deltares.nl/delft3dfm2d3d/D-Flow_FM_Technical_Reference_Manual.pdf); [Rana](https://docs.ranawaterintelligence.com/h_surfaceflow.html)
- [Certain] (C) Critical-depth floor in the face momentum (D-Flow FM scheme 6, Stelling-Duinmeijer): free overfall exactly at 1.705 E^1.5, continuous into submergence, no weir law, no switch, staggered-native. Caveats from the source: valid for flow perpendicular to the crest; neighbouring faces switched to inflow-only advection; oblique cases "not fully understood". Cost: a few operations per crest face per step. — [D-Flow FM TRM s.6.7.2-6.7.3](https://content.oss.deltares.nl/delft3dfm2d3d/D-Flow_FM_Technical_Reference_Manual.pdf)
- [Certain] (D) Projected weir width: wu = |n·nw|·Δx sums exactly to the crest length on any staircase (D-Flow FM; SFINCS's per-cell segment length is equivalent). Caveat: applied to the face area, it throttles along-line flow (Wadi2D problem 3), so it belongs on the critical branch only. Cost: one stored factor per crest face. — [D-Flow FM TRM eq. 6.216/6.247](https://content.oss.deltares.nl/delft3dfm2d3d/D-Flow_FM_Technical_Reference_Manual.pdf); [SFINCS](https://sfincs.readthedocs.io/en/latest/input_structures.html)
- [Certain] (E) Explicit weir law with Villemonte or energy-based submergence and a blend near drowning (TUFLOW HPC, ANUGA, MIKE, SFINCS, ICM, Iber). Evidence: 0.4 to 2.4 mm MAE in flumes (Iber). Cost: a switch with documented oscillation (HEC decay exponent; MIKE dampening 0.01 m; D-Flow FM relaxation 0.6; ANUGA blend 0.9 to 0.95). Wadi2D already has this as crest lines (0.98 to 1.0 of critical). — [García-Alén 2021](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/water/water-13-02152/article_deploy/water-13-02152.pdf); [ANUGA](https://anuga.readthedocs.io/en/develop/setup_anuga_script/riverwalls.html)
- [Certain] (F) Align faces with the line (HEC-RAS, ICM, ANUGA, MIKE FM and TUFLOW FV meshes). Exact crest and clean along-line flow. Cost: unstructured mesh, 8-face and collinear-face failures, small cells cutting an explicit dt, and for Wadi2D a new engine. — [HEC-RAS mesh page](https://www.hec.usace.army.mil/confluence/rasdocs/r2dum/latest/development-of-a-2d-or-combined-1d-2d-model/development-of-the-2d-computational-mesh)
- [Certain] (G) Refine along the line (quadtree or 3Di levels, HEC near spacing). Halving the cell halves the staircase offset. Cost: cells and dt in proportion. 3Di's own docs say so; TUFLOW's quadtree pays only from three levels (N02 finding 46). — [Rana](https://docs.ranawaterintelligence.com/h_surfaceflow.html)

### Inferences
- [Likely] Recommended order for Wadi2D, cheapest and best evidenced first:
  1. B: ridge-only lift for every line kind. This removes problem (1) and keeps watertightness where a real crest exists.
  2. C: critical-depth floor on every breakline face. This removes problem (2) without a weir law, and lets the `breakline` kind of W36 stand without capping.
  3. D: projected width on the critical branch of crest faces only. This fixes the 45-degree overcount without throttling along-line flow, and removes the length-factor part of problem (3).
  4. Banks stay refinement lines (W26) unless a real ridge exists.
- [Likely] Each step needs its own test before merging: the 0/22.5/45-degree crest sweep free and submerged against the analytic 1.705 E^1.5; the García-Alén flume set; and the four W36 gorge cases.

### Gaps
- No source shows the critical-depth floor on a lattice staircase with flow oblique to the line. That combination is untested anywhere read and is the main risk of step 2.
